On Nov 24 the review subscription hit its weekly limit, every new head got no Security verdict, and
`pr-gates` held every PR. Since #4174 (Nov 23) the review runs on every push, so the question was
how much of that the per-push reviews cost, and how to cut it without weakening the verdict.

**Baseline** (read from `gh run list` and the runs' own result messages):

- Review runs per merged PR: 0.98 for the 307 PRs opened Nov 16-23 before #4174, and 1.40 for the
  58 PRs opened after it (81 runs). Every PR needs one review of its final head, so 1.0 is the floor
  and the most any scheme can save is the extra 0.4.
- Cost per run, API-equivalent, every run on Sonnet 5.5: a PR's first review $0.153 and 12 turns
  (n=25), a push review $0.131 and 10 turns (n=22). Re-review mode, "only new findings", saved 14%.
  The whole review spent about $43 in the week of Nov 17-23 (328 runs).
- Debounce would not help: only 1 of 18 consecutive push pairs arrived while the earlier review was
  still running, because a review takes about 40 seconds.

**Change.** On a push, if the reviewer finds its own earlier summary on the PR, it runs the Security
lens only: it doesn't read REVIEW.md or AGENTS.md (40 KB), doesn't review the code again, and posts
a one-line summary so the verdict gate still sees a verdict. The Security lens still judges the
whole diff for the exact head, with the same criteria, model and turn budget. A PR with no earlier
summary (a conflicted open, a review lost to quota) gets the full review on its next push. A full
re-review on demand is still the `re-review` label. Before Nov 23, pushes got no review at all, so
the general review goes back to how it was then.

**Target, evaluated Dec 1:** general reviews per merged PR at 1.05 or below, a push run's mean cost
at $0.07 or below (from $0.131), and review spend per merged PR down at least 15% (from about
$0.21 to $0.18 or below). The `review-completes` SLO and `security-verdict` pass rate must not
drop.

**What this does not fix.** Cutting the review by half is not possible without dropping review that
is still wanted. A 50% cut would need a cheaper model or a smaller first review, which is a quality
call. The review's cost was probably not what used up the weekly limit either. At about $43 a week,
it is likely a small share of a subscription the agents also use. The lasting fix is to stop the
required `security-verdict` from depending on a shared subscription, and that is a separate
decision.

Revert if `review-completes` drops, if a push run skips the Security lens or posts no verdict, or if
a fix-up push lands a bug that the old push re-review would have caught.
