DOR-4261. QA found the 0.135.7 DMG rejected by Gatekeeper (`spctl -a -t open --context
context:primary-signature`: "Unnotarized Developer ID") while the app inside it was notarized and
stapled. electron-builder's `mac.notarize` submits only the `.app`; `dmg-builder` signs the DMG
and never submits it. DOR-2728 did not drop a step: the DMG was never notarized. A person opening
the download gets a Gatekeeper warning on first open; the app itself was always fine.

What changed, in build-macos, signed builds only (`APPLE_DEVELOPER_CONFIGURED`), on tag pushes
and dispatches alike: after packaging, a step submits the DMG with `notarytool submit --wait
--timeout 20m`, fails the job on any status but `Accepted` (printing Apple's log), then staples
it. The three Apple secrets the package step already uses are scoped to that step only; no new
secret. Stapling changes the DMG's bytes after electron-builder hashed them, so a second step
(no secrets) rebuilds `<dmg>.blockmap` with electron-builder's own block-map code and rewrites the
DMG's `sha512` and `size` in `latest-mac.yml` (`apps/desktop/scripts/refresh-dmg-update-info.ts`,
unit-tested). The existing verify step, before upload and attach, now also requires `xcrun
stapler validate` and `spctl --assess --type open --context context:primary-signature` to pass on
the DMG.

Hypothesis: the new submission does not make build-macos fail more often (target equals the
baseline). It adds one Apple round trip to the job, about the size of the `.app` one (roughly 9
of the 13.7 package minutes on v0.98.0), well inside the 56-min timeout; the 20-min notarytool
cap keeps a stuck submission from eating the publish step's budget. The fix itself is proved by
the first tag run's verify step passing on the DMG, not by this metric.

Revert or revisit if notarization of the DMG fails on runs where the `.app` notarized fine, or if
an updater or download check trips on the refreshed `latest-mac.yml`. DOR-4043's DMG click-test
is not folded in: it needs a Playwright Electron test and screenshots, which is its own change.
