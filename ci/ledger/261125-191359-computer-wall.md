DOR-5158, step C1 of spec `computer-side-starts-alone` (§4). Every computer-side caller is to reach the space side through a port, and the crossings go one seam at a time. The wall holds the line while they do: at this branch's base (`8f57112503`) computer-side files hold 520 imports of space-side code in 231 files, 109 of front-door code in 53, and name 23 space tables from `@dorkos/db` in 10, and none may be added.

`apps/server/eslint-rules/sides.json` puts every folder and root file under `services/` on one side, and every table `@dorkos/db` exports on one of two lists. `apps/server/eslint-rules/computer-wall.js` is an ESLint rule in the server's lint (the required `lint` check): it resolves every import of a computer-side file, type-only and `declare module` included, holds each of the three columns to the file's entry in `computer-wall.baseline.json`, and fails a file the side map does not place. `scripts/computer-wall/check-computer-wall.ts` measures the same with the rule's classifier, lowers entries (`--update`, never raises), and with `--base` refuses an entry raised or added by hand. The `typecheck` job runs it with `--base` beside the turn port's ratchet, on the same base, and pre-push runs it at the branch point; its test (`scripts/__tests__/check-computer-wall.test.ts` › "the repo") runs in the queue's repo-wide suites.

The real measure is the baseline's totals, which should fall with each seam (C2 to C20) until `space` and `tables` are empty at C23: `jq '[.files[].space] | add' apps/server/eslint-rules/computer-wall.baseline.json`. The scored metric is the price: the rule reads each computer-side file's imports once and should not move the lint job's pull-request p90 past 11.0 minutes. The turn port's ratchet (261125-065516) prices the same kind of rule on the same job's merge-group leg in an overlapping window, so read the two verdicts together; an `inconclusive` here from that overlap is expected, and the comparison then is the lint p90 in the week after both merged.

Revert or rework if baseline conflicts between concurrent PRs become a regular cause of DIRTY PRs, if the rule shows in the lint p90, or if callers work around it (a re-export through a kernel or computer-side file) instead of moving to a port.

Guard, not the hypothesis: the wall rides the required lint job, so it must not slow it. `gate.wf.lint.lint.duration_p90@pull_request` was 10.9 min (`wf.lint.lint@pull_request`, ci-steward-data snapshots 2026-11-19 to 2026-11-24, n=650 runs) and should stay at or under 11.0. That window overlaps the turn-port ratchet's entry, so a small move there may not be the wall's. The allowance target is "never above 652", and every later seam step lowers it.
