## The short version

- **One channel, the one that exists.** A computer dials its home over the sealed, signed relay channel already built for linked Macs. The home now answers the hello with its own feature list, and each side uses only what both name. The protocol stays at version 1.
- **One way to run a turn.** On the home, a remote computer face forwards the turn port over the channel, and `turn.start` carries the whole start request. On the computer, the channel's requests land on the same computer face a Mac uses in-process. Reads (chat lists, history, the live stream) go through one forwarding runtime for each computer and runtime type, which refuses anything that would start, steer or stop a turn.
- **Every port of step 6 gets a wire form**, in one table (§5).
- **Trust stays on the home.** The signed rules copy of the gate note is sent before each turn and pushed on every change. A computer that does not offer the required features is not ready, and its runs wait with a reason a person can read.
- **Nothing is lost or doubled.** Every message that can outlive a connection carries a ULID the receiver uses to drop or join a repeat. The computer keeps a small outbox, and the home decides a restarted computer's earlier runs only after that outbox is delivered.
- **A computer is a record in a space**, an agent runs on one computer at a time under a lease, and no rules copy outlives that lease.
- **Adding a machine** is one command: SSH first, the tunnel second. The local sealed computer moves onto the same contract.
- **A Mac alone sees no change.** The in-process path stays; everything here is added beside it.

## Goals

- A home (the person's Mac, or their DorkOS in the cloud) runs an agent's turns on another computer through the same turn port it uses for its own, with the same shapes.
- Every refusal, level and mark is decided on the home, as the gate note requires, and the computer can only make a turn stricter.
- A dropped connection or a restart of either side loses no word, fact, record or tool call's effect, and doubles none.
- Two builds of DorkOS, one older, can talk: each uses only what both name, and a computer that cannot carry the rules gets no turns.
- A person can add a machine they own with one command, and see it as a computer in their space.
- Each step ships alone and leaves the app working.

## Non-goals (out of scope)

- Starting and stopping cloud agent computers on demand, their image, and reading their link code from the hosted service's assignment (roadmap item 14). This spec builds the channel, the record and the operations such a computer uses; item 14 builds how it starts and links.
- The account directory, one sidebar grouped by computer, and linking a Mac from the web (item 13). The computer record here is readable through a capability; item 13 shows it.
- Team spaces (item 15). Every computer in this spec is linked by its home's owner; the rules for a computer someone else linked are written down (§5.3) for item 15 to build on.
- The private space as a tenant on the space store (item 16). The new space tables here live in the machine store, each with a ticket naming its move.
- The own-process test (item 7).
- What travels with an agent when it moves between computers or homes, what an agent that lives in the cloud may do on the person's Mac, which connections it can use there, and how the app on a Mac reaches agents in the cloud (ADR `261125-113808`, "Still open"). This contract joins one home to its own computers (§19, question 4).
- Changing what the five chat operations do for a Mac linked to a hub. They keep their flag, their passkey rule and their behaviour.

### 1.2 Who dials whom, and when

- **The computer always dials.** Nothing dials into a computer. It opens one WebSocket to its pinned home's origin, `https:` turned into `wss:`, at `RELAY_COMPUTER_PATH = '/api/relay/computer'` (`relay-dialer.ts:56`, `:208`). The relay secret rides the upgrade's `Authorization` header (`:209`), never a frame.
- **Only one thing makes it dial:** a `viewer_open` command on the computer's command stream from the hosted service (`services/core/remote/command-dispatcher.ts:273-274`, into `RelayDialer.onViewerOpen`, `relay-dialer.ts:161`). There is no dial at boot and no reconnect: a dropped or idle connection stays down until the next `viewer_open` (`relay-dialer.ts:18-20`). After a failed dial, a new one waits 15 seconds (`RELAY_REDIAL_BACKOFF_MS`, `:73`).
- **The computer dials only when** the `sessionRelay.enabled` experiment is on, Remote Access is on and a pin exists and is not paused (`dialablePin`, `services/core/session-relay/index.ts:72-77`), and an owner account exists. It checks the pin and the owner again on every frame it receives.
- **The home answers only when** its role is `hub` (else 404, `relay-endpoint.ts:181`), it has a serving origin, the request carries no browser headers, the hub signing key is loaded, and the bearer matches a stored secret hash (`:182-190`).
- **One connection per computer.** The computer holds at most one. The home keeps one live connection per computer and replaces it only after a new dial's `finish` verifies (`services/core/hub-relay/hub-relay-registry.ts`; at most 3 unproved dials per computer, `:19`).
- **The computer chooses its own channel id** when it links: its cloud instance id when it is linked to a DorkOS account, else a local id (`relay-link.ts:268-280`).

### 1.3 The hello and the handshake

1. The computer sends `hello` in plain JSON: its `computerId`, its version range, its `features` (at most 64 strings of at most 64 characters, `session-relay.ts:202`), its app version, a fresh nonce and a fresh X25519 key. `RelayHelloFrameSchema` is strict.
2. The home checks that the `computerId` is the one the bearer secret belongs to, picks the highest common version, and answers `welcome` or `refuse`, signed with its long-lived Ed25519 key over the transcript, under the label `dorkos-session-relay/v1` (`session-relay.ts:733`).
3. The computer checks the signature against the key it pinned at link time, derives one key per direction, and sends `finish`, its own signature, as its first sealed frame.
4. The home checks `finish` against the computer's key it pinned at link time (`LinkedComputerSchema.computerKey`). It sends and serves nothing before that.

**What the hello carries, and does not.** The home stores the computer's features and uses them only to refuse a request the computer lacks with `501 needs_update` naming the feature (`computer-connection.ts:334-336`, `:413`), and to decide whether to add a `device` field. **The home sends no feature list.** The computer advertises `chats.list`, `session.stream`, `session.send`, `session.interrupt`, `request.device`, and `interaction.approval` while a passkey is pinned (`relay-request-server.ts:87-93`). The dialer drops every feature that is not in `SESSION_RELAY_FEATURES` before it sends `hello` (`relay-dialer.ts:204-206`), so an agent-computer feature cannot be advertised today. The home decodes every computer frame with `RelayComputerFrameSchema` alone (`computer-connection.ts:440`): a `call` frame closes the connection.

### 1.4 The five chat operations

All five are keyed by chat (`sessionId`); none names a run. The home asks, the computer answers; there is no computer-to-home request on the built path. They serve only person-facing chats of registered agents that are not system agents (`services/core/session-relay/relay-serves.ts`). A relayed send lands in `dispatchSessionMessage` with the origin `session-relay`, not the turn port. `session.interrupt` stops the chat's running turn **and empties its queue** (`session-relay.ts:321-322`). `interaction.respond` (`:381`) approves or denies only with a passkey proof, and refuses the `answer` kind (`relay-request-server.ts:607`); the hub's only route that calls it sends the `answer` kind (`routes/hub-computers.ts:430`). A Mac resolves the runtime for a relayed request in-process, through `runtimeRegistry` (`services/core/session-relay/live-deps.ts:105`, `:151`, `:171`), never through a forwarding runtime.

## 4. The channel

Each change below says what exists, what changes, its feature id, and what an older peer sees. "Older" means a build of DorkOS from before the step that adds the feature.

### 4.1 The hello, both ways (S1; DOR-5164)

**Exists.** The computer's `hello` carries its features; the home answers with a version and a signature and sends no feature list (§1.3). The dialer filters its features to the chat set (`relay-dialer.ts:204-206`).

**Changes.**

- The dialer advertises every feature its server serves: the filter at `relay-dialer.ts:204-206` goes, and the list stays under `RelayAdvertisedFeaturesSchema`'s cap (64 items, `session-relay.ts:202`). A test pins the full list under the cap with room for growth.
- The home sends its own list in the `features` frame (`RelayFeaturesFrameSchema`), as its first sealed frame after it accepts `finish`, and only to a computer whose record says `agent-computer` (§4.2) and which named at least one agent-computer feature (`hubFeaturesFrameFor`, `session-relay-agent-computer.ts:735`, widened from call features to every agent-computer feature). It rides the sealed channel after `finish`, so only the proven home can send it.
- **Each side uses a feature only when both named it.** The home computes the agreed set once per connection (`negotiatedRelayCallFeatures`, `:680`, widened the same way), and every send checks it. A request the computer did not name is refused before it is sent, as today (`501 needs_update`). From an agent computer, a `call` the home did not name is answered with the same refusal (`relayCallFeatureRefusal`, `:711`) instead of closing the connection.
- `hello` itself does not change: it is a strict schema, and an older home would refuse a field it does not know. Anything new about the computer (its `bootId`, a ULID made each time its process starts) rides `computer.status`, after the features are agreed (§5.9).

**Feature id.** The frame needs none; every feature in it is one.

**Bounds and refusals (W1, on Security's terms).** A named list holds at most 64 ids, each at most 64 characters of `a-z`, `0-9`, `.`, `_` and `-`; a `hello` or `features` frame past that is refused whole, never trimmed. So a feature id is lowercase on the wire, and the two reads with capitals in their names take the ids `session.last-ids` and `session.read-from` (§5). An id a build does not know is carried only: never in the agreed set, a log line or a decision. The agreed set is fixed once per connection, as the intersection of both signed lists. The computer accepts the `features` frame only as the first sealed frame after `finish`, and closes on a second or later one; the home closes on one from the computer. On an agent computer's connection the home answers a `call` it cannot read with `needs_update` naming no feature and echoing nothing but the call's id, names a feature in a refusal only from the known list, and closes the connection past 32 refused calls in a minute. All of it is in `packages/shared/src/wire/features.ts`, `services/core/hub-relay/agent-computer-hello.ts` and `services/core/session-relay/wire-clients/hub-features.ts`.

**Older peers.** An older computer names no agent-computer feature, so the home sends it no `features` frame (its strict web frame schema would refuse one) and nothing changes for it. An older home ignores features it does not know and sends no `features` frame, so a newer computer agrees on no agent-computer feature, sends no `call`, and serves only the chat operations. A newer computer never sends a `call` to a home that did not name the call's feature, because an older home closes the connection on a frame it cannot decode (`computer-connection.ts:440`).

### 4.2 The role on the home's record of a computer, and the record's id (S1)

**Exists.** `LinkedComputerSchema` v1 has no role (§1.7). The schema file's comment says the home must take what a computer is from its own record; there is no field to read. The computer chooses its channel `computerId` itself (§1.2).

**Changes.**

- `LinkedComputerSchema` moves to v2: `role: 'computer' | 'agent-computer'` and `hostId`, the id of the computer's record (§8). A v1 row reads as `role: 'computer'` with no `hostId`.
- **The record's id is the home's, not the computer's.** The home mints it when the link succeeds, as a `dork:host` reference. The channel's `computerId`, which the computer chose, is a field of the record (`channelComputerId`) and stays the channel's key.
- **The role comes from the link code, never from what the computer sends.** A code minted to add a machine (§11), for the local sealed computer (§10) or for a cloud agent computer is bound to the role `agent-computer` when it is minted. A Mac linking to a hub for the chat operations stays `computer`. The signed link answer (`link-response`) names the role, so the computer's pin records it.
- The home decodes a computer's frames with `AgentComputerFrameSchema` (`session-relay-agent-computer.ts:636`) only when the record says `agent-computer`; for a `computer` it keeps `RelayComputerFrameSchema`, so a `call` frame from a linked Mac still closes the connection. The home sends agent-computer requests only to an `agent-computer`, and never sends it `session.send`, `session.interrupt` or `interaction.respond`: its turns start, stop and are answered only through the turn port (N1).

**Feature id.** None: the role is the home's own record.

**Older peers.** An older home cannot read a v2 file: the whole file fails to parse and reads as no linked computers, so it serves none (it fails closed). DorkOS supports upgrades, not downgrades; the step's changelog fragment says a downgrade unlinks agent computers. An older computer reads a `link-response` with a role it does not know as it reads any field it does not know: its link schema is strict, so the home sends the role only to a computer whose link request names the new pin version (§4.7).

### 4.3 Dial at boot and after a drop (S1)

**Exists.** A dial happens only on `viewer_open`; nothing reconnects (§1.2).

**Changes.** A computer whose pin says `agent-computer` dials when it boots, and again after any drop, with backoff: 1 second, doubling to at most 60 seconds, with up to 20% jitter, reset after a connection that stayed up for 5 minutes. **It never closes its connection for idleness**: `SESSION_RELAY_IDLE_CLOSE_MS` and the `viewers` count apply only to a pin with the role `computer`. This departs from `design-agent-computers.md` §2, which closes an agent computer after the idle window; the presence lease (§4.5) and the runner lease (§9) need the connection up, and it carries no cost while idle beyond the 30-second heartbeat (signed by Security, §14). It still closes on the silence timeout (90 seconds) and on any frame that fails to open. A pin with the role `computer` keeps today's rule: it dials on `viewer_open` only.

**Feature id.** None: dialling is the computer's own behaviour.

**Older peers.** An older home accepts the dial as it accepts any.

### 4.4 One live instance

**Exists.** The home replaces a computer's live connection when a new dial proves itself (`hub-relay-registry.ts`).

**Changes.** For an agent computer, the home promotes a new proved dial only after its first `computer.status` names a `bootId`. It replaces the live connection when the `bootId` is the same (a reconnect), or when the live connection has been silent past the silence timeout. A proved dial with a different `bootId` while the live one is still heard is refused and recorded as an event on the computer record ("two copies of this computer dialled at once"), and the person is told once (signed by Security, §14). One process per data folder still holds on the computer itself (`lib/instance-lock.ts`).

**Feature id.** `computer.status.runs`.

**Older peers.** A computer that does not name `computer.status.runs` keeps today's rule: a proved dial replaces the live one. Such a computer is not ready for turns anyway (§5.1).

### 4.5 Presence as a lease

**Exists.** Nothing records when a computer was last heard beyond the live connection.

**Changes.** A computer is **present** while its presence lease holds. The lease is granted when the home promotes the connection (§4.4) and renewed by every `computer.status` call (§5.9). It lasts 90 seconds, the silence timeout. The computer sends `computer.status` every 30 seconds and at once when a run starts or ends. The home writes `lastSeenAt` on the computer record (§8) at most once a minute. A computer whose presence lapses is shown as away; its runs follow §6.5.

**Feature id.** `computer.status`. **Older peers.** A computer that does not name it is present while its connection is up, as today, and is not ready for turns (§5.1).

### 4.6 The home end on a Mac: endpoint, link route and key (S2; DOR-5170)

**Exists.** The relay endpoint and the link route answer only on the hub role (`relay-endpoint.ts:181`; `routes/hub-link.ts:165`). The link route is exempt from the session gate on a hub only, and refuses browsers, oversized bodies, too many wrong codes, a computer id already linked under another key, and a wrong or spent code (`routes/hub-link.ts` header). The signing key exists only on the hub; on a person's own computer "both readers throw" (`hub-signing-key.ts:24`). The key file is on the protected-path list (`services/core/protected-paths/protected-paths.ts:105`).

**Changes.**

- **The home end runs wherever the space side is.** A Mac serves it when the `remoteComputers.enabled` experiment is on (W1) and it has linked an agent computer, or a link code for one is outstanding. The registry, the connection class and the computer store are the hub's own, unchanged.
- **Where each home serves it.** The hub keeps `/api/relay/computer` and `/api/hub/link`, unchanged. A Mac serves its endpoint at `/relay/computer` and its link route at `/relay/link`, both outside `/api`, as the gate note §3 asks. **A computer picks the path from its pin** (`dialPath`, §4.7); nothing assumes how the hosted service routes a hub's traffic (N8).
- **The Mac's endpoint** checks the relay credential before it reads anything else from the request, and never goes around the session gate: it is not a session route at all, and it answers nothing under `/api`.
- **The Mac's link route** (signed by Security, §14) answers only while a link code minted on that Mac is outstanding (codes are minted only by the owner, from a signed-in session or by `dorkos machine add` run as the owner); otherwise it answers 404 as a person's computer does today. It runs the same refusals as the hub's route (no browser headers, the body cap, the cap on wrong codes, no takeover of a linked id, the code spent the moment it matches). **Each link code is bound to the pipe it was minted for**: an SSH forward, the VM's host address, or the Mac's remote access address for the tunnel. Each pipe reaches the Mac on its own listener (a loopback port the Mac opens for that one SSH forward; the address the VM sees; the remote access ingress, which already marks its requests), and the route refuses a redemption that arrives on any other, spending no code.
- **A Mac makes its key when its owner mints its first agent-computer link code,** before any `link-response` must be signed. It is made the same way the hub's is: a file only the owner can read, written in one atomic step, never in the environment or the config, under the same protected file name. Later links reuse it. If the key file is gone, it never becomes a silent new key: the Mac refuses to serve, every linked computer is shown as needing to be linked again, and linking again pins the new key. The key's readers allow a Mac that is a home; they still throw on a Mac that has never minted such a code.
- **No credentials cross.** A Mac never hands a far computer a member's credentials (ADR `261122-173853`); sign-ins on the far computer are its own.

**Feature id.** None: the endpoint, the route and the key are the home's own.

**Older peers.** A computer from before this spec never links to a Mac home; every computer that does is new.

### 4.7 The pin

**Exists.** `RelayPinSchema` v2 (`pin-store.ts:58`) holds a bare `https` origin, the home's key, this computer's id and name, the relay secret, level and pause.

**Changes.** The pin moves to v3 and gains `role` (from the signed link answer), `dialPath` (`/api/relay/computer` for a hub, `/relay/computer` for a Mac), and `linkedVia`: `https`, `ssh-forward` or `local-vm`. **`linkedVia` is set from the link code on the home, never from what the computer sends**: the home binds each code to its pipe when it is minted, records the value on the `LinkedComputer` row, and names it in the signed link answer, from which the pin takes it. **An origin that is not `https` is allowed only** for `ssh-forward` (a loopback address on the far computer, the near end of the SSH reverse forward, §11) and for `local-vm` (the host's address as the VM sees it, §10). Every frame is still sealed and signed, and the home's key is still the pinned one. A v2 pin reads as `role: 'computer'`, `dialPath: '/api/relay/computer'`, `linkedVia: 'https'`. Security signed this (§14). Three checks change in the same step, each only for `ssh-forward` and `local-vm`: `linkToHub` refuses an origin that is not `https` (`relay-link.ts:258`) and a host that is not public (`checkPublicHost`, `:265`); the dialer turns only `https:` into `wss:` (`relay-dialer.ts:208`), and gains `http:` to `ws:` for those two.

### 5.1 The minimum feature set

A computer is **ready for turns** only when it names every one of: `turn.start`, `turn.start.run`, `turn.stop`, `turn.answer`, `rules.copy`, `computer.status`, `computer.status.runs`, `run.fact`, `records.put`, `asks.raise`, `agents.sync`, `agents.sync.facts`, `tools.list`, `tools.call`, `tools.call.run`, `call.cancel`, `session.stream`, `agent.pause`, `sessions.end`. The rules copy without the run id on tool calls is not enough: a computer that names `rules.copy` and not `tools.call.run` is not ready.

**A computer that is not ready gets no turns, and nothing is refused.** Its agents' runs wait at admission with the reason `computer_not_ready`, which the app shows as "This computer needs a newer DorkOS." They are released at once when that computer says hello again with the set (§6.4). Optional features (`tools.list.exposure`, `tools.call.images`, `tools.call.marks`, `tools.call.facts`, `kit.call`, `config.changed`, the reads) change what is offered, never whether a turn may run: without `kit.call` the home leaves the kit's tools out of `tools.list`; without `tools.call.images` an image result is sent as its text part only; without `tools.call.marks` the home relies on in-order handling (§5.3).

### 5.2 Turns

**`turn.start` carries the whole request.** Under `turn.start.run`, the request is `TurnStartRequestSchema` whole (`turn-port.ts:611`): `runId`, `attempt`, `continuesRunId?`, `sessionId`, `agentId`, `cwd?`, `content` inline, the full `origin` and `wireOrigin`, `originRef?`, `tier`, `permissionCeiling?`, `systemPromptAppend?`, `additionalDirectories?`, `prepare?`, `accountRef?`, `playbookId?`, `settings?`, `seedContext?`, `commandIntent?` (§17.1), and every field step 6's `turn-start-carries.ts` adds, plus the run's prompt facts (step 6 §5.3). The computer face gets exactly what a Mac's gets (N1). The old `turn.start` shape and its answer `{ sessionId, turnId }` are never used: the home sends `turn.start` only to a ready computer. A start with no `agentId` is served only by a Mac's own computer face (step 5 §3.2).

**The frame budget, in bytes.** The encoded request must fit one frame of `AGENT_COMPUTER_MAX_BODY_BYTES` (`session-relay-agent-computer.ts:142`: the inner frame cap less 256 bytes, 786,112 bytes). It fits by construction. The rules copy is not in the request: it goes first, by `rules.put`. Every field has a cap on its encoded JSON, in bytes:

| Field                                                                      | Cap (bytes, encoded) |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------: |
| `content`                                                                  |              560,000 |
| `systemPromptAppend`                                                       |               32,768 |
| `additionalDirectories` (at most 32 paths of 1,024 bytes)                  |               32,768 |
| the turn-start carries                                                     |               32,768 |
| the prompt facts                                                           |               49,152 |
| `settings`                                                                 |               16,384 |
| `seedContext`                                                              |                8,192 |
| everything else (ids, origin, ceiling, account, playbook, prepare, intent) |                8,192 |
| **Total**                                                                  |          **740,224** |

The rest of the frame (45,888 bytes) is left for keys and framing. The space face sizes the carries and the prompt facts before it builds the request, and refuses at `start()`, before anything is saved, a start whose words or other fields pass their caps, naming the cap. Most 200,000-character messages fit; one written mostly outside ASCII may not, and is refused with the cap named.

**Paths.** `cwd` and `additionalDirectories` are the computer's own paths, from its `agents.sync` answer (§5.5). The home never maps a path itself.

**Which runs the computer holds.** The computer keeps, in memory, the id of every run it accepted in this boot. It answers `already_held` to a `turn.start` for any of them, including a run that has since ended, until the home has acknowledged that run's `ended` fact (the answer to its `run.fact`); then it lets the id go. A new boot starts with none. So a repeated `turn.start` never runs a turn twice. The computer writes a run's `started` fact to its outbox, and that write is committed and flushed to disk, before it hands the words to the runtime. So after any crash, either the fact is in the outbox or the words were never handed over; never the words with no fact. "No `started` fact" always means "the runtime never had the words" (proof D20).

**The remote start, step by step.** Every transition named is in step 5's table (§4.3); this spec adds none.

1. **Admission says go** (§6.4). In one transaction the `start_turn` handler writes the chat's binding when it has none (§6.3), and moves the run `queued` → `starting` (transition 4, which step 5 already gives the handler for a remote computer). The run's owner becomes `host:<hostId>:<bootId>` and its lease is the home's view of its copy (§6.5).
2. **The copy first.** The handler sends `rules.put` with a freshly signed copy for that run, at the version kept on the run's row (§5.6), and waits for its acknowledgement.
3. **Then `turn.start`.**
4. **The answer.**
   - `accepted` or `already_held`: the handler waits for the `started` fact (transition 7).
   - `chat_busy`: back to `queued` (transition 8) with `wait: 'chat_busy'`, as step 5 §3.1 says for a remote computer.
   - `rules_missing` (no valid copy for that run arrived): the handler sends `rules.put` again and repeats the start once; a second `rules_missing` is a fault, recorded on the computer record, and the run goes back to `queued` (8) and waits `computer_not_ready`.
   - `not_this_computer` (the computer holds no runner lease for the agent): back to `queued` (8), waiting `moving`. A move releases it only after a status from the agent's new computer names the lease (§9), so an ordinary move records no fault. It is recorded as a fault only when no move is under way.
   - Any other refusal at the door: cancelled from `starting` (transition 9), as step 5 says.
5. **No answer** (the connection dropped, or the request's timeout passed). The handler writes nothing and waits. It never cancels the run as `start_failed` once `turn.start` may have reached the computer. The run keeps `starting`, and `isLive` answers `unknown`.
6. **The computer comes back.** Its first status lists the runs it holds and, for every run it does not hold, whether a fact for it is still in its outbox (`owed`, §5.9). **The home decides nothing about a run until the computer has delivered the facts it owes for it.** The status handler (`services/core/hub-relay/remote-face/status-handler.ts`, new) is the one writer that acts on a `starting` run:
   - the status lists the run as held: treat it as accepted and wait for its `started` fact;
   - the run is owed a fact: wait for it;
   - the same `bootId`, the run is not held and nothing is owed: the handler reopens the run's `start_turn`, which sends `rules.put` and `turn.start` again; the computer's `already_held` rule makes this safe;
   - a new `bootId`: the run is **awaiting its computer** until nothing is owed for it, then the decide step of §6.5 decides it.
7. **Lost contact.** When the home's own view of the run's copy ends (§5.6) with still no contact, the run ends `lost_contact` with `delivery: unknown`, by transitions that already exist: a `starting` run passes through `started` in the same transaction (7, then 11); a `started` run ends by 11; a `running` run by 12. Passing a `starting` run through `started` writes a start the home never saw, so its `ending_detail` records "assumed handed over". **Nothing is replayed.** For a `starting` or `started` run, a person's own words come back as a restored draft, as step 5's recovery does for a run that ended from `started` (its §7.3). A `running` run's queue row was already deleted at `delivered`, so there is nothing to restore: the person sees the line "Lost touch with <computer> during this reply. How it ended is unknown." Any other sender is told through its own path (a report to a chat, a task run's ending). Recovery never continues the run.
8. **A late fact** for a run already ended `lost_contact` never starts anything. The run keeps its ending and gains the late ending in `ending_detail`. If the late fact is a `completed` and the person's restored draft is still unsent, the draft stays, never sent by itself, and shows the line "Your agent did answer this. Send it again only if needed."

**A turn the runtime opens by itself** (step 5 §4.3, transition 2) carries the run id the computer minted, owned by that computer's boot; the home inserts it once. If the chat already has a live remote run, step 5 §4.7's rule for that case applies: a run of the chat that is `starting` goes back to `queued` (transition 8) as the runtime-opened run is inserted, and is handed over again after it ends. On a remote computer that `starting` run was sent, so the computer holds it by its `already_held` rule and starts nothing twice. Security signed the recording of computer-minted ids (§14).

**How `start-turn.ts` changes.** `handOver` (`services/runs/effects/handlers/start-turn.ts:109`) already answers `await` for a `starting` run (`:117`). For a remote computer the handler writes `starting` itself before it asks the face (step 1). Its `handTo` (`:155`, which cancels as `start_failed` at `:167`) no longer reaches `start_failed` after that write: the remote face never throws for a lost connection, it returns "no answer", and the handler returns `await`. `start_failed` stays for a throw before `starting` is written, when nothing was sent. `handOver`'s queue branch (`:119-123`) and the held-run check (`:87-95`) read the queue store the chat's binding picks (§6.4), so a remote run's row is never taken as removed. On a Mac, a run with a queue row is left to the dispatcher's pump; for a remote chat nothing pumps, so the handler hands the run over itself (§6.4).

**The per-run stop.** `turn.stop { runId }` stops one run and never touches a queue. With `backgroundWorkId` it stops one registered item of that run. It answers with the receipt vocabulary of ADR `260823-021312`. **A stop is final, and works when the agent is stuck:** when the runtime gives no `acked` or `closed` receipt within 10 seconds, the computer ends the runtime's processes for that chat, then answers `closed`. Every stop request is written to the run's `ending_detail` before it is sent (step 5 §4.4). **A stop to a computer that is away** answers at once with the receipt `unconfirmed`. The home stops renewing that run's copy, and sends `rules.drop` for it the moment the computer is back. The run ends when its copy's life ends at the latest, and the stop request on the run means recovery continues nothing.

**Answers.** The turn port's `answer(runId, interactionId, response)` sends `turn.answer`, a new operation in the agent-computer set only, bound to the run, the ask's id and a digest of the exact input the card showed. It works once and before the ask's expiry. A computer whose pin's role is `computer` refuses it. **The home sends `turn.answer` only after the same proof of the person that the local card needs**: a passkey wherever answering that card in-process, or by `interaction.respond`, requires one, and the person's signed-in session otherwise. So the wire never carries an answer weaker than the home would accept itself. The proof stays on the home; the frame carries the answer, bound to the run, the ask and the digest, and an agent computer has no other controller (gate note §5). `interaction.respond` keeps its passkey rule and is used only for linked Macs. §14 records this as signed. **An answer given while the computer is away** is kept on the home (`wire_pending_answers`, §6.6) and sent when the computer is back, if its status still lists the ask as pending. If the ask is gone (the computer restarted), the card is withdrawn and the person sees "The computer restarted, so this answer was not used."

**`isLive(runId)` has three answers.** `live`: the computer is present and its last status listed the run as held. `not live`: a status of the run's own boot did not list it, nothing is owed for it, and the run was not re-sent. `unknown`: the computer is not present, or the run is awaiting its computer. **Unknown is treated as live**: the chat's queue holds and nothing restarts the run, for as long as the home's view of the run's copy lasts. A run awaiting its computer is never ended `lost_contact` while that computer is in contact: its status renews the run's lease, and the decide step decides it (§6.5).

**Run facts and their ids.** `accepted`, `started`, `delivered`, `waiting_on_person`, `resumed_from_person`, `background_work` and `ended` travel as `run.fact` calls, each keyed by its `runId` and carrying its own call id and the `bootId` that made it (§5.7).

### 5.3 Tools

### 5.4 The home calling a tool that runs on the computer

The tools of the computer tools kit (step 6 §5.9: diff, workspace, browser seat, local browser, compaction, instructions preview, `get_session_count`, `accounts_probe`) are listed and gated on the home and run on the computer. After the gate allows a call, the home sends `kit.call` with the call's `agentId`, `sessionId`, `runId`, tool, input and caller facts (which chats the caller may see). The computer answers with the same outcome shape, images included. It keys each request by its ULID: a repeat while the original runs joins it; a repeat after it finished gets the stored outcome, kept 10 minutes. The home gives up a `kit.call` with `cancel`. Tools whose effect is the computer itself (`local_browser_*`) are called on the computer and never cross.

### 5.5 Agent facts, `agents.sync` and the run's tokens

- **The put** carries the agent's manifest, identity files and packages, plus (under `agents.sync.facts`) its `authorId` and a `factsVersion` that only rises. The computer stores a read-only copy, replaced on each put, and **never reads it for a trust decision**: trust rides only the signed rules copy (gate note §3).
- **The answer** carries the paths the computer chose for the agent under its own data folder: `homePath` and `deskPath`. The home stores them on the agent's placement (§9) and uses them as `cwd` in later starts.
- **When it is sent:** when an agent is placed on the computer (§9), when its manifest, files or packages change, and after a reconnect for each placed agent whose `factsVersion` the computer's status does not name.
- **Remove** is sent when an agent leaves the computer. The computer stops its live runs, ends its background copies, deletes the synced copy, and answers.
- **The run's tokens (signed by Security, §14).** The run's agent token for DorkOS's own command line and the run's local `/mcp` token are made on the computer for each run, never sent over the wire, and revoked when the run ends and again when its background phase ends (§5.6), whichever is later. Each is accepted only on the loopback listener, and reaches only the computer entry's own loopback listener (`/mcp` and `/agent-mcp`, the only routes it serves besides health, step 6 §7.3), which maps the token to the run and forwards each call as `tools.call` under that run. So on a computer, the agent's command line can do only what a DorkOS tool can do, as that run's agent; a command-line verb that needs the home's HTTP API answers that it is not available on this computer. Neither reaches anything on the home: the home serves a call under a run only after checking that the run's placement is the channel's proven computer (§5.3). This departs from step 6 §5.3, where the space side mints them in-process (§17).

### 5.6 The rules copy

**Its design is the gate note's** (§3 the fields, signing, checks, life; §4 level and marks; §5 cards; §10 requirements). This section states what the wire carries and where this spec departs. Security signed every part of it, the background part included, on 2026-11-25 (§14); W10's pull request still gets Security's read.

- **Frames.** For a new turn the copy goes first, by `rules.put`, and the start follows (§5.2). A change is pushed with `rules.put`, never polled. The end of a copy is `rules.drop`. A renewal is a new signed copy with a later end, in the `renewals` list of `computer.status`'s answer.
- **Who signs what.** The home's key signs, under its exact label list, only what the home sends:

  | Label line                | What the home signs under it                                                  | Where                                                                                                      |
  | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
  | `dorkos-session-relay/v1` | the handshake answers `welcome` and `refuse`; the link answer `link-response` | `answerHello` in `services/core/session-relay/channel.ts`; the link route's signer in `routes/hub-link.ts` |
  | `dorkos-agent-rules/v1`   | the rules copy                                                                | the rules signer (new, W10)                                                                                |

  Each label is a whole line ending in a newline, none a prefix of another; the list is exact and checked before anything is parsed; `HUB_KEY_SIGNS_ONLY` becomes this list. The computer's own key signs `finish` (`finishComputerHandshake` in `channel.ts`) and `link-request` (`signRelayLinkRequest` in `services/core/session-relay/link-signature.ts`); it never signs a copy. The signed bytes of a copy are a canonical encoding (fixed field order, no duplicate keys). The signed body carries `computerId`, `agentId`, `runId`, `version` and the copy's life.

- **The copy's life is a length of time.** The gate note's `notAfter` field is sent as `lifeMs`: at most 300,000 milliseconds (5 minutes). The computer counts it on its own steady clock, starting when the copy arrives. Neither side compares a wall-clock time with the other's. The home counts the same copy on its own steady clock, for `lifeMs` plus the silence timeout (90 seconds), from when the status that asked for its renewal arrived, or, for a copy pushed by `rules.put`, from when it was sent. So the home's view of a copy ends after the computer's whenever the copy arrives within the silence timeout (§17 amends the gate note's wording).
- **The acknowledgement.** The answer to `rules.put` is the computer's acknowledgement. For a stricter change (a pause, a lowered level, a new mark, an ended grant) with no acknowledgement in 5 seconds while the channel is up, the home sends `turn.stop` for that run (the gate note says `session.interrupt`; an agent computer is never sent that, and `turn.stop` is its per-run form). With none by the end of the home's view of the copy, the home counts the computer unhealthy: its agents' runs wait `computer_not_ready` until it acknowledges again.
- **Two live turns of one agent on one computer:** the version counts per run, and each change is pushed to every live run of that agent.
- **The version is kept on the run's row** (`rules_version`, a new column), raised in the transaction that signs each copy, so a renewal after the home restarts never carries a lower version than the computer last accepted.
- **A stricter push in flight when the channel drops.** The computer keeps the copy it has until that copy's life ends. When the channel is back, the home sends the push again at the same version; with no acknowledgement in 5 seconds, it sends `turn.stop`. If the channel does not come back, the run ends when the copy's life ends, at most 5 minutes later.
- **No copy, no run.** The computer refuses `turn.start` with `rules_missing` when no valid copy for that run has arrived (§5.2). With no valid copy, the computer's native checks take the strictest behaviour, as the gate note §3 says.
- **Where the computer holds copies.** Exactly as the gate note §3 says: the copy itself may be kept on the computer's disk, owned by the computer-side user, so a turn keeps its rules across a short drop; the last version accepted per run and the list of runs it was told to start are kept only in memory; a copy is accepted only for a run whose process still lives, so a restart never reopens an older copy. After a restart the computer deletes every copy on disk without reading it for a decision.
- **Never two runners.** No copy's life, in the home's view, passes the end of the agent's runner lease on that computer (§9): the home never signs or renews one that would. Until the one-runner step (W18) lands, renewals are bounded by the computer's presence instead: the home renews a copy only while the computer is present. When the runner lease lapses on the computer's own clock, the computer ends every run and every background copy of that agent at once.
- **Background work (Security's direction, specified here).** The gate note §3 ends a copy with a hard stop when its turn ends; its §12 leaves open whether it may outlive the turn while background work is registered. Under the same five conditions as its §12 item 9:
  1. When a run's turn ends with registered background work (step 6 §5.1, `background_work_registered`), the home re-signs that run's copy with `phase: 'background'`, the ids of the registered items (`backgroundWorkIds`) and `deadlineMs`, the time left until the deadline. With no registered work, the copy is dropped at the turn's end.
  2. In the `background` phase the copy decides only native calls the runtime attributes to one of those items. A native call it cannot attribute gets the strictest behaviour, which with nobody to ask is a refusal.
  3. It runs at the run's level and marks as they stood when the turn ended, or stricter. A lowered level or a new mark reaches it at once as a pushed version.
  4. Its life is still at most 5 minutes, renewed on `computer.status` only while an item is registered, the deadline has not passed and the runner lease holds. The deadline is the one the home holds for that run's tool calls (default four hours after the turn ends), measured on the home's steady clock; the computer's reports cannot extend it. **The home's refusal to renew past the deadline is what enforces it.** `deadlineMs` is never a wall-clock time: it is the milliseconds left when the copy was signed, counted on the computer's steady clock from receipt, like `lifeMs`. The computer may show it, and never relies on it: a copy it holds ends with its life whatever `deadlineMs` says.
  5. A person's stop, a pause, a lowered level, the end of the chat, the last item ending, the deadline, a move of the agent (§9) or the lapse of the runner lease ends it: the home sends `rules.drop`, the computer deletes the copy, and its next use is refused. If the drop cannot be sent, the copy ends with its life.
  6. New fields of the copy: `phase`, `backgroundWorkIds`, `deadlineMs` (§17 amends the gate note's §3 table).

### 5.7 Records, run facts and asks

- **Records.** Every record kind of step 6 §5.5 crosses as `records.put { id, bootId, record }`. The home writes each as **that computer's report**, kept apart from its own decision records and shown labelled with the computer's name (gate note §2 and §10).
- **Run facts** cross as `run.fact { id, bootId, fact }` (§5.2).
- **Asks** (`asks.raise`, step 6 §5.7) cross as calls. The home builds the card, waits for a person, writes any standing decision itself, and returns only the answer. With the home out of reach, the computer refuses the ask at once (gate note §3). A repeated `asks.raise` id joins the waiting card.
- **Native cards** are the runtime's own approval asks. They travel as approval events on the turn's `session.stream`, as the gate note §5 says. The home cleans the tool name, input and command before showing them, labels the card with the computer's name, never words it in DorkOS's own voice, and answers once with `turn.answer` (§5.2). A native answer approves nothing else. On reconnect the computer lists its pending asks in its status and the home shows them again; if the computer restarted, the ask is gone and the home withdraws the card.
- **Ids and repeats.** Each record, fact and ask carries a ULID made on the computer. The home keeps the ids it has written, keyed by `(computerId, bootId, callId)`, for 7 days after the run settles (or after the record's time, for a record with no run), and answers a repeat `{ ok: true }` without writing again. A record delivered twice is written once.
- **Order.** The computer sends outbox entries in the order they were made, one at a time per run, the next only after the last was answered. Entries with no run go in their own order.

### 5.8 The config notice, pause and resume

- **`config.changed { version, view }`.** The view (`ComputerConfigViewSchema`, new, strict) holds only sections a computer may read: the runtimes' defaults it runs, the agent context settings, and, when the computer runs Claude Code, the account section `initClaudeAccountApplier` acts on, as account references only. It never holds a credential, a sign-in, a key, a relay secret or an owner-trusted section, and proof O16 checks that no field can carry one. The computer applies the view over its own config file's values and answers with the version. It is sent after `finish` and on every change. Security signed the view (§14).
- **`agent.pause { agentId }`, `agent.resume { agentId }`.** The computer face's `pause` and `resume` (step 6 §5.6). The answer is the acknowledgement a stricter change needs (§5.6). The pause also rides the next pushed copy as `paused`. The four moments a pause can meet a run (step 5 §4.7) do what that section says, with the home's queue holding the words a pause holds.

### 5.9 `computer.status`

The computer's report of itself, every 30 seconds and at once when a run starts or ends. Under `computer.status.runs` it carries: `bootId`; `held`, each run it holds (accepted in this boot and its `ended` fact not yet acknowledged) with the rules version it holds (up to 64, `AGENT_COMPUTER_MAX_TURNS`); `owed`, the id of every run, of this boot or an earlier one, for which a fact is still in its outbox; its live chats with their working folders (for `endSessionsWhere`, §6.2); the `factsVersion` it holds for each agent; its pending native asks; its runtimes and their types (§6.2); and whether its outbox is full. The answer carries `renewals` (signed copies for runs still held, never past the runner lease) and `leases` (the runner leases this computer holds, §9). Each status renews the presence lease (§4.5) and, for each held run, the run's lease on the home (§6.5). The status handler on the home (§5.2, item 6) acts on it.

### 5.10 Contract files

The new schemas live in new files, so no file over 500 lines grows: `packages/shared/src/wire/turns.ts` (`turn.start`'s caps, `turn.stop`, `turn.answer`, `run.fact`, `sessions.end`), `wire/tools.ts` (the tools fields, `kit.call`, `cancel`), `wire/rules.ts` (`rules.put`, `rules.drop`, the label list, the canonical encoding), `wire/reads.ts`, `wire/status.ts` (`computer.status`, `agent.pause`, `config.changed`, the config view), `wire/records.ts` (`records.put`, `asks.raise`) and `wire/features.ts` (every feature id, the minimum set of §5.1, and `AGENT_COMPUTER_RELAY_FEATURES` extended by re-export). Each imports step 6's port schemas rather than copying them, and round-trips every schema through JSON in its tests (O2).

## 6. The home's pieces (S3, S4, S6)

### 6.1 The remote computer face, and the registry of faces

**Exists.** One computer face per process: `setComputerTurnPort` and `getComputerTurnPort` hold a single value (`computer-face.ts:44-58`). The `start_turn` handler asks it (`start-turn.ts:155`) and uses `holds` (`:87`) and `withdraw` (`:102`).

**Design.**

- **A registry of faces keyed by computer** replaces the single value: `computerFaces.for(hostId)`. This Mac's own face is registered under the Mac's own computer record (§8). The barrel keeps `getComputerTurnPort()` as the face for this Mac, so no in-process caller changes (N7).
- **Which face a run goes to.** The chat's binding names it when the chat has one (§6.3); otherwise the agent's placement (§9) does; a run with no agent, or whose agent has no placement, goes to this Mac's face, as today. After a move, a run of an old chat is never sent to the new computer: its chat stays bound to the computer it ran on (§6.4).
- **The remote computer face** (`services/core/hub-relay/remote-face/`, new; front door in step 6's side map, importing only the turn port's contract from `packages/shared`) implements the computer face over the channel to one computer:

  | Face method                              | Over the wire                                                                                                                           |
  | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
  | `start(request)`                         | `rules.put`, then `turn.start` with the whole request (§5.2); answers the computer's answer, or "no answer" when the connection dropped |
  | `stop(runId, backgroundWorkId?)`         | `turn.stop`; the receipt, or `unconfirmed` for a computer that is away (§5.2)                                                           |
  | `answer(runId, interactionId, response)` | `turn.answer`                                                                                                                           |
  | `isLive(runId)`                          | `live`, `not live` or `unknown`, from the last `computer.status` (§5.2)                                                                 |
  | `withdraw(runId)`                        | answered on the home: true, unless the run is `starting` there, then false                                                              |
  | `holds(runId)`                           | answered on the home: true from `starting` until the run's `ended` fact or its decision after lost contact or a new boot                |
  | `removeQueued(sessionId, runId)`         | answered on the home, on the home's queue for remote chats (§6.4)                                                                       |
  | `pause(agentId)`, `resume(agentId)`      | `agent.pause`, `agent.resume`                                                                                                           |

- **On the computer**, the wire's requests land on its own computer face, the same code a Mac uses in-process (S3). There is no second way to start a turn.

### 6.2 The forwarding runtimes (S4)

**Exists.** Every caller resolves a runtime through `runtimeRegistry` (`resolveForSession`, `services/core/runtime-registry.ts:441`; `resolveForAgent`, `:198`). A chat is bound to a runtime type once (ADR-0255). `computer-local` is a runtime registered under its own type that reaches another machine (§10).

**Design.** **One forwarding runtime for each computer and runtime type**, registered under the type `remote:<hostId>:<runtimeType>` (`services/core/hub-relay/forwarding-runtime/`, new; front door in step 6's side map; no runtime SDK). They are created from the runtimes the computer names in its status (§5.9) and stay registered while it is away, so a chat bound to one keeps its binding. **Locks stay on the home. A forwarding runtime never starts, steers or stops a turn: the turn port is the door.** Its refusal is an error with the code `REMOTE_TURNS_USE_TURN_PORT`, thrown before any frame is sent. A wire read while the computer is away throws `REMOTE_COMPUTER_AWAY`, which the app shows as the computer being away.

**The stream cursor.** On the home, a durable stream names each frame by the chat, a generation and a number (`streamFrameId`, `services/core/streams/session-stream-delivery.ts:230`). It accepts a client's resume only when the cursor's generation equals what `streamGeneration` answers (`:136-157`), and passes `subscribeSession` only the number (`sinceCursor`, `agent-runtime.ts:2187-2192`). A generation looks like `g<n>`; `g0` is `UNOWNED_STREAM_GENERATION` (`packages/shared/src/session-stream.ts:1322`), and the wire's cursor pattern requires that shape (`session-relay.ts:240-243`). So the mapping is:

- The forwarding runtime keeps, for each chat, the computer's `bootId` and the computer's own generation for that chat, learned from `session.snapshot` (read when the chat is first opened) and from every stream frame.
- Whenever either changes, it mints a new home generation of its own (`mintStreamGeneration`, `session-stream.ts:1347`) and remembers which computer generation and boot it stands for. `streamGeneration` answers that home generation at once, without waiting.
- Before it has seen anything for the chat, it answers `g0`, so the client's first resume gets one fresh snapshot.
- `subscribeSession(ctx, sessionId, sinceCursor)` sends `session.stream` with a wire cursor built from the chat id, the computer's generation it holds and `sinceCursor`. The numbers are the computer's own, so they pass through unchanged.
- If the computer finds the cursor stale (it restarted, or its counter was replaced), the stream ends with `StaleResumeCursorError` (`session-stream.ts:1365`); the forwarding runtime mints a new home generation, and the durable stream sends a fresh snapshot, as it does today for any stale cursor.

**Paths and lists.** Paths in reads are the computer's own (§5.2). The computer answers lists unfiltered; the forwarding runtime filters them by who may see them before any caller does (step 6 §5.12).

**Conformance.** Each forwarding runtime passes the shared runtime conformance suite (`runtimeConformance`) for its reads, over the wire against the fake computer of §12, with its refusals declared as unproven reasons in the suite's own pattern.

### 6.3 Where a chat's computer is recorded

A new space table in the machine store, `chat_computer_bindings` (`session_id` primary key, `host_id`, `agent_id`, `runtime_type` (on the computer, for "Runs on"), `settings_json`, `title`, `bound_at`). **It is written by the `start_turn` handler, from the agent's placement, in the transaction that writes `starting`** (§5.2), together with `session_metadata.runtime` (`remote:<hostId>:<runtimeType>`). It is written once and never moves (ADR-0255); after an agent moves, its old chats keep their binding (§9). `runtimeRegistry.resolveForSession` returns the bound forwarding runtime. A ticket names the table's move to the space store with the private tenant (item 16).

### 6.4 The queue and admission over the wire

- **Which store and which face.** A chat with a binding (§6.3) uses the store and the face its binding names. A chat with no binding yet uses those its agent's placement names (§9), or this Mac's when the agent has none. So a new remote chat's first message goes to the remote queue, never to the Mac's.
- **The home keeps the queue.** A remote chat's waiting words live in a new space table, `remote_chat_queue` (`id`, `session_id`, `position`, `content`, `client_message_id`, `state`: `queued`, `in_turn` or `restored`, `created_at`), served through the same queue API and the same `queue_update` stream as `session_message_queue`, so the app shows, edits, moves and removes them as it does today. The queue routes pick the store by the rule above.
- **Who writes a row.** The space face writes it at `start()` for a run whose words belong in a composer (`wordsWaitInComposer(origin)`, step 5 §3.2), in the transaction that writes the run, and the run's `queue_message_id` points at it. Other runs keep their words at their source, as step 5 says.
- **Who takes the next row.** The `start_turn` handler, when the chat has no live run, no run awaiting its computer, no ended run without a recovery decision and no continuation waiting (step 5's launch gate, §7.5), takes the oldest row in `position` order, then the oldest run without a row, by step 5 §7.5's rules. A row goes `in_turn` at `started` and is deleted with `delivered`, as step 5 §4.3 does for a Mac's row; a run that ends `lost_contact` turns its row `restored` (§5.2, item 7). The home's dispatcher pump is not used for remote chats.
- **After a move.** A chat stays bound to the computer it ran on (§9). Its waiting rows are kept and never sent (their runs wait `bound_elsewhere`, §9); the person sees them with the chat's line "This chat stays on <old computer>. Start a new chat to continue." and may copy or remove them.
- **Admission.** For a run bound for a remote computer, step 5's admission runs every check for every run, composer words included (on a Mac those skip the chat-busy check because the dispatcher queues them; over the wire nothing on the computer queues). It adds five waits, never refusals. Each applies only to a run that is still `queued` and was never sent; a run that was sent keeps its status while contact is lost (§5.2):

  | Wait                 | When                                                                                                                                               | Released by                                                                               |
  | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
  | `computer_not_ready` | The computer does not name the minimum feature set (§5.1), did not acknowledge a stricter change in time (§5.6), or answered `rules_missing` twice | Its next hello that names the set; its next acknowledgement                               |
  | `computer_away`      | The computer is not present                                                                                                                        | The first status after its next promoted connection (§4.4). This is the only release      |
  | `computer_busy`      | Its status says its outbox is full (§7.2)                                                                                                          | A status that says it is not                                                              |
  | `moving`             | The agent is being moved (§9), or a start was answered `not_this_computer` (§5.2)                                                                  | A status from the agent's new computer that names its runner lease                        |
  | `bound_elsewhere`    | After a move, the run's chat is bound to the computer the agent left (§9)                                                                          | The agent moved back to that computer; otherwise never, and the person may remove the row |

  Each release wakes the waiting runs at once through admission's wake registration, as `agentPause()?.onResumed` does for `paused`, so a run starts when its reason clears and not at the next retry.

- **Stop.** The turn port's `stop` sends `turn.stop` for one run and leaves the queue alone; the person's Stop button keeps its meaning (it stops the run and empties the chat's queue), with the queue emptied on the home.

### 6.5 Who owns a remote run, and what a restart, a new boot or lost contact does

- **Owner.** A run on another computer is owned by that computer's `(hostId, bootId)`: its `owner_epoch` is `host:<hostId>:<bootId>`.
- **Its lease is measured on the home's steady clock.** The home keeps each remote run's lease end in memory, measured from when the status that renewed it arrived, on the home's steady clock. `lease_until` is saved only for the home's own restart: after a restart, every saved remote lease is treated as running one full length (the copy's life plus the silence timeout) from the moment the home booted, whatever time is saved. Nothing compares a saved wall-clock time with the clock now, so a clock that jumps on either side moves no lease. The same rule holds for the runner lease (§9). Security signed this (§14).
- **The home's own restart.** The home's boot recovery (step 5 §7.1 and §7.3) **skips runs owned by a computer** and leaves them to the rules below; a remote run is never `process_died` because the home restarted. In `services/runs/run-store.ts`: `lease()` (`:594-596`) stamps this boot and a wall-clock `leaseUntil`; a remote run gets a new `leaseRemote(hostId, bootId)` instead, which stamps the remote owner and keeps the lease's end in memory as above. `renewLeases()` (`:609`) is unchanged (it renews only this boot's runs); a new `renewRemote(hostId, bootId, runIds)` renews from a status. `deadLiveRuns()` (`:651-666`) and step 5's recovery read leave out every run whose owner starts `host:`. When the home is back, its computers redial (while they redial, their runs are `unknown` and treated as live), and each first status renews the runs it holds (§17 amends step 5).
- **A new `bootId` (the computer restarted).** Every run its earlier boot owned is **awaiting its computer**: `isLive` answers `unknown`, the chat's queue holds, and the home writes nothing yet. (Step 5's word "undecided" stays for an ended run with no recovery decision.) The status handler calls the **decide step** (`decideLeftoverRun`, in `services/runs/recovery/`, new) for each such run once the computer's status no longer lists it as `owed`, so every fact the earlier boot left for it has arrived. **A fact that arrived meanwhile wins**: a run whose `ended` fact came ends with that ending. The decide step then applies step 5 §7.3 step 4's rules to the run as it now stands (a `starting` run with no `started` fact goes back to `queued` by transition 8, since the computer commits and flushes `started` to its outbox before the runtime has the words, §5.2; a `started` run ends `process_died` with `delivery: unknown` and its row `restored`; a `running` run ends `process_died` and recovery's rules decide whether it continues, at most three times per chain and only for a runtime that declares it can), and stamps `recovery_decided_at` in the same transaction, so step 5's launch gate (§7.5) releases the chat (§17 amends step 5 §7.3).
- **Lost contact.** When the home's view of a sent run's copy ends with still no contact, the run ends `lost_contact` with `delivery: unknown`, by transitions 7 and 11, 11 or 12 (§5.2, item 7), and its `recovery_decided_at` is stamped in the same transaction, so recovery never continues it. `lost_contact` is observed: the home did not hear from the computer within the copy's life. A fact for that run that arrives later changes nothing but `ending_detail` (§5.2, item 8).
- **New closed values.** The ending `lost_contact`; the waits `computer_not_ready`, `computer_away`, `computer_busy`, `moving`, `bound_elsewhere`; the refusals `rules_missing` and `not_this_computer`. No new transition. `turn-port.ts` is over 500 lines and may not grow, so **the first turns step (W15)** moves `RefusalReasonSchema`, the wait reasons and `RunEnding` into `packages/shared/src/turn-port-vocab.ts`, re-exported from `turn-port.ts`, and adds the values. The database mirrors those lists (`packages/db/src/schema/runs/turn-runs.ts:24-41`, `TURN_RUN_ENDINGS` and `TURN_RUN_WAIT_REASONS`) with CHECK constraints (`turn_runs_wait_reason` at `:176-179`, `turn_runs_ending` at `:184-187`), so W15 also adds the migration that widens both, and the `rules_version` column (§5.6). A later step that adds a value extends the migration in its own pull request (§17).

### 7.2 The outbox

- **What:** every `run.fact` and `records.put` the computer has made and the home has not answered. Each entry: `id` (a ULID), `bootId`, `runId?`, `kind`, `body`, `createdAt`.
- **Where:** a machine-store table, `computer_outbox`, on the computer's list of step 6's §6 (§17). It survives a restart of the computer; entries keep the `bootId` of the boot that made them.
- **How long:** until the home answers it; then it is deleted.
- **Order:** §5.7. After a restart, entries of earlier boots are sent first. While a run has an entry here, the status lists it as `owed` (§5.9).
- **Bound:** at most 10,000 entries or 64 MiB. Past either, nothing is dropped and nothing is refused: the status says the outbox is full, and the home's admission waits the computer's runs `computer_busy` until it drains (§6.4).

| The id of every run it accepted in this boot, for its `already_held` answer (§5.2) | memory                                                                                               | Until the home acknowledges that run's `ended` fact, or the process ends |
| `kit.call` outcomes, by request id                                                 | memory                                                                                               | 10 minutes                                                               |
| The outbox                                                                         | `computer_outbox`                                                                                    | Until answered                                                           |
| Runtime transcripts and the durable session stream                                 | as today (runtime-owned, ADR-0310)                                                                   | As today                                                                 |

### 7.4 While the home cannot be reached

The gate note's §3 says what native calls do: those the copy allows keep running until its life ends, then the turn ends on that computer. The rest:

- **DorkOS tool calls** fail at once with a plain message that the home cannot be reached, except a call already sent: it is sent again with the same id after a reconnect, inside the copy's life, and joins or gets the stored outcome (§5.3).
- **Asks** are refused, since nobody can see the card.
- **Facts and records** go to the outbox and are sent, in order, after the next promoted connection.
- **The stream** is not buffered on the wire: the computer's durable session stream keeps the events, and the home resumes from its last cursor.
- **No new turn starts:** only the home starts turns.
- **Background copies** end with their life; renewals stop with the channel.
- **The runner lease** lapses on the computer's clock 5 minutes after the status its last renewal answered; then the computer ends every run and copy of each agent whose lease lapsed (§9).
- **The dialer** keeps trying, with the backoff of §4.3.
- **After a reconnect** the computer sends `computer.status` first (so the home learns its `bootId`, the runs it holds, the runs it owes facts for, and its versions), then drains its outbox, then serves requests.

## 8. The computer record (S7; DOR-5168)

**Exists.** No computers table (§1.7). The home's linked-computer file holds keys for the channel; the space store's `agents.runs_on` holds a kind with no id; `worksOn` is a field of the public directory contract that nothing writes; `dork:host` parses and nothing mints it.

**Design.** A computer is space data. It lives where that space's agents live today: for the private space, the machine store of its home (`packages/db/src/schema/space-computers/`, new), with a ticket naming its move to the space store when the private space becomes a tenant (item 16, ADR `261122-173853`). Its owner on the server is a new space-side domain, `services/space-computers/` (a working name; DOR-4921 settles names), which `contributing/project-structure.md` gains in the same pull request. The record's type is `SpaceComputer` and its table `space_computers`: `ComputerRecord` already names the local sealed computer's record (`services/computers/store.ts`), and step 6 plans `ComputerRecordSchema` for the records sink.

**Which agents work on it** is the placement (§9), joined, never a copy. **`DirectoryAgent.worksOn`** (`packages/cloud-api/src/directory.ts:138-142`) is projected only for an agent placed on a `cloud` computer, as its `cloudComputerId`; for every other agent it stays null, as the contract says (item 13 wires the report).

**Read** through two capabilities, `computers.list` and `computers.get`, projected onto the HTTP API under ADR `261125-114705`'s rules (the existing `/api/computers` routes stay those of the local sealed computer until §10 retires them). Showing them in one sidebar grouped by computer is item 13's.

**Written** only by linking (a new record), unlinking (the record is kept, marked unlinked, so its agents' runs still name it), presence (§4.5) and a person renaming it. No agent caller may write it (§5.3).

**Must not change.** The linked-computer file stays the channel's key store, and the channel reads nothing else to accept a dial.

## 9. One runner at a time (S8; DOR-2874, its step DOR-2883)

**Exists.** Per-chat locks, the one-live-run-per-chat index and the one-process-per-data-folder lock (§1.8). Nothing says which computer runs an agent.

**Design.**

- **Placement.** A new space table in the machine store, `space_computer_agents` (`agent_id` primary key, `host_id`, `home_path`, `desk_path`, `facts_version`, `placed_at`, `lease_until`). It is the agent's record of where it runs. **It is not a field in `.dork/agent.json`**, which departs from `design-agent-computers.md` §1 (`worksOn` in `agent.json`): where an agent runs is the person's decision, and an agent can write its own folder (§17). `.dork/agent.json` stays the agent's identity (ADR-0043). An agent with no row runs on the home, as today. Only a person writes or moves a placement (§5.3; signed by Security, §14).
- **The runner lease.** The computer named by the placement holds the agent's runner lease while it is present. It is renewed in the answer to each `computer.status` (`leases`) and lasts 5 minutes. The computer counts it on its steady clock from the moment it sent the status the renewal answered. The home keeps its end in memory, on its own steady clock, from when the status the renewal answered arrived, plus the silence timeout; the saved `lease_until` is used only after the home restarts, as a full length from boot (§6.5). Neither side compares wall clocks, so a clock that jumps moves no lease.
- **Never two runners.** No rules copy, background copies included, lives past the runner lease in the home's view (§5.6). When the lease lapses on the computer's clock, the computer ends every run and every background copy of that agent at once.
- **A start is refused for a computer that is not the agent's:** admission sends a run to the computer its chat's binding names when it has one, else to the agent's placement's computer. After a move, a never-sent run of an old chat bound to the old computer stays `queued` with the wait reason `bound_elsewhere` (the chat is bound to a computer the agent no longer runs on). Its `start_turn` effect is parked: not retried and not cancelled, so nothing is sent. It is released only if the agent is moved back to that computer; if the person removes the row, the run is cancelled with it (step 5's transition 5); a computer refuses `turn.start` with `not_this_computer` for an agent it holds no lease for, and the run waits `moving` until a status from the agent's computer names the lease, a fault only when no move is under way (§5.2); and the home refuses a `tools.call` under a run another computer owns (§5.3).
- **Moving an agent** to another computer of the same home is the deliberate move of ADR `261125-113808` rule 4. **In this spec a move is for an agent with no live run**:
  1. A person asks to move the agent. If it has a live run, the app says "Wait for its current work to finish, or stop it, then move it." New runs wait `moving`.
  2. The home stops renewing the agent's lease on the old computer, sends `rules.drop` for every background copy of the agent, and sends `agents.sync` remove.
  3. It waits for the later of the old lease's end and the last copy's end, in its own view, or for the old computer's answer to the remove if that comes first and the computer reports no run and no copy of the agent.
  4. In one transaction: the placement names the new computer, and the move is recorded.
  5. It sends `agents.sync` put to the new computer, which takes the lease on its next status. The move ends, and its `moving` waits are released, only when a status from the new computer names the lease; waiting runs then start there.
- **A run still `queued` when the move starts** was never sent, so nothing of it is on the old computer. If its chat has no binding, it follows the placement to the new computer once the move ends. If its chat is bound to the old computer, it stays queued there and is never sent (§6.4).
- **Chats after a move.** A chat's transcript belongs to the runtime on the computer that ran it (ADR-0310), and its binding is written once (ADR-0255). So **the agent's existing chats stay bound to the old computer and take no new turn**. The person sees them read-only (readable while that computer is present), with the line "This chat stays on <old computer>. Start a new chat to continue." New chats run on the new computer. What else travels with an agent stays the open point ADR `261125-113808` names (§19, question 4).
- **Moving an agent to another home** (from a Mac to the cloud, or back) is that ADR's other move, of the whole agent. It needs its own design and is not built here.

## 10. The local sealed computer (S9; DOR-5167)

**Exists.** A Lima VM on a Mac (darwin and arm64 only), behind the `computers.enabled` experiment, running plain OpenCode as the guest user `agent` (`services/computers/guest/guest-recipe.ts:31`). The host runs an OpenCode client against the guest (`services/runtimes/opencode/computer/provider.ts`), rewrites its HTTP (`wire.ts`), maps folders and chats, and bridges the guest's MCP calls back to the host's loopback tool listener (`startup/computer-mcp-bridge.ts:18`). The guest's firewall lets it reach the host (`192.168.5.2`) only on DNS and the MCP bridge's port (`guest-recipe.ts:95-100`). The host composes it as a runtime with the type `computer-local` (`startup/computers.ts:38`, registered at `:127`). It passes the shared runtime conformance suite as an in-process runtime against a faked guest (`services/runtimes/opencode/computer/__tests__/conformance.test.ts`, DOR-4902).

**Design.**

- **The guest runs the computer entry** of step 6, with the role `agent-computer`, as a guest user of its own (`dorkos`), separate from the `agent` user that runs agent processes and from `desktop`. The computer entry's pin, signing key, outbox and held copies belong to `dorkos`; `agent` can neither read nor write them (gate note §3).
- **The host reaches it through the same remote computer face and forwarding runtimes as any other computer.** The guest dials the host's DorkOS port at `192.168.5.2`, the host as the VM sees it, path `/relay/computer`; its pin says `linkedVia: 'local-vm'` (§4.7). **The guest recipe changes**: the rule at `guest-recipe.ts:100` lets through the host's DorkOS port instead of the MCP bridge's port, which retires.
- **Linking** happens at create: the host mints a link code bound to the role `agent-computer` and the kind `local-sealed`, hands it to the guest on the standard input of its exec channel (never on a command line or in a file), and the guest links at `/relay/link` as any computer does.
- **The guest's DorkOS is the host's exact version,** installed by the guest recipe from the pinned artifact (`services/computers/backend/artifact-pins.ts`). A version change of the host reinstalls it at the next start.
- **What moves into the guest:** the chat map between DorkOS chat ids and the guest's OpenCode sessions (`services/runtimes/opencode/computer/session-map.ts`) and the folder mapping between host and guest paths (`folders.ts`). The guest's computer entry owns its own paths and chats, as every computer does, and the host never maps a path on the computer (§5.2).
- **What retires,** once the guest passes the wire conformance suite and, through the forwarding runtime, the shared runtime conformance suite: the host-side OpenCode client and its HTTP rewriting (`services/runtimes/opencode/computer/`), the MCP bridge (`startup/computer-mcp-bridge.ts`) and the `computer-local` runtime type.
- **Chats already bound to `computer-local`** are rebound once, by an upgrade step, to `remote:<hostId>:opencode` and the `local-sealed` record, with a changelog fragment. This is a one-time exception to ADR-0255's "written once", listed in §17. It is safe because nothing moves: the same guest, with the same OpenCode store, the same transcripts, and now the same chat map and folder mapping, which moved into the guest with them, runs the chat before and after; only the name of the path to it changes.

**Must not change.** The VM's lifecycle (`ComputerService`), its desktop view and its control lease (`frame`, `acquire`, `release`, `input`), which are not turns and keep their own routes; the `computers.enabled` experiment and its default; and that the VM never starts on boot. The agent's home (its record, rules and chat list) stays on the host, as for every remote computer; the guest holds and maps its own paths.

## 11. Adding a machine (S10; DOR-5169)

**Exists.** Nothing (§1.9).

**Design.** One command on the home installs the matching DorkOS on another computer, links it with a one-time code, starts the computer entry there, and the computer dials back. The same contract runs over both pipes; the pipe is for reach only.

**Over SSH, first (developers).** `dorkos machine add <ssh destination> [--name <name>]`:

1. Uses the person's own `ssh` program, so their keys, agent and known-hosts file are used as they are and DorkOS holds no SSH secret. An unknown host key is asked about by `ssh` itself; DorkOS never turns host-key checking off.
2. Checks the far side: operating system, CPU and Node version against the install script's floor.
3. Installs exactly the home's DorkOS version (`npm install -g dorkos@<version>`, as `apps/site/scripts/install.sh` does), under a dedicated user for the computer side and a second user for agent processes, when the far side lets it make them; otherwise it stops and says what it needs.
4. Opens an SSH reverse forward from the home to the far side, so the far computer reaches the home's `/relay/link` and `/relay/computer` at a loopback address on its own side. The home supervises the forward and restarts it with backoff.
5. Mints a link code on the home, bound to the role `agent-computer` and the kind `added`, and passes it to the far side's `dorkos machine link` **on standard input**, never on a command line, in the environment or in a file. The code is bound to that one SSH forward: a redemption through any other pipe is refused (§4.6), and the home sets `linkedVia: 'ssh-forward'` from the code (§4.7).
6. Starts the computer entry there as a service of the operating system (a systemd user service on Linux, a launch agent on macOS), so it starts at boot.
7. Waits for the first promoted connection, then shows the new computer record.

`dorkos machine list`, `dorkos machine remove <name>` (unlinks, stops the far service, and leaves the far side's files for the person to delete) and `dorkos machine update <name>` (installs the home's new version on the far side) complete the set. Each is a capability first, then a command line verb, and only a person may call them (§5.3).

**Over DorkOS's tunnel, second (founders).** The same steps, from the app, with a sign-in in place of SSH: the person runs one install line on the far machine, signs in with their DorkOS account there, and approves the machine on the home. The code is bound to the home's remote access address, and a redemption through any other pipe is refused (§4.6). The far computer reaches the home's `/relay/link` and `/relay/computer` through the home's own remote access address. **The sign-in only lets the far machine fetch the one-time code the person approved; the home pins the far computer's own key when the code is redeemed, as for every link, and the hosted service never vouches for a key** (`packages/cloud-api/src/hubs.ts:69`). Security signed this (§14). Anything this needs from the hosted service lands first in `packages/cloud-api` (N8).

**Requirements** (from `contributing/architecture-target.md` §2): identity fails closed (N5); no secrets in configuration or on a command line (the code travels on standard input or through the signed-in flow; the relay secret and keys live in owner-only files); presence is a lease that must be renewed (§4.5); at most one live instance (§4.4); a stop is final and works when the agent is stuck (§5.2).

**A Mac home that sleeps.** An added machine's turn needs its home: when the Mac sleeps, the channel goes quiet, and every turn on its added machines ends within about five minutes, at the end of its copy's life, as `lost_contact`. Pick (§19, question 8): **the Mac stays awake while a computer it is home to has a live run**, through the keep-awake service it already runs during a turn (`services/core/keep-awake/`).

**Must not change.** Nothing on the home, other than the new record, until the person places an agent on the new computer.

## 16. Build steps

## 18. Risks

- **A large new surface on a trust boundary.** About thirty operations cross a line the gate note treats as untrusted. Each is a feature both sides must name, every trust decision has a row in §14, and the wire suite runs every refusal in-process and over the wire.
- **Callers that still call a runtime directly.** A caller that starts, stops or answers a turn through a runtime breaks for remote chats, by design (O14). W9 moves the known ones first, and W15 waits for step 5's caller steps.
- **Time across machines.** Leases are counted on each side's steady clock, never by comparing wall clocks, with the home's view always the longer one by the silence timeout. The margin is a judgment call to tune from real use; L2, L4 and L6 test the edges.
- **Runs awaiting their computer after a restart.** A restarted computer that never finishes delivering the facts it owes keeps those runs awaiting it and their chats held. The app shows them as waiting on that computer, and lost contact ends them if the computer goes away.
- **The outbox can fill** while the home is away for a long time. It never drops and never refuses; the computer's runs wait `computer_busy`, which a person can see.
- **Chats whose computer is away**, and chats that stay on an agent's old computer after a move, cannot be read until that computer is back. The app says so rather than showing an empty chat.
- **SSH reverse forwards are fragile.** The home supervises them; the computer redials. The tunnel path (W24) is the answer for people who cannot keep one up.
- **A Mac home kept awake.** Keeping the Mac awake while an added machine runs a turn uses power; the alternative is lost turns (§19, question 8).
- **The browser tables' tie** (step 6 §6) blocks the agent's browser on a remote computer until its issue lands; W15 refuses such a start plainly.
- **Version drift on added machines.** A home update leaves added machines on the old version until `dorkos machine update`. Their runs wait `computer_not_ready` only if the minimum set changes; the app shows each computer's version.

## 19. Open questions, each with a pick

1. **SSH first, or the tunnel first, for adding a machine.** Pick: SSH first. It needs no hosted service, and a Mac is home. Other option: the tunnel first, for founders.
2. **Where a remote chat records its computer.** Pick: on the home, beside the chat's runtime binding (`chat_computer_bindings`, written at the first start, §6.3). Other option: on the computer only, with the home asking each computer which chats it has.
3. **The rules copy outliving its turn while background work is registered** (gate note §12). Decided: Security signed §5.6's design on 2026-11-25, with `deadlineMs` never relied on by the computer. The option not taken: the copy ends with the turn, and background work on another computer runs no native tool after it.
4. **What travels with an agent when it moves, what an agent that lives in the cloud may do on the person's Mac, and how the app on a Mac reaches agents in the cloud** (ADR `261125-113808`, still open). Pick: not on this contract; a move here takes the placement only, and a link between two homes is item 13's design. Other option: let a cloud home place agents on a Mac's computer face here.
5. **Where an agent's placement lives.** Pick: a home record (`space_computer_agents`) that only a person writes (§9). Other option: `worksOn` in `.dork/agent.json`, as `design-agent-computers.md` §1 proposed, which an agent could change in its own folder.
6. **Who makes the per-run local tokens over the wire.** Pick: the computer, for its own loopback listener; they never cross, and the home checks the run's placement against the channel's proven computer (§5.5). Other option: the home mints them and sends them with the start, as step 6 §5.3 does in-process.
7. **A computer without the minimum feature set.** Pick: its runs wait `computer_not_ready` until it says hello with the set (§5.1). Other option: the strictest level with no native allows, which the gate note also allows and which would run turns on an older computer.
8. **A Mac home that sleeps while an added machine runs a turn.** Pick: the Mac stays awake while a computer it is home to has a live run (§11). Other option: it sleeps, and those runs end as `lost_contact` within about five minutes.
9. **A move of an agent with a live run.** Pick: not allowed in this spec; the person waits for its work to finish or stops it (§9). Other option: the move stops the run itself, which ends work the person did not ask to end.

Decided by the rulings and no longer open: one channel, extended (§4); the home end wherever the space side is (§4.6); turns through the turn port on both ends (§6.1); reads through forwarding runtimes (§6.2); a small outbox on the computer (§7.2); the computer as a record (§8); one runner (§9); the local sealed computer on the contract (§10); SSH then the tunnel (§11).
