# Let a Community member erase their account and messages

**Slug:** community-member-erasure
**Author:** Claude (for DOR-2247)
**Date:** 2026-09-23

---

## 1) Intent & Assumptions

- **Task brief:** A person on a Community host can leave a community or be removed from it, but nothing erases what they wrote. Any host serving people in the EU (GDPR Art. 17) or California (CCPA deletion) will be asked to erase a person. Build erasure as its own contract, owned by the person (self-service) and the community owner, never by host authority. It rewrites history that is immutable today (entries, mentions, attachments, export contents) and must decide what a thread shows where an erased message was. It blocks the launch of hosted communities, so scope it to what a host needs at launch.
- **Source material:** Open Question 7 of `specs/community-host-operator-api/02-specification.md` (branch `flow/community-host-operator-api`, DOR-2243), which excludes member erasure from host authority and frames the questions this spec answers.
- **Assumptions:**
  - The Community server (`apps/community`) is the controller's system. Its PostgreSQL rows and BlobStore objects are what the host can be held to. Copies outside it (downloaded exports, other people's DorkOS installations, screenshots, host backups) are outside its reach, and the product must say so rather than imply otherwise.
  - The public wire (`@dorkos/shared/community-wire`) is parsed with `z.strictObject` by DorkOS installations that update on their own schedule. An erasure must not add a field to an existing wire object or a new SSE event type, or older installations would reject every entry.
  - Operator pre-authorized decisions in this programme (brief). Decisions below are made with a recommendation and recorded; none is left to ask.
- **Out of scope:**
  - Host-started erasure of any kind, including a host acting on an emailed request. The host's path is the existing offline password recovery (`src/recover-password.ts`) so the person can sign in and erase themselves.
  - Deleting individual messages as moderation, or editing messages. Neither exists today.
  - Finding a person's name or words quoted in free text by other people or their agents.
  - Reaching into downloaded export files, host backups, or a DorkOS installation that is offline or modified.
  - Legal holds. The Community server does not implement one.

## 2) Pre-reading Log

- `AGENTS.md`: the Cloud rule (nothing private in this repo), the writing-for-humans standard for UI copy, Worktrees, Hard Rules.
- `specs/community-host-operator-api/02-specification.md` (branch): hold, host-started deletion, import with historical members (`members.user_id` nullable, `origin='imported'`), migrations 0012 to 0015, the rule that host authority never reads or writes community content, and Open Question 7 (this work). Its phase 4 already changes the owner export's member query to a `LEFT JOIN` so historical authors survive a re-export.
- `decisions/260920-201101-separate-community-retention-from-permanent-tenant-deletion.md`: archive is retention, deletion is a separate owner-authorized, seven-day cancellable job with a blob inventory and a bounded worker. Host authority cannot delete an active tenant or read content. This spec reuses the same shape one level down: a cancellable window, a bounded idempotent worker, blobs through the inventory.
- `decisions/260920-192429-scope-host-accounts-through-immutable-community-memberships.md`: accounts and sessions are host-wide; community authority is through memberships unique on `(community_id, user_id)`; host operators are separate from community roles. So "erase my account" spans every membership on the host, and "erase me here" is one membership.
- `apps/community/src/schema.ts`: every table and every column that can hold a person's data (inventory in the Codebase Map below).
- `apps/community/src/routes/members.ts`: `remove()` (used by leave and removal) deactivates the member, deletes channel memberships and read cursors, revokes agents, agent credentials, grants, and pairings, and writes one audit row. It keeps `display_name`, `handle`, `user_id`, and every entry. Leave needs a password and the community name typed exactly; an owner cannot leave.
- `apps/community/src/routes/entries.ts`: entries are immutable. There is no edit or delete route and no edit history table. `payload_hash` is a SHA-256 over the text, mentions, parent, and attachment ids, kept for idempotent replays. Mentions come from the caller (`body.mentions`) or from `resolveCommunityMentions` (`src/mentions.ts`) against the joined roster, so a mention row can exist without an `@handle` token in the text.
- `apps/community/src/mentions.ts`: the address form is `@([A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9_.-]*)`, ignoring fenced code, inline code, and quoted lines. `@[erased]` can never match it.
- `apps/community/src/handles.ts`: handles are derived from the display name at join and are never renamed. `community_handles` reserves them for the life of the member row.
- `apps/community/src/routes/events.ts`, `src/cursor.ts`: live streams and history are seq-ordered per channel; the channel `epoch` invalidates cursors and closes live streams when it changes.
- `apps/community/src/routes/exports.ts`: personal and owner exports are zips that live for one hour in the BlobStore. The owner export includes every member with `email` (inner join to `"user"`), every entry, every bound attachment, and the tenant audit trail.
- `apps/community/src/auth.ts`: Better Auth with email and password plus optional Google and GitHub. `user.deleteUser` is **not** enabled, and `members.user_id`, `invite_uses.user_id`, `host_operators.user_id`, `host_audit_events.actor_user_id`, and `bootstrap_grants.revoked_by` all reference `"user"(id)` without a cascade, so a host account cannot be closed today.
- `apps/community/src/deletion-worker.ts`, `src/storage/managed-blobs.ts`: the worker and blob-inventory pattern to reuse.
- `packages/shared/src/community-wire.ts`: `CommunityWireEntrySchema` and `CommunityWireEventSchema` are strict. `text` accepts any string; `authorDisplayName` needs at least one character.
- `apps/server/src/services/communities/remote/mirror-store.ts`: each DorkOS installation keeps a persisted mirror of the community rooms it can read (`community_mirror_entries`, `room_entries`, and an external author record with the display name). `importEntry` never updates an entry it already has.
- `apps/server/src/services/search/registry.ts`, `row-frontier.ts`: DorkOS message search indexes every `room_entries` row, mirrored rooms included, above a per-room watermark, and assumes rows are only appended. An in-place edit is never re-indexed.
- `apps/server/src/services/communities/remote/remote-community-adapter.ts`: a live stream's `closed` event becomes `room_closed`; a `410` becomes `StaleCommunityCursorError`.

## 3) Codebase Map

- **Primary components/modules:**
  - `apps/community/src/routes/members.ts` (leave, removal, transfer): its `remove()` is reused as the first erasure step; transfer gains a guard.
  - A new `apps/community/src/routes/erasures.ts`: account routes on the `hostApi` router (which already serves `/memberships` to a signed-in account) and owner routes on `communityApi`.

  - `apps/community/src/routes/exports.ts`: owner export member query becomes a `LEFT JOIN`; export commit refuses a snapshot taken before a completed erasure.
  - `apps/community/src/routes/entries.ts`: the new redaction feed.
  - `apps/community/src/auth.ts`: account deletion stays off in Better Auth; the server deletes the account itself inside the erasure transaction.
  - Browser: `src/browser/components/Manage.tsx` (per-community controls), `CommunityChooser.tsx` (host root: account deletion and pending erasures), `CommunityAdministration.tsx` (owner erasure of former members).

- **Shared dependencies:** `transaction`, `lockActiveCommunity`, `requireMember`, `requireSessionUser` (`src/data.ts`); `hashSecret`, `signValue` (`src/security.ts`); the managed-blob inventory and `pending_blob_deletions` cleanup; `auth.api.verifyPassword`.
- **Data flow:** request (person or owner) → `erasure_requests` row, `scheduled`, 72 hours → worker claims due rows with `SKIP LOCKED` → for each membership, batches under the community lock: end membership, husk, tombstone, rewrite mentions, delete attachments and exports, write redaction notices and audit → account row deleted last → blobs removed by the existing cleanup worker → DorkOS installations read the redaction feed and replace their cached copies.
- **Feature flags/config:** none. The 72-hour window is a constant, like the seven-day tenant deletion.
- **Potential blast radius:** every reader of `members.user_id` (now nullable for erased husks as well as imported historical members); the owner export; export commit; ownership transfer and owner-claim redemption (refused for a person with a scheduled erasure); the tenant deletion worker (deletes erasure rows with the tenant); DorkOS mirror and search.

## 4) Research

Industry practice for erasure in chat products (Slack, Discord, Matrix, Mastodon, Zulip) converges on a few shapes:

1. **Hard-delete messages and their rows.** Honest and simple for one message, but it breaks sequence numbers, thread roots, read positions, and cursors that every client holds. `entries_channel_seq_unique`, `entries_parent_tenant_fk`, and gap-free seq replay all assume rows are never removed. Rejected.
2. **Tombstone in place.** Keep the row's position and thread links; replace the author and the text with fixed, content-free values; drop attachments and mentions. Threads keep their shape, other people's replies still read in order, cursors stay valid. This is what Slack ("This message was deleted"), Discord, and Matrix redactions do. **Chosen.**
3. **Anonymize the author, keep the text** (Discord's default on account deletion). Fails Art. 17 for message content, which is the point of the request. Rejected.
4. **Per-client redaction events on the live stream** (Matrix `m.room.redaction`). Right idea, but a new SSE event type breaks every older DorkOS installation's strict parser. **Adapted** as a separate, additive, pull-only redaction feed that older installations simply never call.
5. **Grace period before erasure.** Mastodon and most account-deletion flows allow a short cancel window. It guards against a mistake or a takeover of a signed-in session. GDPR asks for erasure "without undue delay"; a few days is well within that. **Chosen: 72 hours**, with nothing changing until it runs, so a cancel is a full undo.

**Recommendation:** tombstone in place with a de-identified member husk; delete everything that is not needed to keep other people's history readable; rewrite mentions of the person in other people's text; one 72-hour cancellable window for every erasure; account erasure spans every membership on the host; owner may erase a former member; a content-free redaction feed lets DorkOS installations drop their cached copies.

## 5) Decisions

| #   | Decision                                          | Choice                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Rationale                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | What a thread shows in place of an erased message | The entry keeps its id, seq, channel, parent, thread root, and time. `text` becomes `This message was erased.`, author name `Erased member` (or `Erased agent`), no mentions, no attachments.                                                | Threads and cursors keep working. It fits the existing strict wire with no new field, so older installations render it. The same text for every erasure does not reveal who asked.                                                |
| 2   | Per community or the whole account                | Both, from one procedure. "Erase me from this community" erases one membership. "Delete my account" erases every membership on the host (active, left, removed) and then the account.                                                        | ADR `260920-192429` scopes accounts host-wide and authority per membership. A GDPR request to a host is about the account; a person who wants out of one community should not have to close their account.                        |
| 3   | Agents the person owns                            | Erased with them: husked, revoked, every post tombstoned, attachments deleted.                                                                                                                                                               | An agent speaks for its owner and its posts routinely carry the owner's data. Keeping them would leave the person's words under a different label.                                                                                |
| 4   | Mentions of the person in other people's messages | Delete the mention rows. Rewrite every `@handle` token for the person or their agents in the community's other entries to `@[erased]`. Free-text names are not touched.                                                                      | The handle is personal data, and the old handle is released, so a stale `@handle` could later seem to name a newcomer. `@[erased]` can never resolve as a mention. Finding a name in free text is guesswork, so we say we do not. |
| 5   | Attachments                                       | Hard delete: metadata rows removed in the erasure transaction; blobs to the existing pending-deletion cleanup.                                                                                                                               | Nothing else needs them; file names and bytes are the most personal data in a community.                                                                                                                                          |
| 6   | Exports                                           | Every live export archive in an affected community (anyone's) is deleted when the erasure runs. An export whose snapshot was taken before an erasure completed cannot be committed. Downloaded files cannot be recalled, and the UI says so. | Any export can contain the person's messages. Exports live for one hour, so the cost of re-exporting is small.                                                                                                                    |
| 7   | Audit trail                                       | Tenant `audit_events` rows stay; they hold only ids, action names, and field names. New actions `member.erase.request`, `member.erase.cancel`, `member.erase.complete`. No counts, names, or text.                                           | The owner keeps a record that an erasure happened without keeping who it was.                                                                                                                                                     |
| 8   | Timing                                            | 72-hour cancellable window for every erasure. Nothing changes until it runs. The requester (or the current owner, for an owner request) can cancel.                                                                                          | A cancel is a full undo, which a window with partial effects could not offer. 72 hours fits "without undue delay". A person who needs to be gone now can also leave now.                                                          |
| 9   | Who may erase                                     | The person for themselves; the community owner for a member who is no longer active (removed, left, or an imported historical member). Never an admin for someone else, never host authority.                                                | Matches Open Question 7 and ADR `260920-201101`. Making the owner remove a member first keeps erasure from becoming a one-click moderation weapon.                                                                                |
| 10  | The owner erasing themselves                      | Refused while they own the community, as leave is today. "Delete my account" is refused while the person owns any community that is not already `deletion_pending`.                                                                          | A community must keep exactly one owner; the owner-lifecycle trigger enforces it.                                                                                                                                                 |
| 11  | Host operator accounts                            | "Delete my account" is refused for anyone in `host_operators`. Per-community erasure still works for them.                                                                                                                                   | `host_audit_events.actor_user_id` must keep pointing at a real account; a host operator closes their account offline with the host.                                                                                               |
| 12  | Host hold, archive, suspension                    | Erasure requests are accepted and erasures run in every lifecycle state. In `held` and `archived` they are removal, not growth.                                                                                                              | A person's erasure should not wait on the host's dispute with an owner. Legal holds are not a product feature.                                                                                                                    |
| 13  | Imported historical members                       | Reachable only by the owner's erasure (they have no account on this host).                                                                                                                                                                   | They cannot sign in, and the owner is the only person on the host with authority over their history.                                                                                                                              |
| 14  | Reauthentication                                  | Password when the account has one; otherwise a session less than 5 minutes old (a fresh sign-in).                                                                                                                                            | OAuth-only accounts must be able to erase themselves at launch. This matches Better Auth's own fresh-session idea and does not wait on the OIDC reauthentication follow-up.                                                       |
| 15  | Copies on DorkOS installations                    | A pull-only redaction feed (new route, content-free list of changed entry ids with their current wire form). DorkOS installations that know the route replace their cached copy and re-index search. Older ones never call it.               | The only way to reach a member's local mirror without breaking strict parsers. It is good-faith cooperation, not a guarantee, and the copy says so.                                                                               |
| 16  | Host backups                                      | Out of reach. Each completed erasure writes one content-free log line (`community_id`, member id), and a CLI re-runs a list of erasures after a restore.                                                                                     | A host that restores a backup must be able to redo erasures that happened after it was taken. The procedure is idempotent by member id.                                                                                           |
