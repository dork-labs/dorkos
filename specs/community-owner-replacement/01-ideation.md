# Let a host replace a community owner who has left

**Slug:** community-owner-replacement
**Author:** Claude (for DOR-2252)
**Date:** 2026-09-28

---

## 1) Intent & Assumptions

- **Task brief:** Today a host cannot change who owns a community, by design. If the person who made a community leaves and will not transfer it, nobody can take it over, and an organization that paid for the community has no way back in. This needs its own contract: the owner gets notice, there is a waiting period, and every step is audited. It pairs with the host's sign-in through an outside identity (OpenID Connect, which a host can point at "Sign in with DorkOS"), which lets the host prove who should own the community. The hosted service launches without it, with a warning plus the owner's own transfer.
- **Source material:** DOR-2252; `specs/community-host-operator-api/02-specification.md` (host keys and scopes, hold, legal hold, host-started deletion with a notice of at least 7 days and a cancellable window, owner claims, host audit, host links, OIDC); `specs/community-tenancy-contract/02-specification.md` (the offline-only rule for a lost owner); `specs/community-hold-keeps-access/`; `specs/community-member-erasure/`; `specs/community-host-takedown/` (tenant audit rows written by the host, notices shown in the product); ADR `260920-192429` ("lost-owner repair is offline unless a later break-glass contract is approved").
- **Assumptions:**
  - This spec is that break-glass contract. It amends ADR `260920-192429` narrowly and keeps everything else in it: host authority still never reads content, never mints membership in an existing community on its own, and never reaches a tenant route.
  - "Left" covers three real cases that the design must treat the same way: the owner cannot be reached, the owner is reachable but does not answer, and the owner answers "no". Only the first two may end in a replacement online. An owner who objects is not lost; that dispute is settled outside the product, and the offline repair in `RECOVERY.md` stays the last resort for the host that controls the database.
  - The host is the only party that can start a replacement. The host decides who is entitled to ask (for a hosted service, the paying organization); the Community server records the request and enforces the ceremony. No organization, plan, or payment concept enters this repo.
  - Notice has to reach the owner outside the community, because an owner who has left is not reading it. The Community server sends no email today ("no email is sent in the default configuration", `specs/community-server/02-specification.md`), so this spec adds optional outbound mail, and a replacement is only possible on a host that has configured it.
  - A notice can be "delivered" and still unread (a deactivated work address that a mail server still accepts). The waiting period, the in-product notice, and the owner's DorkOS installation are the backstops; the design never claims more than "the mail server accepted it".
  - The operator pre-authorized the decisions in this programme ("go with your picks"). Every choice below is recorded as a resolved decision with its rationale.
- **Out of scope:**
  - How a hosted service decides who in an organization may ask, and any billing, plan, or account concept. That lives in the service, outside this repo.
  - Replacing an owner of a `pending_owner` community (the existing owner-claim reissue already does that) or of a community in `suspended` or `deletion_pending`.
  - Replacing admins, removing the old owner, or moving other people's roles. The new owner does that with the tools owners already have.
  - Rebinding the old owner's history to the new owner. History stays with its author.
  - Reauthentication through the OIDC issuer (spec `community-host-operator-api` Open Question 5, a separate follow-up).
  - Using the new mail capability for other notices (host deletion notices, takedown notices). It becomes possible; each needs its own decision.
  - Time or effort estimates.

## 2) Pre-reading Log

This spec answers each of the four: the contract is this spec, the ceremony is notice plus waiting plus a bound claim, credential handling is explicit (below), and the audit is per community on both planes.
- `decisions/260920-192429-…memberships.md`: "Ordinary owner claims activate only newly created `pending_owner` communities; lost-owner repair is offline unless a later break-glass contract is approved." The ADR drafted here amends only this sentence.
- `specs/community-host-operator-api/02-specification.md` (Non-Goals): "Online lost-owner recovery, including replacing an owner who cannot be reached … a break-glass contract is deferred." This is the deferred contract.
- `apps/community/src/routes/members.ts` `POST /owner/transfer`: password-confirmed; locks the community `FOR UPDATE`; refuses while `held` (`communityHeld()`); requires `active` and the current `lifecycleVersion`; locks both member rows in id order; refuses a successor who is leaving (`memberIsLeaving`); demotes the old owner to `member`, promotes the successor, bumps `lifecycle_version`, writes tenant audit `owner.transfer` with `changed_fields = {owner_member_id}`. The completion step here reuses this exact swap.
- `apps/community/src/routes/owner-claims.ts`: a one-time token hashed in `bootstrap_grants`; `preflight` checks it and sets a signed, `httpOnly`, 30-minute `community_bootstrap` cookie; `claim` needs a signed-in session plus the cookie, takes `pg_advisory_xact_lock(77281503)`, locks community before grant, refuses an account whose erasure is open (`accountErasureOpen`), and inserts the owner member with a minted handle. The replacement claim copies this shape with its own cookie and table.

- `apps/community/src/host/authority.ts`: `authority.require(c, scope)` (a key or a host operator's session, never both); `assertHostActor` re-reads the key `FOR SHARE` inside the write transaction; `recordHostAudit` writes metadata-only `host_audit_events` rows with `actor_kind IN ('person','api_key','offline')`; `hostActorRequester` stores `person:<id>` or `api_key:<id>`.
- `packages/shared/src/community-admin-wire.ts:308`: five scopes today; `communities:legal_hold` is deliberately not implied by `communities:lifecycle`. Migration `0018` sets `cardinality(scopes) BETWEEN 1 AND 5`.
- `apps/community/src/routes/host-legal-hold.ts` and ADR `260924-215422`: a legal hold blocks only permanent deletion and is never shown to owners or members.
- `apps/community/src/exports/authority.ts`: `hasExportAuthority` refuses an owner-scope export download once the requester is no longer the owner. A replaced owner therefore loses their ready owner export without new code; this spec pins it with a test.
- `apps/community/src/routes/erasures.ts:230`: an owner cannot erase their membership or account until they transfer or delete. After a replacement, the old owner is a member and may erase.
- `apps/community/src/password-confirmation.ts`: `403 PASSWORD_REQUIRED` for an OIDC-only account; OIDC reauthentication is a follow-up. So an owner's objection cannot require a password, or an OIDC-only owner could not object.

- `apps/community/src/auth.ts`, `config.ts`, `OPERATIONS.md`, `RECOVERY.md`: no mail code or dependency; "The default deployment sends no email." `specs/community-host-takedown/01-ideation.md` assumes the same and shows notices in the product only.

- `apps/server/src/services/communities/remote/`: DorkOS keeps a per-connection record and reads counts from each Community (`community-attention-cache.ts`). A small owner notice can ride the same read.

## 3) Codebase Map

- **Primary components/modules:** new `apps/community/src/routes/host-owner-replacements.ts` (host routes), `apps/community/src/routes/owner-replacement.ts` (owner notice, objection, claim), `apps/community/src/owner-replacement/` (state machine, completion, worker), `apps/community/src/mail/` (optional SMTP transport and durable notice outbox); `routes/members.ts` and `routes/administration.ts` (transfer and owner deletion end an open replacement); `routes/host-lifecycle.ts` (suspension withdraws it); `deletion-worker.ts` (deletes the rows with the tenant); `host/communities.ts` (projection); browser components `ReplacementBanner`, `OwnerReplacementClaim`, `HostOwnerReplacement`; DorkOS `apps/server/src/services/communities/remote/` and the client community row.
- **Shared dependencies:** `@dorkos/shared/community-admin-wire` (scope, host schemas, projection field), `@dorkos/shared/community-wire` (tenant notice, objection, claim schemas, error codes), `host/authority.ts`, `security.ts` (`randomToken`, `hashSecret`, `signValue`), `handles.ts` (`mintHandle`), `erasure/guards.ts` (`accountErasureOpen`, `memberIsLeaving`).
- **Data flow:** host requests (key or person) → row `notifying` and an outbox message → mail worker sends to the owner's account email with an object-only link → accepted to a verified address with no earlier objection (N days) or anything else (U days) → `waiting` with owner and admin banners, reminder before the end → `claimable` → the bound claimant signs in and redeems → one transaction swaps roles, audits both planes, emails the old owner → members see a notice for 7 days. Owner objection (by link or in the product; starts a cooling-off), owner transfer, owner deletion request, host cancel, suspension, or expiry end it at any open step.
- **Feature flags/config:** `COMMUNITY_SMTP_URL`, `COMMUNITY_MAIL_FROM` (all or none; no mail when unset), `COMMUNITY_OWNER_REPLACEMENT_NOTICE_DAYS` (default 14, 7–90), `COMMUNITY_OWNER_REPLACEMENT_UNREACHABLE_DAYS` (default 30, 14–180, never below the notice days), `COMMUNITY_OWNER_REPLACEMENT_OBJECTION_COOLDOWN_DAYS` (default 90, 30–365). No feature flag: without mail, the route answers `409 NOTICE_DELIVERY_UNAVAILABLE`.
- **Potential blast radius:** the host scope list and its database check; owner transfer and owner deletion (they end an open replacement); suspension; the deletion worker; the member-facing banners; the DorkOS connection row. The tenancy and administration isolation suites must stay green unchanged.

## 5) Research

**What comparable services do.** (General knowledge of public policies, not re-verified for this spec.) Large platforms that let someone other than the owner take over a space share one shape: a claim by someone with a stronger tie to the space, a message to the current owner, a waiting period of roughly one to a few weeks, and the owner keeping the ability to say no. Reddit's process for inactive moderators messages them and waits before granting a request; Slack and GitHub route primary-owner recovery for paid organizations through support with proof of the organization; Google Workspace proves control of the domain before resetting a super administrator. None lets an operator swap an owner silently and at once.

**Potential solutions:**

1. **Keep offline repair only (today).** The host edits the database by hand. _Pros:_ nothing new to secure. _Cons:_ no notice, no waiting period, no audit trail a member can see, and every hosted-service support case becomes a production database change. Rejected: the brief exists because this is not good enough for a paid service.
2. **Host names the new owner and it happens at once.** _Pros:_ simple. _Cons:_ breaks the tenancy contract's core line (host authority cannot hand out access to content) with no chance for the owner to respond. Rejected.
3. **Noticed, objectable, time-delayed replacement completed by a bound claim (recommended).** The host asks, the owner is told by email and in the product, a waiting period runs, the owner can object during it (from an object-only link in the email, with no sign-in) or, where their state and account allow, transfer or delete, and only then can the person the host named redeem a one-time claim, bound to their outside identity when the host uses OIDC. _Pros:_ mirrors the host-deletion precedent the operator already approved (notice, delay, cancellation, audit); the owner's rights survive; the host never reads content; the new owner proves themselves by signing in. _Cons:_ needs outbound mail; a present owner who objects blocks it (intended), and a cooling-off stops the host re-asking at once.
4. **Require a hold first, like host-started deletion.** _Pros:_ reuses one gate. _Cons:_ a hold stops every member from posting for weeks to fix an ownership problem that does not affect them. Rejected; a hold stays allowed but is not required.

**Notice channel.**

1. **SMTP, configured by the host (recommended).** Every mail service and every self-hosting operator speaks it; "accepted by the mail server" is a real, checkable event; one small, widely used dependency (`nodemailer`).
2. **A signed webhook to the host.** No dependency, but every self-hoster must build a mailer before they can use the feature, the owner's address leaves the server for an arbitrary endpoint, and a webhook that answers `200` without mailing would make "notice delivered" untrue. Rejected.
3. **In-product only.** The owner who left does not open the community. Not real notice. Rejected as the only channel; kept as a second channel beside mail.

**Who becomes owner.**

1. **An existing member only, by member id.** The host has no route that lists or looks up members, and the person an organization wants in charge may never have joined. Rejected.
2. **Anyone who holds the one-time claim (bearer).** Matches today's owner claims. The link is not a defence (the host can reissue it), so on hosts without OIDC the owner's protection is the notice, the wait, and the objection.
3. **The claim plus a named OIDC subject (required when the host has OIDC).** The claimant must be signed in with the one account linked to that subject on this host's issuer, which is stored with the subject. With "Sign in with DorkOS" this is the proof the brief asks for. Option 2 is used only on hosts without OIDC.

- **Recommendation:** option 3 with SMTP notice and a bound claim (subject binding when the host has OIDC). Details and every resolved choice are in section 6 and `02-specification.md`.

## 6) Decisions

The operator said "go with your picks"; nothing was asked. Each is a resolved decision. Rows marked "review" were revised after spec review on 2026-09-28.

| #   | Decision                          | Choice                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Rationale                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Who can start a replacement       | A new host key scope, `communities:ownership`, or a host operator's session with password reauthentication. SSO-only host operators have no password and use a key. `communities:lifecycle` does not imply it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | It hands someone full control of content. The legal-hold precedent keeps such powers on their own scope.                                                                              |
| 2   | Who the new owner can be (review) | Whoever redeems the one-time claim while signed in. On a host with single sign-on the request **must** name an identity; the issuer is stored with the subject, the claim is refused if the issuer changed, and exactly one account may match. A named person with no account can sign up through the claim. A request filed with no identity is refused at claim time if the host has since turned single sign-on on. The named identity stops a stranger using a leaked link; it does not stop a stolen key, which picks the identity. Never the current owner. | The host cannot look up members, and the right person may never have joined. The issuer subject is the only real proof of who should own it.                                          |
| 3   | Notice to the current owner       | Email through host-configured SMTP (minimal: what is happening, the deadline, how to object), plus a banner for the owner and admins, plus a notice on the owner's DorkOS connection. Reminder 48 hours before the end; email on every ending. Without mail the request is refused (`409 NOTICE_DELIVERY_UNAVAILABLE`).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | The owner who left is not reading the community. Refusing without mail keeps every replacement honest.                                                                                |
| 4   | Owner who cannot sign in (review) | The notice, reminder, and claim-reissued emails carry a one-time, **object-only** link: hashed at rest, single use, dead when the request closes, never deleted when a send fails (a timeout may still deliver), no sign-in needed, and a `GET` never objects.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | No password reset by email, `/change-password` is disabled, and an organization may close the owner's SSO account. Objecting only keeps the status quo, so the link is safe to email. |
| 5   | Waiting period (review)           | Starts when mail resolves. N (default 14, minimum 7, maximum 90) only for accepted mail to an address a sign-in service (the host's OIDC issuer, Google, or GitHub) has marked **verified**, with no earlier objection, no withdrawal in the last 30 days, and a reason other than "the owner has left the group". Otherwise U (default 30). Password-only accounts are never verified, so they always get U.                                                                                                                                                     | Matches the host-deletion notice. An unconfirmed address, a failed email, or a repeat request is weaker notice, so it gets the longer wait.                                           |
| 6   | Objection and re-asking (review)  | "Keep ownership" from the email link or in the product (session, no password), any time before completion. It ends the request for good, and the host cannot file another for that community for C days (default 90, minimum 30); every later request uses the long wait. No override.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | An owner who answers is not lost. The cooling-off stops a host wearing the owner down.                                                                                                |
| 7   | What the owner can do (review)    | Copy offers only what this owner can do: keep ownership always; transfer only when the community is `active` and the account has a password; delete only when the account has a password.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Transfer and deletion already need a password, and transfer needs `active`. Offering them to an owner who cannot use them is dishonest.                                               |
| 8   | The claim link (review)           | Not a defence: the host that issues it can reissue it, and a stolen key also picks the named identity. On every host the defences are notice, wait, and objection. Reissuing it emails the owner (with a fresh object-only link), shows in the owner's banner, and is audited.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Honest about where protection comes from; a reissue is a signal the owner should see.                                                                                                 |
| 9   | What happens to the old owner     | Stays an active `member` (the same demotion as `POST /owner/transfer`), keeps connections, agents, and history, and loses owner-only powers at once, including any owner-scope export.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Same outcome as a voluntary transfer.                                                                                                                                                 |
| 10  | Lifecycle rules                   | Start in `active`, `archived`, or `held`. Refused in `pending_owner`, `suspended`, and `deletion_pending`. Suspension or a host-started deletion withdraws an open request. Completion keeps the lifecycle.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | The owner must be able to object for the whole wait.                                                                                                                                  |
| 11  | Legal hold, erasure, imports      | A legal hold neither blocks nor is mentioned. A claimant whose erasure is open, or who is leaving, is refused. A notice to an erased or missing account fails with `RECIPIENT_UNAVAILABLE`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | A legal hold must stay invisible (ADR `260924-215422`); the erasure guards match owner claims.                                                                                        |
| 12  | Audit                             | A fixed table of host and tenant rows per transition (see the spec). The host plane never records a member id, subject, reference, or email.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Every step is audited on both planes; the host plane stays people-blind.                                                                                                              |
| 13  | Build order and scopes (review)   | Lands after import (`0019`) and takedown (`0020`). Takedown adds `communities:takedown` and the `system`/`host` audit actors; this work adds only `communities:ownership`, with scope counts relative to `main` at build time.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Avoids clashing migrations and double-added constraints.                                                                                                                              |
