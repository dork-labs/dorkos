**Words.** A **published page** (a "page" in this spec) is what publishing gives: a saved copy with a link (`meta/VOICE.md`). Its **source** is the thing it was copied from: a doc, a mini app, or a folder of web files. Its **home** is the DorkOS that keeps and serves it. The **content host** is the web address pages are served from, a domain apart from the app's. A page's **link key** is the random name that gives the page its own origin on the content host. Its **audience** is who can open it. A **view pass** is a page's own cookie, the only cookie the content host ever sets: on one page's own origin, for a viewer who proved they may open a private or password page. No cookie of the app ever reaches the content host. **Widening** gives someone new access to a page (§9.2).

## The short version

- **To publish is to save a copy.** A person or an agent picks a doc, a browser-only mini app, or a folder of web files an agent wrote. DorkOS shows the list of files, then saves a copy as a published page with a link. Nothing in a page reads the original again. Publishing again saves a new version under the same link.
- **A page is served apart from the app.** It comes from a content host on a registrable domain that is not DorkOS's own and not the sign-in's, with each page on an origin of its own, in a sandbox. No cookie of the app ever reaches the content host; the only cookie there is a page's own view pass. No page can read or set the app's storage or another page's.
- **A page holds none of its publisher's power.** In stage 1 it has no server half at all. A published mini app gets a small page runtime whose only service is storage in the viewer's own browser. A page loads and fetches only from its own origin.
- **A new page is private.** Only its owner opens it, through a short-lived signed handoff from the app. The steps are only the owner, anyone with the link and a password, and anyone with the link. Each call names the audience it wants, and every widening needs the yes.
- **An agent may publish and update. Widening needs a yes.** When an agent widens a page, or changes what a page already shared beyond its owner shows, the Owner answers a card the agent can never answer (the agent's manager may too, later, under the decision record's rule). A turn that read words from outside may ask, and the card says so. Narrowing and taking down need no yes.
- **No secret is copied.** Files the repo already protects, and files whose names say they hold a secret, are never copied, and the list shows them as left out.
- **One always-on home, by one rule.** An install with a content host serves its own published pages. Else, a Mac linked to its owner's DorkOS in the cloud, with publishing from this Mac turned on, makes the copy and sends it there, and is a client of the pages kept there. Else publishing says why it cannot, and offers what it has. A folder of an agent on a cloud agent computer is copied by that computer. A person's DorkOS in the cloud serves pages once the contract names a content host for it.
- **Everything is recorded.** Every publish, update, change of audience, pin and takedown is in the audit trail with who did it and from which turn. Taking a page down deletes its files, and its link then answers that the page is gone.
- **Nothing is findable.** Every page asks not to be indexed, and none is listed in public until reporting, scanning and takedown exist. Every page can be reported, and anyone with the link opens a page only once reporting is built.

## Goals

- A person or an agent can publish a doc, a browser-only mini app or a folder of web files, and get a link that opens on any browser.
- A founder's customer, with no DorkOS of their own, can open a shared deck, play a published game or use a published calculator.
- An agent can do the whole job: build the thing, publish it privately, and ask to share it. A person decides who sees it.
- The owner sees in one list every page, who can open it, and which version it shows, and can narrow or take down any of them in one step.
- A page can never act as its publisher, read the app's storage, or read another page's.
- A server a person runs can publish with no hosted service.
- Each step ships alone and leaves the app working.

## Non-goals (out of scope)

- The sealed box and a mini app's server half (stage 2), and named grants that let a page reach its publisher (stage 3). §14 says what stage 1 leaves room for.
- Listing a page in public, and search engines finding it. Reporting, scanning and takedown come first (ADR rule 11).
- Named people and the members of a space as audiences (§14.3).
- A page made from a doc in a team space (team spaces are item 15).
- Taking a copy ("Open in your DorkOS").
- Anything of the viewer's that uses the viewer's own account.
- Comments on a page.
- A web address of one's own for a page.
- Moving a page to another home.
- An agent manager's yes on a card (§9.4).
- Prices and plans. Nothing here names one.

### 1.2 What a mini app is on disk, and how its page half is built and served

- **On disk:** a folder with `extension.json` and a page entry (`index.ts`, or a compiled `index.js`), and an optional `server.ts` for its server half (`contributing/extension-authoring.md`). An agent's own mini app lives in its folder's `.dork/extensions/<id>/`.
- **The manifest** (`packages/extension-api/src/manifest-schema.ts:408`): `id`, a semver `version` (`:414`), `clientApiVersion: 2` (`:433`), `capabilities` (`:446`; among them `http`, the app's own server routes), `serverCapabilities` (`:450`), `dataProxy` (`:452`), `storage` (`:454`), `tools` (`:465`). A mini app's settings are declared only under its server half's capabilities (`:216`, inside `ServerCapabilitiesSchema`, `:194`). No field says a mini app runs with no host app.
- **The build:** compiled on demand by esbuild into a classic script (`format: 'iife'`) whose `react`, `react-dom` and `@dorkos/extension-api` come from the host, built with `sourcemap: 'inline'`, so the bundle carries the source of every file it was built from (`services/extensions/extension-compiler.ts:298-311`), served by `GET /api/extensions/:id/bundle` (`routes/extensions.ts:368`).
- **The frame:** the page half runs in `<iframe sandbox="allow-scripts">` pointed at `GET /api/extension-frame`, never a `srcdoc` frame, and receives its approved bundle over a private port from the host (`routes/extension-frame.ts:1-14`). The frame's policy is `sandbox allow-scripts; default-src 'none'; script-src 'nonce-…'; … connect-src 'none'; form-action 'none'; base-uri 'none'; frame-ancestors 'self' <trusted origins>` (`:73-90`), with `Cache-Control: no-store`, `nosniff` and `no-referrer` (`:110-112`).
- **What the page half may call:** the version 2 frame API (`packages/extension-api/src/frame-api.ts:26-29`, `FrameExtensionAPI`), which is the extension API (`packages/extension-api/src/extension-api.ts`) without `executeCommand`, `openCanvas` and `navigate`, plus a frame `navigate`, `request` and `media` to its own server routes, `settings`, a frame `openCanvas`, and `download` (`frame-api.ts:45-99`). From the extension API it keeps `registerPage` (`extension-api.ts:382`), `setPageBadge` (`:403`), `loadData` and `saveData` (`:472-475`), `notify` (`:480`), the decision calls and `startWork` (`:511`).
- **Approval and pins:** an approval is recorded per id, copy and files digest. A copy that runs by origin (the person approved another copy from the same source, or trusts that source) runs from a content-addressed snapshot under `{dorkHome}/extension-snapshots/<digest>/` (`services/extensions/pin/extension-snapshots.ts:1-21`; `contributing/extension-authoring.md:74`). An agent's own mini app runs from its folder. The page half is always pinned by `clientDigest`, the sha256 of the exact bundle bytes; a bundle that differs is not served (`:1810`). A mini app's `assets/` folder is read-only files for its server half (`:1359`); the page half reaches no network (`connect-src 'none'`), so a mini app's page half has no saved data of its own except what `loadData` returns.
- **Text that says otherwise:** `contributing/extension-authoring.md:1350` and `:1794` still say the page half runs in the app's own page with the person's access, and `routes/mini-app-access-page.ts:3-4` says the same. The code is the frame. §18.4 corrects the guide.

### 1.3 How an agent's web files are viewed today

An agent writes files in its folder and opens one on the canvas with the `browser` kind (`packages/shared/src/schemas.ts:7111`). The server serves it through `GET /api/workbench/serve/:token/*`, authorized by a signed token and exempt from the session gate (`routes/workbench-serve.ts:15-26`). The token lasts 30 minutes (`config/constants.ts:46`). Only the file types on one list are served as themselves; any other is a download (`routes/workbench-serve.ts:74-100`). A protected file is refused (`:318`). A server in the `hub` role, and an agent computer, serve nothing here (`:142-149`). It is for the person alone, inside their own app.

An agent that runs on a cloud agent computer has its home in the person's DorkOS in the cloud, and its folder on that computer (ADR `261125-113808`). The contract between a home and a computer (item 8, spec `space-computer-contract` §5) has no operation that copies a folder's files to the home.

### 1.5 What keeps versions

- A canvas document is one row with an integer `rev`; no history of earlier revisions is kept.
- A mini app keeps its semver version, and its approved files as content-addressed snapshots by digest (§1.2).
- The marketplace keeps a content-addressed cache by commit and installs as a file-scoped transaction (ADR-0304).
- Nothing keeps versions of something published, because nothing is published.

### 1.6 What a person's DorkOS in the cloud answers on

A server in the `hub` role (`env.ts:291`) serves only once it holds a confirmed assignment, only through the hosted front door, and only when `Host` is the assignment's one address (`services/core/hub/hub-serving.ts:9-13`; `admitHubRequest`, `:208`). The assignment carries one `address`, "the hub's one origin … Answer to this exact host and nothing else" (`packages/cloud-api/src/hubs.ts:173`, `:201`). It sleeps when idle, and wakes for the things item 13 lists (Y13s §5.4, K2), none of which is a viewer of a page.

### 1.7 What `packages/cloud-api` has for this

Nothing. It has no publishing route, no page, no content host and no second address for a home. What exists nearby: the hub assignment with its one `address` (`hubs.ts:173`), the home's next wake (`PUT /v1/hubs/self/next-wake`, `hubs.ts:642`; `routes.ts:98`), and viewer-open for a linked Mac.

### 1.8 Capabilities, references, cards and the audit trail

- **A capability** is one `CapabilityDefinition`, made with `defineCapability` (`services/core/capabilities/capability-definition.ts:440`). Two fields bear on widening: `alwaysAsksForInput` (`:164`) and `permissionDirectionForInput`, "narrowing skips Ask; widening asks even at Full" (`:170`). A call that widens counts as raising permissions and keeps its card (`callRaisesPermissions`, `services/core/capabilities/permission-enforcement.ts:351-357`). A card's sentence comes from `approvalSentence` (`capability-definition.ts:281`).
- **Who answers a card:** deciding needs positive proof of a person (`services/core/approvals/decision-authority.ts:1-20`), and an agent "can never answer ANY prompt, its own included" (`routes/session-answers.ts:91`). So today no agent answers any card.
- **A turn that read words from outside:** `turnReadOutsideWords` (`services/core/turn-power/outsider-turn.ts:31`) combines the outsider mark (`marksOf`, `services/core/turn-power/turn-levels.ts:214`) and the outside-words note (`session_outside_words`). The gate holds a fixed list of actions in a marked turn (`HELD_IN_A_MARKED_TURN`, `services/core/capabilities/marked-turn-holds.ts:31`) and sets `forOutsideWords` on such a card. The front-door raise cards are a separate path that refuses a marked turn (`services/core/approvals/agent-raise/agent-raise-cards.ts:20-21`); this spec does not use them.
- **Permission areas** are a fixed list (`packages/shared/src/permissions/permission-ids.ts:33-47`); none is for pages.
- **References:** `DORK_KINDS` has ten kinds, and "New kinds are added, never reinterpreted" (`packages/shared/src/dork-ref.ts:22-45`). There is no `page` kind.
- **The audit trail:** `audit_events` (`packages/db/src/schema/audit/audit-events.ts:23`), written with `recordAudit` (`services/audit/audit-trail.ts:66`), with an actor, a chat (`session_id`) and a `links` field. Capabilities record through the registry.
- **Hand routes** stay on one allowlist by category, among them `bytes` and `html-page` (`http/hand-routes/allowlist.ts:23-30`).
- **The front door** builds every request for the one Hono app (`http/front-door.ts`); `http/hono-api.ts` is 627 lines and may not grow.

### 1.9 Secrets

- **Protected files:** `protectedPathRefusal` asks the protected list on the real path and fails closed (`services/core/protected-paths/protected-file-guard.ts:47`); `linkedFileRefusal` refuses a hard-linked file (`:76`). The list covers DorkOS's own keys and database, the home's sign-in folders, and, at any depth in an agent's folder, `.env` files, keys and credential files (`AGENT_FOLDER_SECRET_PATHS`, `services/core/protected-paths/protected-paths.ts:215`).
- **The secret-name scan** finds files whose names say they hold a secret, by name only, never opening one (`services/core/secret-file-scan/scan-folder.ts:1-16`).

## 4. The page and its store (PS1)

### 4.1 Where it lives

A new folder `services/publishing/`, placed on the **space** side in `sides.json`: a page is space data, owned by a member, kept on its home. Its tables join the space list. On a person's DorkOS in the cloud that is a way in to a Mac's private space, the page store is the cloud home's own store, outside the private space, owned by the person: a published page is a copy its owner chose to place there (§13.4), the third named exception to "nothing of a Mac in the cloud" (§18.6). The copy respects the wall between the sides. Canvas documents and mini apps are space side (`sides.json:34`, `:39`), so the space-side store reads a canvas document and builds a mini app's bundle itself. It calls the computer side for one thing only, the files of a folder, and **the copy of a folder is always made by the computer side where the folder is**: a separate folder, `services/publishing-copy/`, on the **computer** side (§5.1), walks a folder, or checks a given list of paths in it, and hands back a file list and the bytes; it never writes a row. On this computer, that call goes through the face the space side already uses to reach it, and no step adds a crossing to the wall's lint. On a cloud agent computer it is one new operation over item 8's contract (§13.6, PS18). The page server is `services/publishing/serve/`, reached from the front door (§6.2).

**Why this is in the core.** By ADR `261124-150519`'s test, a mini app cannot do it right: publishing routes requests by host at the front door, before the app; it serves a second origin, apart from the app's; and it holds the store's keys (the view key, and the link keys). The core also acts on it, since the front door and the audit trail read the store.

### 4.3 The files, by content address

A file's bytes live once, under `{dorkHome}/pages/blobs/<first two hex characters>/<sha256>`, the way approved mini app files live in content-addressed snapshots (§1.2). A write goes to `{dorkHome}/pages/tmp/`, is hashed as it is written, and is renamed into place only when its hash is the name; an existing blob is not written again. The data folder comes from `lib/dork-home.ts`. `pages/` joins the data-folder entries of the protected list and the folders every agent launch is denied writes to, beside `extension-snapshots/`. A blob is deleted when no `page_files` row names it, in the same step that deletes the last row (§8.1, `page.take_down`).

### 4.4 Owner and publisher

- **The owner** of a page a person publishes is that person. **The owner of a page an agent publishes is the agent's owner**; the agent is recorded as the publisher, of the page and of each version it saves. In stage 1 a home has one owner (a server a person runs, or their DorkOS in the cloud), so every page on it is that person's.
- **Who may act on a page:** its owner, and the owner's agents. Each act is recorded with its actor (PG8). Once item 12's gate step (G5) lands, the capabilities take the member gate like any other, and no handler asks "is this the owner"; until then they run as every capability runs today on a one-owner home.
- **A page made from a team space's doc** is not built here: team spaces are item 15, and a space holds its members' data, so who owns such a page is decided with them (§14.3).

### 4.5 Versions and the pin

A viewer sees `pinned_version` when it is set, else `newest_version`. A page keeps its newest versions up to `publishing.limits.versionsPerPage` (default 20) and its pinned one; saving a version past that deletes the oldest unpinned one, and the answer says which. Pinning names an existing version; unpinning shows the newest again. Which of these changes need a yes is §9.2.

### 4.6 The `page` reference kind

`dork:page:<id>`, where `<id>` is the page's `id` (§4.2). It is added to `DORK_KINDS` in `packages/shared/src/dork-ref.ts` under that ADR's own rule that kinds are added, never reinterpreted, and recorded as an amendment on ADR `261122-124821` (§18.1). A link `<origin>/r/dork:page:<id>` opens the page's row in the owner's list of published pages (§12.2), never the content host: a reference never carries a location, and the page's link is its link key's, not its id's. Every capability of §8 answers a page's reference beside its id.

## 5. What a copy holds (PS2, PS7, PS9)

### 5.1 Where the copy is made, and what it may read

The space-side store makes the copy (§4.1). It reads a canvas document through the canvas's own reader (`canvas-file-access.ts` for a file-backed one) and builds a mini app's bundle through the mini apps service. It asks the computer side (`services/publishing-copy/`) for the files of a folder (web files, a doc file and the pictures it names) and to check a mini app build's input files (§11.4). The computer side answers a file list and the bytes; it writes no row. A caller can copy only what it may already read:

| Caller                 | Web files and doc files                                                                     | Canvas documents                                                                             | Mini apps                                        |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| An agent               | Inside its own folder only (its project path). The path it gives is relative to that folder | One it may read today: its own chat's canvas, or a room's canvas in a room it is a member of | Its own, in its folder's `.dork/extensions/`     |
| The owner, as a person | Inside the folder of any agent on this home, named by `agentId` and a relative path         | Any canvas document on this home's private space                                             | Any mini app installed and approved on this home |

A path is resolved to its real path, which must stay inside the root it was given; a source outside answers `not_found`, the same as one that does not exist.

**An agent on a cloud agent computer.** Its folder is on that computer, so the copy of a web files source or a doc file is made there (§13.6). Until PS18 lands, such a source answers `not_publishable` with reason `folder_on_agent_computer`: "This agent's folder is on its cloud computer, which can't send files yet." A canvas document or a mini app that the cloud home holds publishes without it.

### 5.2 The walk, and what is left out

Every file of a source goes through these checks in order. A file that fails one is left out of the copy and named in the list with its reason; the copy never holds it (PG6).

| Check                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Left out as              |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| A symbolic link (the walk never follows one, file or folder)                                                                                                                                                                                                                | "a link to another file" |
| A file with more than one hard link (`linkedFileRefusal`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | "a linked file"          |
| A protected file: `protectedPathRefusal` on its real path, failing closed                                                                                                                                                                                                   | "a protected file"       |
| A name the secret-name rules match (`AGENT_FOLDER_SECRET_PATHS`, the scan's list), at any depth                                                                                                                                                                             | "a secret file"          |
| Any part of its path starts with `.` (`.git`, `.env.example`, `.DS_Store`), or is `node_modules`                                                                                                                                                                            | "a hidden or tool file"  |
| A path under `_dorkos/`, which the content host keeps for itself (§6.3)                                                                                                                                                                                                     | "a name DorkOS keeps"    |
| A source map (`.map`): it can carry the source of the files it was built from                                                                                                                                                                                               | "a source map"           |
| A type not on the served-types list, the same list local web files use today (`routes/workbench-serve.ts:74-100`, less source maps: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, JSON, SVG, PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, ICO, BMP, WOFF, WOFF2, TTF, OTF, plain text, WebAssembly, PDF, MP4, WebM) | "not a web file"         |

A file is read once, from one descriptor, as the canvas reads one (`canvas-file-access.ts`), and hashed as it is copied. Its content type is the list's type for its extension.

### 5.3 Web files

The source is a folder. It must hold `index.html` at its top, which becomes the entry; without it, `not_publishable` with reason `no_entry_file` ("This folder has no index.html at its top."). The files are copied as they are: nothing is rewritten. A page made of web files is the answer to "a folder of plain web files: a doc or a mini app?": neither. It is its own kind, `web_files`, because a doc has no code (ADR rule 7) and a mini app has a manifest and a runtime; it is served like a mini app's page half, with no runtime.

### 5.4 A doc

The source is a markdown file (`.md`), or a canvas document whose content is `markdown` (inline, or from its `sourcePath`) or `csv`. Any other canvas kind answers `not_publishable` with reason `kind_not_publishable` and the kind's name.

The doc is rendered once, at publish, into one `index.html`, with a renderer that never passes raw HTML through, and a fixed stylesheet the server ships (`_dorkos/doc.css`). The wrapper carries the fixed line "Published with DorkOS. Not DorkOS itself." and the "Report this page" link (§6.3). A CSV becomes one table. A picture the markdown names by a relative path inside the doc's folder goes through §5.2 and is copied; a picture named by an outside address is not fetched, and shows as its link. The copy holds no script and no source markdown.

**Why rendered once:** a published doc has no code (ADR rule 7), so the page cannot render markdown in the viewer's browser. Rendering at publish means the files the publisher saw in the list are what every viewer gets, the copy is checked once, and a later change to the renderer never changes an old version.

### 5.5 A mini app

Its page half only, built as it is built today, and only if it runs with no host app. §11 says what the copy holds and what publishing checks.

### 5.6 The list, before the copy

`page.preview` answers the list: every file to be copied (path, bytes, type), every file left out (path and reason), the totals, the warnings (for web files, each outside address an HTML file loads from, since a page loads only from its own origin, §6.3), and the list's digest (§4.2). In the app the publish dialog shows it (§12.1); an agent reads it in the answer.

For an update, `page.preview` also takes a page's id in place of a source: it copies again from the page's recorded source and answers the list and its digest. `page.publish` and `page.update` take that digest as `expectDigest`, make the copy again, and refuse with `source_changed` and the new list when the digests differ. So the copy is exactly the list the publisher was shown, whoever the publisher is.

### 5.7 Limits

Defaults in config, in a new block `publishing.limits` (`packages/shared/src/config-schema/publishing.ts`, with its semver-keyed migration). They are settings only a person may raise: each key joins the operator-only keys that an agent's settings call names when it would raise one, and an agent may only lower them.

| Limit                                                                | Default | Over it                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pageBytes`: the bytes of one version                                | 25 MB   | `publish_limit`, `limit: 'page_bytes'`                          |
| `pageFiles`: the files of one version                                | 1,000   | `publish_limit`, `limit: 'page_files'`                          |
| `pages`: the pages on this home                                      | 200     | `publish_limit`, `limit: 'pages'`                               |
| `totalBytes`: the bytes the store holds for this home's pages        | 2 GB    | `publish_limit`, `limit: 'total_bytes'`                         |
| `versionsPerPage`: versions kept for each page, besides a pinned one | 20      | The oldest unpinned version is deleted, and the answer names it |

A page has no expiry in stage 1: it stays up until its owner takes it down, and the owner's list shows every page, its audience and its version (§12.2). The limits name no price and no plan.

## 6. Serving (PS3)

### 6.1 The content host, and its one setting

On a server a person runs, one setting names it: `DORKOS_PAGES_HOST`, a bare host name (no scheme, port or path), read in `env.ts` with the other front-door settings. Setting it is the operator's word that this server is always on, and the setting's help says so (§13). "A registrable domain" has one meaning here: the registrable domain by the Public Suffix List's **public section** only. An entry in the list's private section never makes two hosts count as different for this rule.

At boot the setting is checked. It is used only when all of these hold; otherwise the server boots, does not serve pages, logs the reason, and the app names it (§8.4, reason `content_host_rejected`):

1. **Not DorkOS's own.** Its registrable domain (public section) is not one of the product's own domains: a fixed list in code (`DORKOS_OWN_DOMAINS`, in `packages/shared/src/publishing-schemas.ts`).
2. **Apart from the app.** Its registrable domain (public section) is not the app's: not that of `DORKOS_PUBLIC_URL`, nor of the address the server is served on, nor, on a cloud home, of the sign-in issuer.
3. **Not nested.** It is neither under nor above the app's host.
4. **A site for each page.** It has its own explicit rule in the Public Suffix List (`tldts` with private domains allowed, already a dependency), found as a listed rule and never by the list's default rule for an unknown name. Then each `<link key>.<content host>` is a site of its own. The setting's help says that the list browsers ship must hold the rule.

This is the one way to be a content host in stage 1, on a server a person runs and on the hosted side alike. A content host with no entry in the list is a named later step, with its own design and Security's read (§14.3).

Under `DORKOS_TEST_RUNTIME` only, a name under the reserved `.test` suffix is accepted, so tests can map page origins to the test server. On a person's DorkOS in the cloud the content host comes from the hosted contract (§13.2), with the same four checks. A unit test of the boot check proves the cookie separation rule: it refuses a content host that is not an explicit rule, one of the product's own domains, and one whose public-section registrable domain is the app's (PP18).

Unset, this server does not serve pages, and §13's rule decides what publishing does. The operator arranges a wildcard name and a wildcard certificate for `*.<content host>` in front of the server, as for the space server's separate address today. The content host serves only `https`.

### 6.2 Routing by host

`http/front-door.ts` reads `Host` before the one Hono app. A `Host` of the form `<label>.<content host>`, where `<label>` is a link key's shape (26 lowercase base32 characters), goes to the page server, a Hono app of its own in `services/publishing/serve/`. Any other name under the content host, and the content host itself, answer `404` with the content host's own page. The app's routes are never mounted for the content host, and the page server is never reached under the app's own `Host`. `http/hono-api.ts` does not grow.

The page server answers `GET` and `HEAD` for files, `POST /_dorkos/password` (§9.5), `GET` and `POST /_dorkos/report` (§6.5) and `GET /_dorkos/enter` (§7); every other method answers `405`, for every name under the content host alike. A WebSocket upgrade for a content host name is refused. A request that asks for a service worker script (it carries `Service-Worker: script`) answers the content host's `404`, so no page installs a service worker. The page server runs no page code, calls no service and reads only the store (PG5).

Paths: `/` is the version's entry; `/<path>` is that file; a path that ends in `/` is that folder's `index.html`; anything else answers the content host's `404` page.

### 6.3 The sandbox, and every header

**Why every page keeps its origin.** A doc runs no script, and keeps `allow-same-origin` so that its stylesheet and pictures load from its own origin with its view pass, also when the page is private. A code page's scripts run with `allow-same-origin` so that a mini app's storage can live in the viewer's browser on that page's own origin (§11.2). That origin is the page's alone, apart from the app (§6.1), so it holds nothing of the app's and nothing of another page's.

**Links that open.** A link in a page opens: in the same tab, by the page's own navigation, and in a new window through `allow-popups` with `allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox`, so the new window is an ordinary page and not sandboxed in turn. `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` gives a window opened on another origin no handle back to the page. These are the sandboxing flags and opener rules as the HTML standard documents them; PS5's browser proof clicks both kinds of link (PP34).

**A page loads and fetches only from its own origin.** That is what `default-src 'self'` and `connect-src 'self'` require of a page's scripts, styles, pictures and requests. The content rules do not govern a person following a link away from the page. The list before the copy warns about each outside address an HTML file loads from (§5.6).

**Whose it is.** Every answer says whose it is. The HTML the page server itself writes (the wrapper around a doc, the shell around a mini app, the password form, and the page for a taken-down link) carries the fixed line "Published with DorkOS. Not DorkOS itself.", with a "Report this page" link to `/_dorkos/report`, written by the server outside the page's own content. What is guaranteed differs by kind. On a doc (rendered with no raw HTML and no script) and on the page server's own pages (the password form, the taken-down page, the report form), no script of the copy runs, so the line is always shown. In a mini app's shell, and in a folder's own HTML, the copy's script runs in the same document, so there the guarantee is the header and the content host's own domain, and the line in the shell is a courtesy. Guaranteeing the line for those two kinds, by a shell that carries the line and frames the app on the same page origin, is a named later step (§14.3). Every file the page server serves, a folder's own HTML included, carries the header `X-DorkOS-Served-By` with the same words.

**Where a link leads.** Content rules govern what a page loads, fetches and posts. They do not govern where a person goes when they follow a link out of a page, so a published page could look like a sign-in screen and lead a person to an outside address. Stage 1 therefore requires three things: the content host is never on or near the product's own domains (§6.1, checks 1 to 3); every answer says whose it is (above); and a "Report this page" route on every page's origin (§6.5), which is built before anyone with the link can open a page (§9.1, PS19).

**Cookies.** The content host sets one cookie only, the view pass (§7), on one page's own origin. It reads the `Cookie` header only for that cookie, and never passes it on. No app cookie is ever set, read or reachable there (PG4).

**Not indexed.** Besides the header, every name under the content host answers `/robots.txt` with the same `User-agent: *` and `Disallow: /`, whether or not a page is there and whatever the page holds, and the shells DorkOS writes (a doc's and a mini app's `index.html`) carry `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` (PG10).

| A page whose audience is `owner`, with no valid view pass    | `404`, "There's no page at this address."                                                                                                                             |
| An unknown link key, or a name that is not one               | `404`, the same body                                                                                                                                                  |
| A link key in `page_link_tombstones`                         | `410`, "This page was taken down.", with `Clear-Site-Data: "cache", "storage"` so the browser clears that origin's cache and storage; the page carries the fixed line |

So a link to a page its holder may not open answers exactly as a link to nothing (PG12): the same status and bytes for every path, for `robots.txt`, for the reserved paths under `/_dorkos/` (a handoff that does not open a page, and a password posted to a name that has no password page, answer the same `404`), and the same `405` for every other method. **Two stated exceptions:**

- **A password page's form** shows the page's title, because the person holding the link and the password needs to know which page the password is for, and the page's audience is people with its link. A wrong password answers `200` with the same form and the line "That password didn't work."
- **A taken-down link** answers `410`, "This page was taken down.", as the ADR asks, and says nothing else. A link on a DorkOS address stops working when its owner takes the page down.

### 6.5 Reporting a page (PS19)

Every name under the content host answers `GET /_dorkos/report` with a short form the page server writes (a reason from a fixed list, and at most 500 characters of text), carrying the fixed line, and `POST /_dorkos/report` with "Thanks. Your report was sent." The answer is the same for every name, whether or not a page is there and whoever may open it (PG12).

- **For a page that exists**, the report reaches its owner as a notice: "Someone reported “{title}”. Check it, or take it down." It names the reason and carries the text as words from outside, never as an instruction. On a person's DorkOS in the cloud it also reaches the hosted side's takedown, through the contract's report call (§13.2, item 6).
- **Capped for each address:** at most 5 reports from one address in an hour, across every name of the content host; past it, `429` with `Retry-After` and "Too many tries. Wait a few minutes and try again."
- **Recorded:** each report that reaches a page is an audit row: actor `external`, action `page.report`, the page's reference, the reason. Never the reporter's address beyond what the cap keeps for an hour.
- **Before the link audience.** The route exists before any audience past "only the owner" and "a password" can open: the step that opens `link` needs it (§9.1, §20).

## 7. How the owner opens a private page (PS4)

The owner's app session never reaches the content host. The app hands over a short-lived signed handoff, once, and the content host turns it into a view pass for that one page's origin.

1. The owner presses **Open** in the app. The client calls `page.view_link` (person only, §8.1). The server checks that the caller is the page's owner, in person, and mints a handoff: `v1.<epoch>.<expires at>.<nonce>.<mac>`, an HMAC-SHA256 over those fields and the page's link key, under the view key `{dorkHome}/pages/view-key` (32 random bytes, made at first use, on the protected list), live for 60 seconds, its nonce single use.
2. The answer is `{ url, expiresAt }`, where `url` is `https://<link key>.<content host>/_dorkos/enter?h=<handoff>&to=<path>`. The client opens it in a new tab.
3. The page server checks the mac against this origin's link key, the expiry, that its epoch is current and that its nonce is unspent; then it spends the nonce and answers `303` to `to`, setting `__Host-dorkos-view=<value>; Path=/; Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax; Max-Age=1800`. `to` must be a path that starts with one `/`; a path that starts with `//` or with `/\`, or anything else, becomes `/`. The value is `v1.<epoch>.<subject>.<expires at>.<mac>`, the mac taken over those fields and the origin's link key, where `<subject>` is `owner`, or `password` (§9.5). The value names no page.
4. Every later request on that origin checks the pass: its mac against this origin's link key, that its epoch is the page's current epoch, and that it has not expired. A failed check is the `404` of §6.4.

A narrowing or a new password raises the page's epoch, so every view pass for it ends at once (§9.6). A restart loses unspent handoffs, which last 60 seconds; the owner presses Open again.

**Why a cookie, and why only this one.** A page's own files name each other by paths such as `/assets/app.js`, which a grant carried in the path would break. The view pass is `HttpOnly`, so no page script reads it; host-only with the `__Host-` prefix, so no other origin receives it; and it opens one page, for 30 minutes. It is not an app cookie: it grants nothing in the app.

**Who may have a handoff.** The page's owner, in person, at the app. Not an agent (an agent reads its pages through `page.get`) and not a program. A page whose audience is `link` needs no handoff; the owner opens its link like anyone.

### 8.5 The answers' read class

`page.get` and `page.list` return titles and file lists from the person's sources; their read class is `self`, as for the running-chats read (`services/core/capabilities/dorkos-tool-reads.ts:114`): the calling turn is marked as one that read outside words when an answer holds a version that a turn which read outside words saved (`read_outside_words`, §4.2). `page.preview` answers file names from a folder the caller may already read; its read class is `local`.

### 8.6 The audit record

Each call is recorded by the registry, as every capability is: the actor (person, agent or program), the chat, the action, `target_type: 'page'`, the page's id and title, the outcome, and `links` with `dork:page:<id>` and, when the call came from a turn, `dork:turn:<id>`. Visibility is `space`.

The handoff and the view pass are never logged, and the answers that carry them have `Cache-Control: no-store`.

## 9. The audiences and the yes (PS6, PS11)

### 9.1 The steps

| Audience   | Who can open it                              | Words in the app                        |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| `owner`    | The owner, through the handoff (§7)          | "Only you"                              |
| `password` | Anyone with the link and the page's password | "Anyone with the link and the password" |
| `link`     | Anyone with the link                         | "Anyone with the link"                  |

They are ranked in that order. A new page is `owner` (PG2). Each call names the audience it wants, which may be any of the three; a move up is widening and needs the yes of §9.3 when an agent asks, and a move down is narrowing. ADR `261125-183122`'s rule 4 lists named people, a space's members, anyone with the link and listing in public; the password step sits between the owner and anyone with the link, and §18.2 adds a dated note to that record for it. **"Anyone with the link" opens only once the report route is built (§6.5):** the sharing step that offers `link` needs the report step (PS19 before PS6), and PS6's test asserts the route answers on a page's origin before `page.set_audience` accepts `link` (PP48).

### 9.2 What widening is

Widening gives someone new access. The gate decides it from the input alone: `permissionDirectionForInput` gets only the input (`capability-definition.ts:170`) and cannot read the live row. So each call that can widen carries a before-value, `expect`, the page's state as the caller read it (from `page.get` or the app's list): `{ audience, newestVersion, pinnedVersion, shownDigest }`. The direction is worked out from `expect` and the call's own fields. The handler then checks `expect` against the live row inside its transaction, before any write, and answers `page_changed` with the live state when it differs, changing nothing (PG13), as the field's own rule asks of a classification based on before-values. **A narrowing compares only `expect.audience`. A widening compares the audience, the version (`newestVersion`, `pinnedVersion`) and the digest (`shownDigest`).** An unknown page is refused as `not_found` before the gate, so no card is raised for it.

| Call                | Widens when                                                                                                                          | Otherwise                                                                                 |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `page.set_audience` | The new audience ranks higher than `expect.audience`; or it is `password` on a `password` page (a new password hands out new access) | Narrows when it ranks lower; no direction when it is the same                             |
| `page.update`       | `expect.audience` is not `owner` and `expect.pinnedVersion` is null, so the audience would see the new version                       | No direction: the version is saved and shown to the owner alone, or hidden behind the pin |
| `page.pin`          | `expect.audience` is not `owner` and the call changes which version is shown                                                         | No direction                                                                              |
| `page.take_down`    | Never                                                                                                                                | Narrows                                                                                   |
| `page.publish`      | Never: a new page is private                                                                                                         | No direction                                                                              |

**On a Mac that publishes to its cloud home** (§13.4), one more rule holds whatever the direction: a marked turn's `page.publish` or `page.update` always asks, and its card carries the outside-words line, because the call moves the Mac's content off the Mac.

**"No direction", stated in full.** The field's type becomes `(input) => 'narrow' | 'widen' | undefined`. Its TSDoc states the requirement: `undefined` declares no direction, which means "decide by tier and area as usual"; it never skips the gate, and it does not by itself force a card. ### 9.3 The card

The registry's approval hold raises it, as for every call that asks.

- **The sentence** (`approvalSentence`): “Juniper wants anyone with the link to open “Q3 deck”.” For an update: “Juniper wants to show a new version of “Q3 deck” to anyone with the link.” For a pin: “Juniper wants to show version 3 of “Q3 deck” to people with its password.” For a new password: “Juniper wants a new password for “Q3 deck”.” For a Mac's push from a marked turn: “Ostrich wants to send “Q3 deck” to your DorkOS in the cloud.” Each is in §12.6.
- **The view:** the page's title, its audience before and after, the number and digest of the version the audience would see, the files of that version and the files left out of it, and for a new version the files added, changed and removed since the shown one.
- **A turn that read words from outside** may ask. Its card carries the line "This chat read words from outside your team." A new rule in the gate sets it: when the turn's own mark says it read outside words (`turnReadOutsideWords`, read as the gate reads it at `permission-enforcement.ts:411-414`) and the call's direction is `'widen'`, the card's `forOutsideWords` is set. The rule reads the mark itself, not the gate's "stranger turn" value, which is false in a chat set to Full (`:420`), so a card raised in a Full chat still carries the line. The card is raised at every level and never refused for the mark. A run nobody is attending (a scheduled run, an automatic carry-over) raises no card: it is refused at once with the reason, as for every call that would ask (`tier-enforcement.ts:866-871`). `HELD_IN_A_MARKED_TURN` is left as it is: it makes every call of an id ask in a marked turn, and a marked turn must still be able to publish and update a private page on its home with no card. On a Mac that publishes to its cloud home, a marked turn's publish or update always asks (§9.2).
- **The yes is bound to what the card showed.** The card and the approval carry the version's number and its digest, from the call's `expect` and `expectDigest`, and the approval binds the whole input. If either moved while the card waited (another update saved a version, the pin changed, or the files changed), the call refuses with `page_changed` or `source_changed` and nothing changes; the agent reads the page again and asks again.

### 9.4 Who may answer, in stage 1

The Owner, or the agent's manager under the decision record's rule; in stage 1, a person, the owner. The agent that asked can never answer its own card, and today no agent answers any card (`routes/session-answers.ts:91`; `decision-authority.ts`). ADR rule 5 also lets an agent's manager answer, under conditions (a turn that read no words from outside; never the asker, never an agent the asker manages). That is a later step (§14.3): it needs a way for an agent to decide a card, which no record has yet given, and it is listed for Security (§17).

**A person on a phone.** As the code stands at HEAD, a person signed in over Wi-Fi (a phone on the same network as the computer) cannot answer a card for a call that raises permissions, and a widening is such a call (`callRaisesPermissions`, `services/core/capabilities/permission-enforcement.ts:340-357`). This spec does not change that.

### 9.5 The password

- **Made by the server**, never chosen by the caller: 16 characters from a 32-character alphabet with no look-alike characters, shown as four groups of four (80 random bits).
- **Shown once**, in the `password` field of the answer to the call that made it. It is never stored in plain, never in the audit trail, a log or any other answer. An agent that asked receives it in its answer, after the person's yes, and passes it on. The owner who loses it makes a new one.
- **Stored only as a hash:** scrypt with a 16-byte random salt, N = 2^15, r = 8, p = 1, a 32-byte key, in `password_hash`.
- **Checked in constant time:** the form posts to `/_dorkos/password`; the server derives the key from what was typed and compares the two 32-byte keys with `timingSafeEqual`. On a match it sets the view pass with subject `password` and answers `303` to `/`.
- **Limited, by address:** 10 tries from one address in 10 minutes, across every page of the content host; past that, `429` with "Too many tries. Wait a few minutes and try again." Over the whole content host, a slow-down: past 100 tries a minute, each answer waits one second longer, up to ten. The cap is never for each page, so no one's tries can lock a page's own viewers out.
- **A new password** (moving to `password` again) raises the epoch, so everyone who opened the page with the old one is out.

### 9.6 Narrowing

A narrowing raises `audience_epoch`, so every view pass for the page ends at once, and a link page narrowed to `owner` answers `404` to its link at the next request. Narrowing never needs a yes, from anyone.

No path segment is a command word: `view-links`, `versions`, `audience` and `pin` are nouns. `GET /api/pages` pages with `cursor`, `limit` and `nextCursor`. The content host's own routes (`/_dorkos/enter`, `/_dorkos/password`, the files) are not API operations and are not in the API document.

### 10.2 Shapes

In `packages/shared/src/publishing-schemas.ts` (new), so the client and the CLI read them: the source union (§8.2), each input and output of §8.1, the audience enum, the `expect` before-value (§9.2), the problem extensions (`reason`, `offer`, `uses`, `limit`, and the live state of `page_changed`). `password` in `page.set_audience`'s answer is marked write-once and sensitive in the API document, and is kept out of the audit trail and the logs.

### 10.3 Errors

The codes of §8.3. The five new ones, `publishing_unavailable` (409, only when the install is not set up for published pages), `source_changed` and `page_changed` (both 412, the repo's "changed since you looked", each with its live-state extension), `not_publishable` (422) and `publish_limit` (422, only a fixed limit exceeded), go in a group file of their own under `packages/shared/src/problem-codes/` (built: pull request #4458 split the list); each maps to one status. Anything counted over time is `rate_limited` (429) with `Retry-After`, and a passing outage is `temporarily_unavailable` (503), both existing codes.

**Secrets.** The password is write-once (§8.6). The handoff and the view pass are never logged, and the answers that carry them have `Cache-Control: no-store`.

**Read classes.** `page.get` and `page.list` are `self` (§8.5); `page.preview` is `local`.

### 10.4 Stability

Every operation is `preview` until the app's publish flow and list ship (PS10), because PS7 to PS9 may still add a `reason`. They become `stable` in PS10's pull request; after that, a change is a breaking change under ADR `261123-170832`.

## 11. A browser-only mini app as a page (PS8, PS9)

### 11.1 The manifest line

`"standalone": true` in `extension.json`, a new optional field of the manifest (`packages/extension-api/src/manifest-schema.ts`). It says the mini app runs with no host app. The manifest's own rules refuse it, at every load, beside any of: a server half (`server.ts` or a server entry), `serverCapabilities`, `capabilities.http`, `capabilities.events`, `capabilities.rooms`, `capabilities.forward`, `dataProxy`, `storage`, `tools`, `skills` or `exports`, with "A mini app that runs alone has no server half or DorkOS routes." Inside DorkOS nothing changes: the mini app runs in the app's frame as before.

**What checks it:** the manifest's rules (above); publishing's checks (§11.4); and the page runtime, which has nothing else to offer (§11.2).

### 11.2 The page runtime

A script DorkOS ships, built from the frame runtime (`apps/client/src/layers/features/extensions/frame/runtime.tsx`) as an entry of its own, with the frame's bridge replaced by a local one. It gives the bundle `react`, `react-dom` and `@dorkos/extension-api` as the frame does, and calls `activate` with this surface:

| Call                                                                                                                                                              | In a published page                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `registerPage`                                                                                                                                                    | The first registered page fills the window                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `loadData()`, `saveData(data)`                                                                                                                                    | JSON under one key in `localStorage` on the page's own origin, at most 1 MB; each viewer's own, kept in the viewer's browser and sent nowhere. This is the only saved data a mini app that runs alone has: the publisher's own data in DorkOS is never copied, so a viewer starts with none |
| `notify`                                                                                                                                                          | A short line inside the page                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `download`                                                                                                                                                        | A plain browser download, with the frame's types and size limit                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `settings.get()`                                                                                                                                                  | An empty object: a mini app declares settings only under its server half's capabilities (§1.2), which one that runs alone does not have                                                                                                                                                     |
| `navigate`                                                                                                                                                        | `{ ok: false, reason: 'unknown-route' }`, as its contract allows                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `getState`, `subscribe`                                                                                                                                           | The theme only                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `isSlotAvailable`                                                                                                                                                 | True for the page only. Other `register…` calls are accepted and do nothing                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Anything else (`startWork`, `openCanvas`, `answerDecision`, `listDecisions`, `executeCommand`, `projectSettings`, `setPageBadge`, `request`, `media`, `mediaUrl`) | Rejects with an error naming the call; the page shows "This page can't do that outside DorkOS."                                                                                                                                                                                             |

`request`, `media` and `mediaUrl` need `capabilities.http`, which the manifest's rules refuse beside `standalone` (§11.1); so both this list and §11.4's check exclude them, and the check also reads `mediaUrl` by name.

**How the server gets it.** The runtime is built with the app, as an entry of its own in the client's build, into the client's built files. The server reads that one file from the built client it already serves (`http/client-dist.ts`), and takes its sha256 at boot as the runtime's digest. The runtime is copied into each version as `_dorkos/runtime.<digest>.js`, and the version records its digest (§4.2), so an old version keeps running with the runtime it was published with.

### 11.3 What the copy holds

| File                          | From                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `index.html`                  | A shell DorkOS writes: the title, `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">`, the fixed line "Published with DorkOS. Not DorkOS itself." with the "Report this page" link outside the app's own root, the runtime, the bundle |
| `_dorkos/runtime.<digest>.js` | §11.2                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `app.js`                      | The bundle built for publishing (§11.4), with no source map                                                                                                                                                                 |

Never: `extension.json` whole, a server half, the `assets/` folder (its files are for the server half, §1.2), settings values, secrets, the source of the files the bundle was built from, or the mini app's data folder.

### 11.4 What publishing checks, in order

1. The caller may read it (§5.1).
2. Its manifest says `"standalone": true`. Else `not_publishable`, `not_standalone`: "Mark this mini app as one that runs alone first."
3. Its manifest declares nothing of §11.1's list (an older copy may predate the rule). Else `not_publishable`, `uses_host`, with `uses`.
4. **What the published bundle is built from, and what ties it to its source.**
   - **A mini app that runs from a folder** (an agent's own mini app runs from its folder; §1.2): publishing builds from that folder as it stands at preview, in a fresh build with no source map. The preview's digest binds the build's inputs exactly as it binds a folder of web files: it is taken over the list of input files (path, sha256, bytes) the build read, and `page.publish` and `page.update` refuse with `source_changed` when the inputs moved. The inputs' list is kept with the version. Its approval must hold as it holds for running in the app; else `not_publishable`, `not_approved`: "This mini app's files changed since they were approved."
   - **A mini app that runs by origin:** publishing builds from the approved snapshot (`extension-snapshots/<digest>/`), never from the project folder, in a fresh build with no source map; the version records the approved digest (`approved_digest`, §4.2).
   - In both cases the version records the published bundle's sha256. The app's own `clientDigest` pins the bundle the app serves, which carries an inline source map; the published build has none, so it is tied to its source by its inputs or its snapshot, not by `clientDigest`.
5. **Its build holds no protected file.** The build reads the list of its input files (esbuild's metafile). The space side asks the computer side's one call (§5.1) to check every input against the protected list and the secret-name rules on its real path. Any match refuses with `not_publishable`, `protected_input`, naming the file: "This mini app's code includes {path}, a protected file."
6. Its bundle calls nothing a published page cannot do: the published bundle is read for the member names `startWork`, `openCanvas`, `answerDecision`, `listDecisions`, `executeCommand`, `projectSettings`, `setPageBadge` and `mediaUrl`. Each one found joins `uses`, and the answer is `not_publishable`, `uses_host`.

## 13. Where it runs

**The one rule, by what is set.** If a content host is set and passes §6.1 (`DORKOS_PAGES_HOST`, or a cloud home's `contentHost`), this install serves its own published pages: its operator vouches that it is always on, and the setting's help says so. A setting that fails a check answers `content_host_rejected`. Else, if it is linked to its owner's DorkOS in the cloud: with the Mac's switch "Publish from this Mac" on, it makes the copy and sends it there, and is a client of the pages kept there (§13.4); with the switch off, `publish_switch_off`, which offers the switch. Else publishing answers `no_home` (§8.4). When a content host and a cloud home both exist, the content host wins. **Where a folder is copied:** always by the computer side where the folder is; for an agent on a cloud agent computer, that computer (§13.6). PP11, PP20 and PP29 hold this rule from each side.

### 13.1 A server a person runs

The same program, in any role a person runs it in. It serves pages when `DORKOS_PAGES_HOST` names a content host that passes §6.1, and its operator points `*.<content host>` at it with a certificate. It needs no hosted service and nothing of item 13 (PG11).

### 13.2 A person's DorkOS in the cloud, and the contract change (PS13, PS14)

Today it answers only on its one assigned address, and the contract has nothing for pages (§1.6, §1.7). So the first step is a change to `packages/cloud-api`, from an issue labelled `cloud-contract`, agreed with the hosted side before it merges, contract first. `hubs.ts` is 668 lines, so the change first moves the assignment's schemas into a file of their own.

1. **A content host for each cloud home.** The assigned state of `HubAssignmentSchema` gains an optional `contentHost`: a host name with its own explicit rule in the Public Suffix List, whose registrable domain by the list's public section is not one of the product's own domains (the fixed list of §6.1), nor the home's address's, nor the sign-in issuer's (§6.1's four checks). The front door serves `https://*.<contentHost>` and passes each request to this home only, with the same front-door proof and `Host` unchanged. The home checks it as §6.1 says and admits a request whose `Host` is under it to the page server only (§6.2).
2. **The front door adds nothing to a page response**: no cookie, no caching of a response the home marked `private`, and no header that loosens §6.3.
3. **Opening a page wakes the home, within a cap.** A request for the content host wakes a sleeping home the way a request for its address does: a new case in item 13's list of what wakes a cloud home (Y13s K2; §18.6). The cap: at most one wake a minute and 30 an hour for each home from its content host, counted by the hosted side's front door for that home; past it, the request answers `503` with `Retry-After` and the line "This page is starting. Try again in a minute."
4. **One question, answered in the contract's prose:** whether the hosted side may serve a page's files while the home sleeps, without waking it. Until it is answered, the home wakes (§13.5).
5. **Limits:** the contract may name limits for a home's pages; the home applies the lower of its own config and the contract's. No limit is a plan.
6. **Reporting and takedown.** A report a cloud home receives (§6.5) is sent to the hosted side by a new call with the home's key, carrying the page's link key, the reason and the text, at most 5 for each address an hour as the home counts them. The hosted side may take a page down by a new command to the home, which takes it down as its owner's take-down does (§8.1), records the hosted side as the actor, and tells the owner.

The test version of the hosted service (`services/runtimes/test-mode/fake-cloud-v1.ts`) serves the new field. With an older hosted service, the field is absent and the home answers `no_content_host` (§8.4).

Item 13 makes the cloud home exist and wake (its Y0, Y1 and Y2). A self-hosted server needs none of it.

### 13.3 A Mac alone

A Mac is the home of its person's private space, and is only as available as the Mac (ADR `261124-194115`). The ADR rejects serving pages from a laptop through a tunnel. So a Mac with no content host and no DorkOS in the cloud cannot publish: `publishing_unavailable` with reason `no_home`, at the moment the person or an agent tries, and the offer that exists today, the canvas (§8.4). The desktop app offers no content host setting.

### 13.4 A Mac with a DorkOS in the cloud (PS15, PS17)

This is the main case: a founder on a Mac. **The published pages live in the person's DorkOS in the cloud, and the Mac is a client of it.**

**Why a push is the owner's choice.** A published page is "a copy its owner chose to place in the cloud": ADR `261125-183122` amends ADR `261124-194115` to say the cloud may hold the pages a person published, outside the private space, which keeps its one home on the Mac. The owner makes that choice twice: once by turning on the switch at the Mac, and once for each page, by publishing it. On the cloud home the page store is its own store, outside the private space, owned by the person (§4.1).

**The switch.** "Publish from this Mac", with the line "Sends what you publish to your DorkOS in the cloud. Turning off keeps published pages." It sits with item 13's switches at the Mac and follows their rules (its Y16): off by default, also for a link made before it; turned on only at that computer, by its owner's session there; turned off from anywhere the owner is signed in; written only through the Mac's pin store; each change recorded at the Mac. It is a feature the Mac names in its hello and in its consent line, as item 13's W3 asks of every addition to the link.

**Each capability on such a Mac:**

| Capability                                                                                                                   | Where it runs                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `page.preview`                                                                                                               | On the Mac: it lists files, and nothing leaves. Its `{ pageId }` form, for an update, reads the page's recorded source path from the cloud home, copies again from it and gives the digest. A preview does not ask the cloud home whether it has a content host: the Mac learns that only at publish, which answers `no_content_host` |
| `page.publish`, `page.update`                                                                                                | The Mac copies, with every check of §5 and §11, and sends the copy to the cloud home (`page.push`, item 13's new row 11)                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `page.list`, `page.get`, `page.view_link`, `page.set_audience`, `page.pin`, `page.take_down`, the download, and the password | Carried out by the cloud home. The Mac forwards the call over its link (`page.call`, item 13's new row 12) and shows the answer. The cloud home mints the handoff. The app's Published pages screen on the Mac shows the cloud home's list                                                                                            |

- **The gate and its cards run on the Mac**, where the turn runs: the area, the direction from `expect`, the card and its outside-words line. A forwarded call arrives at the cloud home as its owner's, because the link is the owner's own Mac; the cloud home still checks `expect` against its live row (PG13) and refuses a stale one with `page_changed`.
- **A turn that read words from outside** may push, and its `page.publish` or `page.update` always asks on the Mac, whatever the direction and the agent's level (§9.2, the gate's fourth rule in §18.8), with the card's line "This chat read words from outside your team." Pushing moves the Mac's content off the Mac.
- **What crosses in a push:** the file list, its digest and the bytes; the source's kind and its path relative to the agent's folder; the publisher as a reference (`dork:agent:<id>` or the person); whether the turn that published read words from outside; and, for an update, the `expect` it was classified against and whether the Mac's gate allowed it to show. Nothing else of the Mac: no absolute path, no setting, no credential.
- **On the cloud home:** the copy is checked again against §5.2's type list and §5.7's limits, and stored as the person's page, with the source `{ kind: 'pushed', from: 'dork:host:<id>', sourceKind, agentId, path }` (§4.2) and each version's `read_outside_words`. An update for a page shared beyond its owner that does not carry the Mac's allowed widening is saved hidden: the shown version is pinned, if none is, so the audience sees no change until the owner shows it ("A new version waits for you to show it.").
- **The audit trail.** The act is recorded on the Mac, where the turn ran: the actor, the chat, the turn's reference, the action, the page's reference and the outcome. The stored change is recorded on the cloud home: the actor as the owner by the Mac (`dork:host:<id>` in `links`), the publisher's reference, the page's reference, the change (version saved, audience before and after, pin, taken down) and the outcome.
- **The cloud home asleep:** the call wakes it by item 13's wake for a computer's key (its K2 case, widened to a page call, §18.6); if it does not answer after that, `temporarily_unavailable` with `Retry-After`. A cloud home with no content host answers `publishing_unavailable`, `no_content_host`.
- **The Mac offline:** the owner still lists, narrows and takes down from the web, on the cloud home itself.
- **The switch turned off, or the Mac unlinked:** nothing more is sent, and every forwarded call on the Mac answers `publish_switch_off`; the owner acts on the pages from the web. Pages already published stay until their owner takes them down, and the web still lists them.
- **The owner's view link from a Mac:** the Mac checks that the caller is a person, as `page.view_link` requires, and marks the forwarded call as a person's; the cloud home mints a handoff only for a forwarded call the Mac marked so.
- **Item 13's first invariant** holds nothing of a Mac in the cloud but two named exceptions, and its census test fails on any other store. A published page its owner chose to publish is a third exception, added in full in §18.6.

### 13.5 A home that sleeps

A person's DorkOS in the cloud sleeps when idle. Opening a page wakes it through its front door, as opening the app does (§13.2, item 3); the first view after a sleep waits for the wake. Whether the hosted side serves a page's files without waking the home is asked in the contract change, not decided here (§23). A server a person runs does not sleep.

### 13.6 An agent on a cloud agent computer (PS18)

Its home is the person's DorkOS in the cloud, and its folder is on the agent computer (ADR `261125-113808`). The copy of a folder is always made by the computer side where the folder is, so for such an agent the cloud home asks the agent computer, over item 8's contract, with one new operation: `files.copy`, from the home to the computer, read only, under the feature `publishing.copy`. It walks a folder inside that agent's own folder, or checks a given list of paths there, by the same rules of §5.2 and the limits of §5.7, and answers the file list and the bytes in chunks. It writes nothing on the computer. §18.9 gives it in full. Until PS18 lands, or when the computer does not name the feature, a web files or doc file source of such an agent answers `not_publishable`, `folder_on_agent_computer`; a canvas document or a mini app the cloud home holds publishes without it.

## 14. What stage 1 leaves room for

### 14.1 Stage 2: a server half in a sealed box

Not designed here, and nothing here decides anything about the box. Stage 1 leaves room for it:

- **The store** leaves room for a version to name more than its files, since new columns can be added to `page_versions` without changing any.
- **Serving** leaves room for a page's own routes: the content host keeps `/_dorkos/` for itself (§6.3), and a page fetches from its own origin (`connect-src 'self'`). In stage 1 no page code runs on the home, and nothing in the page server runs code.
- **The capabilities** leave room for it: `page.publish` refuses a server half today with `uses_host`, by a reason a later stage can drop.
- **The manifest** leaves room for it: `standalone` means a page half that needs nothing else, and keeps that meaning.

### 14.2 Stage 3: a named grant

Not designed here. Stage 1 leaves room for it: the capabilities' inputs are strict objects, so a later field can be added without changing any. ADR rule 8 already says that whatever arrives through a grant takes the one path for sending to an agent, and is marked as words from outside.

## 22. Risks

- **A content host with its own rule in the list takes time to arrange.** A server a person runs cannot serve pages until its operator has one. The setting's help says so first, and §13's rule then sends to a DorkOS in the cloud or offers the canvas. A content host with no list entry is a later step (§14.3).
- **Pages that load from other addresses.** A deck that loads a library from another address does not get it in stage 1, since a page loads only from its own origin. The list before the copy names each outside address, so the publisher can copy the library into the folder.
- **The bundle check reads names.** A mini app whose own code uses one of §11.4's names for something else is refused, with the call named.
- **Old versions are kept.** A person can forget what an older version showed. The list shows the version each page shows and who can open it, and a page keeps at most 20 versions.
- **A view pass lasts 30 minutes.** An owner who reads a page longer opens it again from the app.
- **Files that may not grow.** `http/hono-api.ts` and `packages/shared/src/config-schema.ts` are over 500 lines: PS3 routes in `http/front-door.ts`, and PS2 moves a block out of the config schema before adding its own. `packages/cloud-api/src/hubs.ts` is moved apart in PS13 before it changes.

## 23. Open questions, each with a pick

1. **Whether pages shared by link expire.** Pick: no; the owner's list shows every page, who can open it and which version (§12.2). Other option: a default expiry, such as 30 days, that the owner can lift.
2. **Whether an agent may publish a new version of a shared page without a yes.** Pick: no, it needs the same yes as widening (§9.2). Other option: no yes for a new version, only for a wider audience.
3. **Whether the hosted side serves a page's files while its home sleeps.** Pick: ask in the contract change (§13.2); until it is answered, the home wakes. Other option: decide now that the home always wakes.
4. **Whether a doc's copy is rendered once at publish or carries its source.** Pick: rendered once (§5.4): a published doc has no code, so it cannot render in the viewer's browser, and rendering at publish keeps what the publisher saw and what viewers get the same. Other option: carry the markdown and a renderer script.
5. **The permission area.** Pick: a new area, `pages` (§8.1), so an owner can stop publishing alone. Other option: the mini apps area, `extensions`.
6. **How the owner's view is carried after the handoff.** Pick: a `__Host-` view pass on the page's own origin (§7), because a page's files name each other by paths from `/`. Other option: a signed prefix in every path, with no cookie, which breaks those paths.

Decided and no longer open: a page is a saved copy (PG1); private first (PG2); in stage 1 a person answers the card (§9.4); which install serves pages, by one rule (§13); in stage 1 a content host has its own explicit rule in the Public Suffix List, on the hosted side and on a server a person runs alike, and a server with none has no published pages until the named later step (§6.1, §14.3); a page on its own origin apart from the app (PG4); no server half in stage 1 (PG5); the agent's owner owns what the agent publishes (§4.4); web files are their own kind (§5.3); a Mac alone cannot publish (§13.3).
