## Overview

Require positive, resource-specific evidence before closing storage, deleting reset data, releasing instance authority or launching a successor. Keep process-exit permission distinct from cleanup success and successor readiness. Specify a conservative availability tradeoff and a staged local proof without implementing a general shutdown framework.

## Background / Problem Statement

The [source census](root-ownership.md) records mixed cleanup guarantees hidden behind a void root promise. Some owners only clear timers or abort; some swallow errors or give up waiting. Composite closers can release dependencies internally before root policy gets a result. Admin reset's current DB/delete ordering follows root lock release, and successor launch proceeds after cleanup failure. A lock inside the deleted directory cannot protect that same recursive deletion merely by delaying its explicit release.

Main admission closure prevents new work at selected ingress seams but leaves admitted operations and other producers active. DOR-2429's workspace generation fence remains a narrow proven property. Neither establishes complete root quiescence.

## Goals

- Preserve owner identity, acquired-resource evidence, original failure, cleanup failures, pending work and exact effect fences.
- Bound observation without reclassifying timeout as drain or skipping independent safe closures.
- Define separate DB, deletion, authority, successor and exit permissions and successful stop/restart order.
- Refuse automated reset until deletion-compatible exclusion is proved.
- Define a minimal laboratory and meaningful acceptance/mutation controls; keep broader implementation explicitly gated.

## Non-Goals

No source implementation, generic lifecycle framework, new endpoint/config/schema/dependency, paid inference or live effects. No claim that current void closers meet the proposed contract. No automatic retry, reopened admission, force-exit deadline or successor-readiness promise. No Cloud/Community redesign, full startup rollback, marketplace retention implementation or lock-layout change. Actual next writer cohort and full terminal-operation adoption remain separately selected work.

## Technical Dependencies

Existing Node HTTP/process boundaries, Express, ws, SQLite via current DB packages, Vitest fake timers/deferred promises and TypeScript AST support suffice for future proof. Reuse the existing WorkspaceReconciler and lifecycle owner in the local laboratory. DOR-2481's exclusive terminal-action protocol is required before production competing action adoption; admission-only DOR-2551 is insufficient. DOR-2483 and DOR-2484 retain startup rollback and retention work.

## Detailed Design

### Selected default and availability tradeoff

Fail closed on destructive handoff. Close admission synchronously, stop producers, collect named cleanup outcomes for at most **30 seconds of observation**, and authorize each subsequent effect only from positive prerequisite evidence. Thirty seconds is a proposed operator waiting budget, not an all-services drain deadline and not a mandate to terminate processes. Keep existing smaller owner deadlines, including the workspace five-second deadline; never lengthen or replace them silently.

If required evidence is failed, unfinished, absent or unknown at the budget boundary, refuse DB closure, reset deletion, explicit instance-lock release and successor launch for every affected resource. Retain a sticky terminal operation and keep main admission closed. Do not reopen a partly dismantled server. Keep the process, database handle and lock held while unresolved local work is still able to use them. Continue observing already-started promises so rejection cannot become unhandled. Retain a bounded in-memory refusal receipt naming blocking owners and whether work is unfinished or failed; emit it only through the permitted reporting channel described below.

**Availability tradeoff:** an uncertain shutdown can leave an unavailable, terminal process holding its directory until an operator resolves it. That is preferable to automatically starting a second writer or deleting data below unfinished work. The 30-second budget limits how long the first decision takes; it does not guarantee process exit in 30 seconds. No extra status endpoint or automatic retry is proposed here. Successful stop/restart remains possible when all required evidence arrives earlier.

This specification defines a proposed contract and staged proof plan only. No source implementation is selected. It does not authorize replacing current behavior until the owners and terminal action protocol have been implemented and verified in a separately selected bounded slice. Current global DB/delete/lock/successor permissions remain unestablished.

### Outcome model: evidence must retain its dimensions

One flat “success” enum is insufficient. Each owner receipt should retain:

- Exact owner/instance and terminal-operation identity, resources it may affect, and whether it was acquired. “Not acquired” needs ownership evidence; an undefined reference after it was discarded is not evidence.
- Admission/production state: closed, open, or unknown. New acquisition must be blocked as well as existing work enumerated.
- Completion state: drained, pending at deadline, failed, or not started because a dependency was unsafe. Drained means all named work and its finalizers have settled, not that a timer was removed.
- Independently proven effect fences, such as “cannot mutate workspace SQLite rows after generation changes.” Attach their exact resource scope and verification reference. A fence is not automatically a drain or a guarantee that an open handle was closed.
- Error records with their original causes, and cleanup errors separately. A failed action may still establish a narrow fence; preserve both facts instead of hiding failure inside “fenced.”
- Deadline/observation timestamps and late outcome events. Freeze the operation's permission decision at refusal; a late success can be reported but must not silently launch a previously refused reset or restart.

The workspace owner can truthfully report pending-at-deadline plus a proven cache-write fence. Existing generic `Promise<void>` closers cannot be upgraded to this representation merely by wrapping their return values. A caught adapter failure, scheduler deadline exhaustion, or bounded pump wait remains failed/unfinished unless its own evidence establishes the needed resource safety.

### Dependency and error-continuation policy

Use a reviewed dependency graph for a selected writer cohort, not a new general framework by default. An edge means an unsettled writer may still use a dependency. Closing consumers before their unresolved producers is prohibited. Stop-admission requests that are synchronous and known safe should run before awaiting any owner, so one hang cannot leave independent producers running.

Examples to preserve and validate: scheduler stop-message publication requires Relay; messaging adapters may drain through Relay; Relay may write trace/DB; turn-end callbacks must unsubscribe before projection watchers close; domain work must finish before shared DB closure. Account usage must retain its persistence dependency through the final flush. Trace exporter/file closure belongs after its producers, but a reporting network failure is not automatically a veto on an unrelated resource.

**Composite closer barrier:** withholding the root's final release is insufficient when a called method releases a dependency internally. The current scheduler releases its leader lock before waiting for runs; Relay close reaches its SQLite index after stop attempts that can swallow failures. A policy collector must not invoke a composite method whose internal effects can cross an unproven permission boundary. In a separately selected implementation, split that owner into producer-admission closure, observable quiescence/finalization, and permission-gated dependency release, or withhold the entire unsafe composite call while invoking only independently proven-safe stop primitives. Merely wrapping its void return with a timeout is forbidden. Record withheld work as dependency-blocked and retain its dependencies; do not describe it as an attempted successful drain. The scheduler leader lease, Relay index and consolidated DB are distinct resources with distinct prerequisites. No split is implemented or authorized by this design.

On one owner's failure, record the original failure immediately and continue independent **safe** cleanup branches. A dependent closer is withheld if it would remove a resource still needed by unfinished work. Mark it dependency-blocked, naming the edge; do not silently skip it or falsely report drained. Do not blanket parallelize the current root sequence. Each awaited call has immediate rejection observation and a separate outcome; failure in reporting must not replace the triggering startup/operation failure.

A root-wide observation timeout does not cancel the underlying closers. The collector may stop waiting and issue its refused permission receipt while already-started cleanup continues. Additional dependent destructive steps cannot automatically launch when those promises later settle. Truly independent safe cleanup can still be attempted, but must neither release a required dependency nor erase the retained decision/error. No independent closer should wait behind a known hanging unrelated branch merely because of current source order.

### Aggregate budget semantics

Propose a monotonic 30,000 ms budget from terminal reservation/admission closure, covering cleanup observation, not arbitrary future destructive action duration. Every dispatched owner sees remaining time; local deadlines can be shorter. At the exact boundary, unfinished owners become pending-at-deadline in the decision receipt. Tests inject clock/deferred work, not wall-clock sleeps.

A synchronous blocking call can overrun an event-loop timer; this budget is not hard preemption. Scheduler's current 30-second loop starts after an awaited relay-stop phase, so it does not satisfy this aggregate contract. Other local deadlines remain identifiable rather than added sequentially into an unbounded total. Do not skip safe synchronous admission closures when remaining time reaches zero.

If cleanup qualifies before the budget, evaluate action permissions. Reset deletion or successor launch is a distinct phase with its own reviewed failure policy; no timeout race may authorize repeating rm/spawn, ignoring an uncertain result, releasing a lock or claiming readiness. Do not invent an automatic “force after 30 seconds” path.

**Important lock/reset constraint:** `apps/server/src/lib/instance-lock.ts:1–24,148–167,223–228` uses a JSON claim file _inside dorkHome_, created with `wx` and removed on release. Recursive removal of the entire directory also removes that authority token. Merely delaying release() until after current fs.rm cannot retain exclusivity during deletion. A later reset design must prove a deletion plan that preserves the claim path for the whole destructive phase, or a separately designed stable exclusion boundary outside that deletion target. Do not change lock layout in this design or assume the current lock can protect its own recursive deletion. Default recommendation until that is resolved: refuse automated reset handoff, even if the DB cohort is clean.

**Process exit caveat:** the instance lock corroborates holder liveness; stale ownership can be reclaimed after death. Leaving a lock file then exiting does not keep a successor out. Exiting ends this process's JS but can leave child/external writers and partially completed operations; a supervisor may immediately restart. Accordingly, unresolved handoff cannot be made safe merely by exiting nonzero. A future clean signal-stop policy may authorize exit independently only when all relevant external-writer and supervisor conditions are proven. Repeated signals should observe the same operation; OS force-kill is not advertised as safe reset/restart.

### Successful stop/restart ordering, conditional on all prerequisites

The proposed normal path below is executable only once the actual writer census, owner contracts, terminal operation owner and late-startup-acquisition fences are implemented and verified. It does not grant permissions to today's composite closers. Every step is once-only for the winning operation:

1. Reserve the terminal kind; close main and other relevant producer admission synchronously. Prevent late startup registration/acquisition, not just new HTTP requests. Preserve the winning admin response handoff without waiting for its own terminal action to finish.
2. Quiesce the actual admitted handlers, command callbacks, detached turns, queues, background writers, separately admitted listener work and owned children. Required children must have observed exit, not merely received a kill request. Keep their storage and messaging dependencies available through completion/finalizers. A resource-specific fence can substitute only for the exact effects and access types it demonstrably prevents.
3. Perform required final flushes after their producers are closed/quiescent. Quiesce and disable/close protected file loggers and exporters before protected filesystem handoff; all subsequent reporting must use the surviving channel below. Record both failure and resource-safety facts. A noncritical reporting failure can be tolerated only by an explicit action-specific rule proving it leaves no relevant local access.
4. Close the shared DB and each owned storage handle only after their complete reader/writer/late-acquisition prerequisites are proven. Observe closure success. Do not infer global DB closure from no-op TaskStore/TraceStore methods.
5. Close and observe the main listener's closure after admitted work is drained, excluding the winning admin request from any self-drain dependency. Its local acknowledgment handoff has occurred; transport cleanup must not wait for that operation's own completion. Explicitly account for established upgrades and other listeners through their own proven contracts. No active handler may still need closed storage. Main-port release must precede successor spawn; a callback timeout is not port-release evidence.
6. Release instance authority only after all protected old-generation effects/handles are safe and required listener closure has succeeded. For reset, the separately proved deletion/exclusion phase must have completed successfully before authority handoff. **Default automated reset remains refused until deletion-compatible exclusion is proven.** This design selects no lock-layout or deletion implementation.
7. For restart only, perform one successor-launch attempt after authority and port handoff. A successful spawn is not successor readiness or confirmed directory acquisition. For stop, perform no successor launch.
8. Exit the old process only under a separate explicit exit permission establishing that no owned external writer can survive in conflict and no unresolved operation still requires its authority. Report clean operation success only when the selected action's success conditions hold. A supervisor may impose additional restart behavior that must be included before granting exit permission.

If a prerequisite fails before authority release, withhold dependent effects and retain authority. If release throws or its outcome is uncertain, do not launch a successor, retry release, reopen admission, or repeat deletion. Preserve the original and cleanup failures and report authority as retained or uncertain according to actual evidence. Do not pretend an attempted release was successful.

If release is confirmed but successor launch fails, authority is already relinquished: report that fact honestly, remain terminal, and do not automatically reacquire the lock, spawn again, reopen service or repeat reset deletion. The operator may recover with a fresh start after inspecting the recorded outcome. Old-process exit may still be allowed if all independent exit prerequisites were proven, even though restart failed; its status must remain failure and must not imply a ready successor. If launch has an ambiguous result, do not retry; identify whether a child may exist before operator recovery. Failure does not grant exit when external writers or supervisor effects remain unresolved.

A failure after irreversible deletion cannot be rolled back by repeating the action. The future reset protocol must define how partial deletion is reported while preserving exclusion; until then it has no automatic success path under this proposed contract. Ordinary stop/restart ordering above is a conditional contract, not an assertion that current shutdownServices supplies its prerequisites.

### Surviving reporting channel and protected-file boundary

Verified source: `apps/server/src/lib/logger.ts:100` uses `fs.appendFileSync(logFile, ...)` for each file-reporter event; `:283–300` sets the path, creates the directory and attaches that reporter. Root initialization at `apps/server/src/index.ts:888,987` supplies `path.join(dorkHome, 'logs')` in the initial admission/eligibility source. At the refreshed merged source these initialization calls are at `index.ts:889,988`; the protected-file dependency is unchanged. The ordinary logger is therefore a protected-tree writer, not a safe channel for post-handoff failure reporting. No terminal file-reporter shutdown API was found in that inspected module; this is an unmet implementation prerequisite, not an assumed existing capability.

Before protected filesystem authority handoff or reset deletion, quiesce log-producing work and flush/close or disable every file reporter/exporter touching that tree, including captured/tagged logger references and late callbacks. Disabling only a root variable does not prove those references inert. An unfenced protected reporter blocks the affected handoff. No normal logger call, rotation, append, directory creation or file reopening is permitted afterward, even to report release/spawn failure.

Keep a bounded final receipt in memory with separate primary-operation and reporting-error records. After protected sinks are disabled, optionally make one bounded-size, best-effort stderr emission only when the sink's ownership and resource classification explicitly establish that this write cannot touch the protected tree or a handed-off resource. A stderr FD redirected to a protected file is not safe merely because its number is 2; unknown ownership means skip output, retain the in-memory receipt and, only if independently permitted to exit, communicate failure through the exit status. Do not inspect or reopen protected paths to rescue reporting after handoff. Bounded-size output is not a hard time bound on an arbitrary synchronous descriptor; use only a known nonblocking/appropriately owned reporting seam or skip it rather than delaying handoff/exit on an unknown sink.

Sink failure adds a separate reporting error without replacing the original cause. It causes no retry, normal-logger fallback, service reopen, repeated deletion, new handoff wait or invented success. Failure to emit through an already classified safe optional sink does not veto otherwise proven handoff; inability to fence the protected file logger does. A late cleanup rejection after the reporting boundary is recorded in memory and may use only this surviving channel. In-memory receipts are intentionally not durable across exit; do not promise that an unavailable sink delivered a report.

### One operation owns all terminal effects

DOR-2481's full terminal owner must synchronously reserve one kind (reset/restart/stop) and close admission, before acknowledgment/scheduling, retaining identity and outcome through cleanup and actions. Signals and already-admitted admin requests must join/refuse that same operation. A signal cannot independently exit while reset deletion is running. A lost acknowledgment cannot release consent or schedule another winner. Do not consume a losing reset token. No automatic retry/reopen on cleanup failure, and no operation promotion when late work finishes.

This dependency is required for full production handoff adoption; it is not authorization to sneak terminal-owner implementation into the admission-only PR. DOR-2551 remains valid on its own with its explicit admitted-work exclusions.

### File and API boundaries

This design adds no runtime API or file. A future selected laboratory belongs beside workspace lifecycle tests and uses disposable local storage and injected effect ports. Do not land an unused generic registry or permission framework. Root production changes require separate selection after every affected prerequisite is proven; a prototype evaluator must not authorize current shutdownServices. The design artifacts live in `specs/shutdown-handoff-outcomes/`, with the refreshed source census in `root-ownership.md` and the proposed ADR in the decision index.

## User Experience

Recommend a bounded in-memory receipt, optionally emitted through the explicitly safe surviving channel: operation kind and local identity, elapsed observation, blocking owners, pending versus failed distinction, retained resources, and actions not performed. Avoid dumping secrets or full filesystem content. Explain that main requests stay refused because cleanup is partial, and that no automatic reset/restart was authorized. Preserve the original failure separately from cleanup/reporting errors.

For non-destructive recovery, the operator can inspect the named process and its owned children, arrange their termination through their existing supervisor, and restart only after confirming the previous writers are gone. For destructive recovery, do not advise deleting the live lock or bypassing it; require confirmed writer termination and explicit renewed reset intent. A force-kill may lose unfinished work and is outside clean completion. This is a deliberate availability cost, not a concealed successful shutdown. No new recovery API or automatic “retry terminal operation” is included in this proposal.

Accepted, cleaned, launched and ready are distinct observations. Preserve the existing accepted acknowledgment under the future exclusive owner, but never label it successful cleanup. The conservative proposed policy may keep a partially stopped process alive and the app unavailable until operator action. Recovery instructions must reflect whether authority remains held, is uncertain or was already released.

## Testing Strategy

Recommend **workspace reconciliation plus an isolated SQLite cache handle and a fake instance-lock/successor port** as the first policy laboratory. Its existing generation fence is already proven, making this a bounded place to test outcome-to-permission interpretation without promising the whole server is ready. Add an independently held synthetic consumer of the same disposable DB to prove a single green owner cannot authorize shared-resource handoff. This second consumer is a test collaborator, not a claim of actual HTTP coverage.

## Performance Considerations

Thirty seconds is the chosen proposed observation default under autonomous design authority, measured from reservation/admission close with a monotonic clock. It adds no runtime work in this design delivery. It is not hard preemption: a blocked event loop can delay observation. Future outcome collection should scale with owned work and resource dependencies, not poll every request or add persistent records by default.

## Security Considerations

Unknown/failed prerequisites deny irreversible effects. Admission closure is not authorization. Preserve reset consent, host/origin/session controls and one winning operation. Keep lock authority and storage available to unresolved writers; do not advise bypassing a live claim. Generic logs must omit secrets. Force-kill and supervisor restart can invalidate authority assumptions and remain outside clean-handoff guarantees until explicitly proven.

## Documentation

This delivery contains the ideation, this contract, refreshed source census and proposed ADR after admission delivery and source/ownership refresh; the design has passed independent requirements review. Preserve DOR-2429's historical verification rather than expanding its guarantee. A design completion note must say implementation remains unselected and list the laboratory/production gates. No release note may claim runtime shutdown behavior from this document alone.

## Implementation Phases

1. Design delivery: define the outcome dimensions, permission tables, observation budget, successful ordering and refusal/recovery policy. The post-admission source and ownership refresh is recorded here; the human assignment is preserved. This phase changes documentation only.
2. Separately select a laboratory proof, if authorized: real disposable SQLite and workspace class, synthetic second consumer, fake authority/listener/successor ports. Prove narrow permission interpretation and mutation sensitivity without root adoption.
3. Select an actual writer cohort only after reviewing its complete access and composite-closer boundaries. Required owner splits, late-acquisition controls and all-owner census expansion remain individually reviewed implementation work, not authorized wholesale here.
4. Only after complete action-specific prerequisites and DOR-2481's exclusive terminal owner are ready may root DB/lock/successor handoff be selected for production. Automated reset has an additional hard prerequisite: deletion-compatible exclusion proved for its entire destructive phase. Until then it remains refused under the proposed policy.

## Open Questions

No unanswered policy choice prevents review of this design. Remaining implementation evidence is an explicit gate, not a completed guarantee.

- ~~Observation budget?~~ **(RESOLVED)** Propose 30 seconds from reservation/admission close. It bounds the first decision only; retain smaller owner deadlines and deny affected handoff when evidence is pending. This reviewable default is chosen under operator autonomy, not measured as a shutdown SLO.
- ~~What if a required owner fails?~~ **(RESOLVED)** Retain the cause, continue independent safe closures, withhold dependencies and irreversible effects. Never reopen terminal admission or automatically retry.
- ~~Can automated reset use the current recursive deletion?~~ **(RESOLVED)** No under this proposed contract. Refuse it until exclusion survives the entire destructive phase; no lock-layout implementation is selected.
- ~~How does stop/restart complete?~~ **(RESOLVED)** Follow the conditional ordering above, with proven storage and port closure before authority release/spawn and a distinct exit permission. Missing census, child-exit or supervisor evidence blocks the affected permission.
- ~~May remote/reporting failures be ignored?~~ **(RESOLVED)** They remain in the in-memory receipt and use only an explicitly safe reporting channel. They may be non-blocking only with positive evidence of no relevant local access and an explicit action-specific classification; unknowns block affected permissions by default.
- ~~What work follows the laboratory?~~ **(RESOLVED)** No actual timer-only production cohort is selected. A fresh ownership/readiness review and separate bounded selection are required.
