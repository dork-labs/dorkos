Simplify: three shapes for the Profile

Portrait header stays. Everything else is on trial. First, what an S-tier designer would cut no matter which shape we pick:

4 nav systems → 1Inner tabs, section labels, chips, and buttons all competed. One way to move.
Chips → one sentence“Working in #team · 2 min” beats dot + chip + badge + pill.
Sessions list → one rowThe sidebar already lists sessions. The profile says “12 · last 2 h →”.
Schedule presets → goneSelling presets is Tasks’ job. Show a schedule only if one exists.
Personality pill → behind the faceTap the face → face + personality picker. Not a status.
Model/effort controls → one rowThe composer already has live pickers. Profile shows the default; tap to change.
Section labels → noneNOW / ABOUT / RECENT explain nothing the rows don’t. Rams: less, but better.
Kebab of everything → rare onlySet default · Block · Unregister · Delete. Nothing else hides there.

Why it works
  • Profile stays a face; Manage stays a settings list. Neither pollutes the other.
  • Grouped settings are the pattern people already know (iOS Settings).
Risk
  • A segmented control is a tab bar wearing a hat.
  • Sessions have no home in the profile — you go to the sidebar.

My pick: A, with C’s grouped list as what “Manage” pushes into. A keeps the profile a face for every identity kind, puts the three things people actually do (message, see work, manage) as one calm row, and hides settings until asked — without inventing a mode switch. B is the right shape for the Manage screen itself, not for the profile.
