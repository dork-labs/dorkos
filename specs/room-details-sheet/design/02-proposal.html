Better than Slack

Slack's sheet is a good read-only roster with a control problem it never had to solve. We have the opposite: a control with no roster around it.

Slack's own “Add people, agents, or apps” button sits three rows under a tab bar that splits Members from Agents & apps. They mix them where it matters and segregate them where it's visible. That contradiction is the seam we walk through.
Steal
✓
Adding is a row, not a second panel

One list. The first item adds to it. This alone kills our intent prop and our second heading.

✓
The count is in the title

“Members 4”. We say nothing.

✓
A second line per person

They use job title. We have something better: managed by, and whether it has ever spoken here.

✓

“Dorian (you)”. We omit the reader entirely.

✓
A right-aligned pill slot

They put “Channel Manager” there. That is exactly where our loudness goes.

Reject
✕
Segregating agents into a tab

Our whole thesis is that agents are participants. One list, one badge.

✕
A search field for four members

Chrome before content. Search appears when the roster earns it — say, eight.

✕
An “Everyone” filter at four members

A dropdown whose every option shows the same list.

✕
Six tabs over three fields

Our room has a name, a topic and an archived flag. Tabs would be scaffolding around nothing.

What they cannot do
★
Say what the room will do

Slack's roster describes people. Ours can describe behaviour — and total it.

★
Presence that means work

Their green dot means a browser tab is open. Ours means a turn is running right now.

The mode control, resolved

Four rungs, not five. Ordered quiet→loud so position is the meaning. Two of today's five values are aliases that swap identity by room kind — so the room decides which four you see, and the stored value always maps to a rung.

In a channel
Silent — never speaks here
@only — only when you @mention it ← direct-only lands here too
Engaged — @mention, then 10 min / 5 messages ← default
Everything — answers every message
In a DM · three rungs, because there are three behaviours
Silent — never speaks here
@only — only when you @mention it ← engaged lands here; its window never opens
Everything — answers you every time ← direct-only lands here
Today the DM menu offers four options that produce three behaviours. Two pairs are indistinguishable.

Where the delight lives

Motion that explains, not motion that decorates. Every one of these is honest — it shows a consequence that is really there.

Hover a pill → the room previews itself

The room meter at the top slides to what it would become, and the sentence rewrites. Let go and it slides back. You learn the whole model in three seconds without reading a word of help.

Change a rung → both meters move together

120ms. The member's bars and the room's bars animate as one system, so cause and effect are the same gesture. This is the confirmation — no toast needed.

An agent working is alive on the disc

The pulse already exists in AgentAvatar and the server already publishes the signal. Opening this panel mid-run should feel like walking into a room where people are busy.

Added agents land, they don't appear

The chip becomes the row: it slides into position and pulses once. Today they silently vanish from one list and silently appear in another.

Remove offers Undo

Archiving a whole room already has undo. Removing one agent — which destroys its session binding — has none. That's backwards.

The empty room opens ready

Not a grey sentence. The picker is already open, already focused, with the create dialog's honest line: “a channel with nobody in it has nobody to answer you.”

One decision I need from you

Everything above fits inside the members panel. The question is whether it stays a members panel.

A

Keep it a members panel

Everything designed above, in the dialog that exists. Topic, rename and archive stay where they are — in the sidebar row menu and their own dialogs. Smaller change, ships sooner, four surfaces still describe one room.

B

Make it the room sheet

The panel above is the design — note the editable topic in the header and “Archive room” in the footer. This absorbs RoomTopicDialog, inline rename and the archive AlertDialog. One place for everything about a room, and three surfaces retire. Bigger change; Slack's tabs are the evidence it's the right shape, minus the tabs.
