What the screenshot proves
1

The setting outweighs the person

The member's name is 14px in a 24px disc. The dropdown under it is 36px tall, full-bleed, and bordered. The eye lands on the control, not on who it belongs to. In a panel called Members, the member is the smaller element.

2

Two identical sentences, stacked

Both agents read “Replies while it is in the conversation.” Twenty-four repeated characters of low-contrast text, twice, telling you nothing about the difference between these two agents — because there isn't one. Sameness should be silent; only difference deserves ink.

3

Two headings, two explanations, one dialog

“Who is in here, and when each agent replies” … then a rule, then “Add agents”, then “They join here and can read everything already said.” The panel explains itself twice because it is two panels.

4

A directory listing, not a roster · worst on screen

144mono · 144x.co · ab-monorepo · AgentFS · Alpha Remount Agent · API Server · Art Blocks Analytics… Every project folder you own, alphabetically, in plain text, with no faces at all — while features/tasks/ui/AgentPicker.tsx ten directories away renders the same list with avatars. Sorted so that numbers come first. There is no recency, no “agents you actually use”, no grouping. This is the file system leaking into the product.

5

The heaviest element is the one you can't press

Full-width, mid-grey, bottom-anchored — it reads as the dialog's primary action and it is disabled. Meanwhile the two things you came to do (change a mode, remove someone) have no visual weight at all.

6

You are not in the room

No count, no “2 agents”, and the human — the person reading — appears nowhere. The panel answers “who is in here?” with a list that omits you and doesn't total itself.

7

Nothing tells you the room is fine

Two agents both in the loudest useful mode is the single most consequential fact on this screen, and it is expressed as two identical grey sentences you have to read and compare yourself.

The mode menu, as a stranger meets it

These five, in this order, are what the dropdown shows. Try to rank them by loudness without reading the source.

Replies to everything
Replies while it is in the conversation ← which conversation? it's in the room, isn't it?
Replies when spoken to directly ← isn't “directly” just @mentioning it?
Replies only when @mentioned ← …then what was the previous one?
Never replies on its own ← “on its own” implies it replies some other way

They are ordered loudest→quietest in code. Nothing on screen says so. No scale, no grouping, no numbers — and the real rule behind option 2 is “10 minutes or 5 other messages, whichever runs out first”, which the UI never mentions even though it's a setting you own.
