## Research Summary

Commissioned 2026-08-09 to answer: what problem do workspaces exist to solve, do we need them, is the name right, how do competitors solve it, what does the community say, why don't our own agents use the managed layer, and what would make the feature 10x. Inputs: two parallel external research passes (competitor product/UX survey + practitioner sentiment, ~90 cited sources, both reproduced in full as Appendices A/B), internal code/disk archaeology, and the 2026-08-09 workspace audit (safe fixes landed as PR #885; direction decision parked as DOR-1056). Builds on `research/20260611_workspace_strategy_runtimes_symphony.md`; its two headline claims — cleanup is the industry's biggest failure mode, port isolation is an unowned gap — are independently re-confirmed here, two months later.

## Key Findings

1. **The core problem is real, proven internally, and narrower than the feature we built.** One checkout, one writer: DOR-500 measured pervasive write interleaving with six agents in one tree; the auto-checkpoint race ate a commit during DOR-101. The demand shape is _isolation plus the services around it_ — env cloning, ports, safe cleanup, one place to see everything — not "a server that can provision checkouts nobody asked for."

2. **Our agents already live the isolation policy — 100% outside the managed layer.** This machine: 23 live worktrees (62 GB) under `~/.dork/workspaces/<project>/<key>` — the manager's own root — versus **0 rows and 0 sidecar manifests ever** in the managed store. Root causes, all verified: no caller (zero client `workspaceKey` senders), no adoption (SQL-only `list()`, prune-only reconciler), and weaker provisioning than gtr (this repo has no `.dork/workspace.json`, so a managed tree would come up with no env, no deps, no built packages, while `.gtrconfig` handles all of it).

3. **Cleanup safety is the trust battleground of the whole category.** Claude Code's tracker carries 10+ distinct cleanup data-loss/stale-state issues (#46444: 10 days of work destroyed; #48927: cleanup destroyed `.git`); Cursor force-deleted user branches; Codex cleanup killed a live agent's worktree; the emblematic story is an agent force-deleting 29 worktrees after a vague "clean up?" approval. Products here are judged on cleanup safety more than isolation elegance. DorkOS's dirty-gate + pin + never-auto-remove posture (and PR #885's retention-honoring sweep) is exactly on-thesis.

4. **Worktrees do not end git races or conflicts.** Anthropic's own #55724: 13 parallel worktree-isolated agents → 8 failed on shared `.git` lock contention; lock failure + eager auto-cleanup = destroyed work. Semantic conflicts survive too: cross-agent PRs conflict at 41.7% vs 19.8% intra-agent (arXiv 2607.04697). Isolation moves collisions from the filesystem to the merge; the review surface is where they land.

5. **Port allocation remains an unowned differentiator.** Conductor is still the only competitor solving ports natively (`CONDUCTOR_PORT`, a block of 10). Everyone else circulates hash-the-branch folklore. DorkOS's server-side port authority (ADR-0284) is built, shipped — and invisible, because nothing provisions through it.

6. **The #1 post-adoption complaint is the "gitignored tail":** `.env`, `node_modules`, build caches don't follow the worktree. A single-purpose tool exists just to copy `.env` into worktrees. A de facto standard emerged in ~8 weeks: **`.worktreeinclude`** (root file, gitignore syntax, only-copies-already-ignored invariant) — now shared verbatim by Claude Code _and_ Conductor. Adopt it; don't invent a third name.

7. **Naming: "workspace" is contested, "worktree" is the practitioner's word, and the unit that matters is the task.** Copilot CLI uses "workspace isolation" to mean the _absence_ of isolation ("shared desk") — the exact inversion of our usage. Slack/VS Code/Notion overload it further; inside DorkOS the word already means six things. Product tools split: workspace (Conductor, Vibe Kanban), worktree (Claude Code, Cursor), environment (Codex, Jules, container-use). Practitioners say "worktree" unglossed for the mechanism and fall back to plain language ("a fresh checkout", "its own copy") to explain the concept.

8. **The skeptic current is mainstream and bounded: the ceiling is human attention (2–4 agents), and the win is _reduced hesitation_, not throughput.** "Your cognitive bandwidth doesn't parallelize" (Osmani). The best reframe: the bottleneck isn't review, it's _unstructured_ review — parallelism works when each tree has a tight contract and an auditable diff. Corollary: **forced isolation is the single most-repeated UX grievance** (8+ duplicate Claude Code Desktop issues); Zed's opt-in-per-thread is the model users ask for.

9. **Cloud vs local: the location is transitional, the primitive is durable.** Cloud-first entrants shut down (Terragon, Vibe Kanban's cloud) while local orchestrators keep shipping; both converge on one shape: structured task → isolated environment → progress → validation → PR → receipt. The durable bet is the provider seam (ADR-0283 already is one). DB branching is the most under-exploited adjacent lever — shared DB/migration state is the pain that makes practitioners quit worktrees, and nobody local solves it.

10. **Isolation is a feature, not a business.** Three category exits in seven months (Crystal → Nimbalyst, Vibe Kanban/Bloop, Terragon); platform vendors give isolation away as plumbing; Conductor monetized by moving _up_ (cloud, multiplayer, teams). Meanwhile **Buzz (Block, July 2026, ~17k stars in a week)** attacks DorkOS's team-room thesis from the other side — identity, channels, provenance, its own git forge — and has _deliberately skipped the working-copy problem entirely_. The defensible DorkOS claim is the **composition**: team room + runtime-agnostic workspace lifecycle with ports, provisioning, and safe retention. No single-vendor tool can copy the runtime-agnostic half.

## Internal reality (verified 2026-08-09)

### The problem, from our own history

- DOR-500: six concurrent agents in one tree → pervasive fine-grained write interleaving; two trees roughly doubled write survival.
- The `Stop`-hook auto-checkpoint (`git add -A` → `stash create` → `reset`) races concurrent git operations; it produced an empty-tree commit during DOR-101 dispatch. (It reproduced _within this very session_: the checkpoint hook unstaged files mid-amend while a background commit ran — one checkout, one writer applies even to one session's own background tasks.)
- Policy answer (AGENTS.md → Worktrees): default to an isolated worktree for any code change. Agents comply daily — via gtr, not via the manager.

### Why the managed layer has zero usage

1. **No caller.** `workspaceKey` on `POST /api/sessions/:id/messages` has zero client call sites; `POST /api/workspaces` is reachable only by curl. No UI create button, no flow integration.
2. **No adoption.** `WorkspaceStore.list()` is SQL-only; the reconciler only prunes rows. The 23 real worktrees in the manager's own root — put there deliberately by `.gtrconfig` "so the manager inherits them in place" — are invisible to it. The inheritance was never implemented.
3. **Weaker provisioning.** The manager reads repo-owned `.dork/workspace.json` (Symphony hook schema) — which this repo doesn't have. A managed tree of dorkos itself would come up unusable (no `.env`, no `.mcp.json`, no install, no built packages, DOR-117 class breakage), while `.gtrconfig` handles copy/install/build/ports today.
4. **Portability.** The agent-side skills work in bare `claude` CLI too; a server-bound flow wouldn't. Any managed path must not break that.

### The six meanings of "workspace" in this codebase

(A) Managed Workspace entity (the only thing `/workspaces` shows); (B) the `/workspaces` Home tab; (C) "agent workspace" = the directory an agent _is_ (`project-agent-workspace.ts`, scaffold instructions — the binding spec renames this to **agent home**); (D) channel workspace (spec-only); (E) Shape "workspace chrome" UI layout (marketplace manifest); (F) pnpm/Obsidian/Docker vocabulary. Externally, add Slack org / VS Code window / Notion tenant — and Copilot CLI's inverted usage. The sidebar redesign spec (`specs/sidebar-now-today-library` §16) already fled the word, adopting "project" for the repo/cwd dimension.

### The page as shipped

`/workspaces` is a first-class Home tab (`home-tabs.ts`), so every user sees it — and it is empty for 100% of users. Card actions are pin/remove only; no create, no adopt, no open-session, no diff/PR affordance, no port/preview link (the data exists: `portBase`). An operator with 23 live worktrees sees "No workspaces yet."

## Synthesis — answers to the operator's questions

### What is the core problem? How does it help the user?

**Two writers in one checkout destroy each other's work; everything else is services around that primitive.** For the user: run several agents at once without fear (the best articulation in the corpus: the win is that _"I hesitate less before spawning a new agent"_); always know where each agent is working; never lose uncommitted work to cleanup; never fight over port 3000. The mission-control framing: workspaces are the **"where" dimension of the fleet** — sessions are _what's happening_, workspaces are _where it lands on disk_.

### Do we really need it?

Isolation: **yes — proven by our own daily usage and by the whole industry converging on it.** The managed layer: **yes, but only refocused.** As built (provision-on-request, no adoption, no caller) it solved the wrong end and has zero usage to show for it. The two capabilities with proven demand are (1) _seeing and safely managing_ the trees that already exist, and (2) _auto-provisioning on intent_ (task/agent binding) with provisioning parity. Both are cheap relative to what's already built: entity, store, reconciler, port authority, dirty-gate, and providers all exist and are tested.

### Is the naming clear? Alternatives?

No. Six internal meanings, an inverted meaning in a major competing CLI, and three consumer-software collisions. Moves, in order of confidence:

1. **Retire "agent workspace" (meaning C) → "agent home."** Already specified in `agent-workspace-binding` §3.8. Uncontroversial; do it whenever that area is next touched.
2. **Stop using "workspace" as a user-facing noun.** Describe the thing in outcome language — "every agent works in its own copy of the repo, on its own branch, with its own ports" — which is what the two most-read practitioner authors do.
3. **Where a concrete noun is unavoidable in v1 UI, "worktree" is the honest one** (all v1 providers are git worktrees; it's the practitioners' unglossed word; Kai and Priya both speak it). Accept that it's provider-narrow: when container/cloud providers ship, the umbrella noun can become "environment" (the Codex/Jules/container-use convention for _where it runs_).
4. **Keep `Workspace` as the internal schema/API name.** Provider-neutral, matches `~/.dork/workspaces/`, and renaming the entity buys users nothing.
5. Brand-metaphor candidates ("desk", "bay" — "every agent gets its own desk") read well in marketing prose but are worse than "worktree" in a control panel; park them for copy, not for nouns.
6. **Coordinate with the sidebar IA**: its "project" grammar is the repo dimension; the isolated copy is a _row under_ a project (branch/task-shaped), not a rival top-level concept. The strongest version of the naming fix is structural: no standalone "Workspaces" surface at all — the fleet view's rows carry the isolation as an attribute.

### How do competing apps solve it?

Full survey in Appendix B. One line each: **Conductor** — best closed loop (workspace → diff+comments → checks → PR → archive), only native ports, now cloud microVMs at $50/mo; **Claude Code** — raw "worktree," strictest isolation enforcement, desktop forces it (loudest grievance), thin provisioning; **Codex** — "environments" picker (Local/Worktree/Cloud), best retention (cap + snapshot + pin + restore), GUI-authored committable setup; **Cursor** — worktrees as plumbing, `/apply-worktree` fold-back verb, the field's cleanup cautionary tale; **Buzz (Block)** — team-room + provenance + own forge, _no workspace layer at all_; **Zed** — opt-in per thread; **Sculptor/container-use** — containers ("environment"); **Jules/Codex Cloud** — cloud VMs; **Vibe Kanban/Crystal/Terragon** — dead or deprecated (isolation-as-business failed).

### What are people saying?

Full sentiment report in Appendix A. Compressed: clobbering fear is real but mostly a founding story — people isolate preemptively now; the _actual_ recurring injuries are cleanup eating work and the gitignored tail; git lock contention survives worktrees; DB/migration state makes people quit; the attention ceiling is 2–4 agents; and what they ask for is automatic invisible isolation, env cloning that includes services, assigned ports, one diff surface, and cleanup that cannot eat work — "receipts and reversibility over speed."

### Do we need to improve UI/UX? DX?

Yes, in this order: (UX) kill or fill the empty Home tab — its current state is a broken promise on a headline surface; then build the fleet "where" view with the review loop. (DX) one provisioning truth (`.worktreeinclude` + setup script absorbed into the manager, gtr as interim provider); keep everything working from the bare CLI; the API surface is already clean (Priya-grade) but undiscoverable.

### Visual companion?

Not needed for this research. Worth using at the design phase: mock the fleet-row (branch + dirty + sessions + ports + diff affordance) and the adopt/bind flows as side-by-side comparisons before building. Recommend running it after the DOR-1056 direction call.

## Appendix A — Practitioner sentiment (full agent report, 2026-08-09)

> Agent-produced; ~20 searches, ~45 sources. Reddit/X were unreachable to the agent (noted honestly inside); HN, GitHub issues, and dev blogs carry the first-person weight. Quotes re-verified before use in shipped copy.

### A1. The pain — what actually goes wrong

**Raw file clobbering in a shared checkout: real, but mostly a founding story, not a recurring complaint (MODERATE).** The clobbering narrative is overwhelmingly told in past tense by people who then built or adopted a tool. Cleanest first-person: the git-lanes author on Show HN — running Claude Code and Cursor on the same repo, "they kept overwriting each other's files" (HN 47285631). A side-project retrospective: don't point agents at the same files, "they overwrite each other's edits" (Harshal Patil). By 2026 the norm is to isolate _before_ being burned: the fear is well-founded, but lived-incident volume is lower than discourse volume.

**Git lock contention — real, measured, and it survives worktrees (STRONG).** Worktrees give each agent its own index, but `.git/` object creation, loose refs, and ref packing still lock shared resources. anthropics/claude-code#55724 reproduces it: 13 parallel worktree-isolated agents → 5 committed, 8 failed on `index.lock`; "at 5 agents: intermittent failures; at 10+: near-certain." #47266 covers `.git/config` lock races. The compounding failure: lock failure → agent exits uncommitted → auto-cleanup removes the worktree → work permanently destroyed. Isolation plus eager cleanup is worse than no isolation.

**The highest-severity, highest-volume category is the cleanup machinery (STRONG).** Distinct Claude Code failure paths: #46444 (auto-cleanup destroyed 10 days of uncommitted work — 300+ files, 160+ hours), #48927 (parallel subagent cleanup destroyed `.git` and nearly the whole tree), #38287 (silently deletes branches with unmerged commits, recoverable only via `git fsck`), #47074 (deletes uncommitted worktree without confirmation), #56639 (desktop Archive deletes dirty worktree, no check, no dialog), #62431 (`/exit` offers to remove a worktree other live sessions use), #12586 (silently creates worktrees without consent), #26725 (stale worktrees never cleaned — the opposite failure), #45645 (stale `worktreeConfig` breaks other IDE agents), #70451 (docs omit killed-agent lock cleanup). Canonical narrative: an engineer approved a vague "Want me to clean up…?" and the agent force-removed all 29 worktrees, losing WIP on every branch; his conclusions — "I stopped approving agent commands on autopilot" and "Safety requires deliberate friction" (Mergify blog). **Product implication: users are hurt by the isolation system's own lifecycle more than by shared-folder collisions.**

**Semantic conflicts no isolation prevents (MODERATE, rising).** "Agent A renames a type to X. Agent B… independently renames the same type to Y… neither worktree is 'wrong' but the code is incoherent" (HN 47866750). Quantified: cross-agent PRs conflict at 41.7% vs 19.8% intra-agent, 29K+ conflicting-PR dataset (arXiv 2607.04697).

**Dev-server/port collisions (STRONG, universally hand-rolled).** Agents in separate worktrees still both bind 3000/5432/8080. Folklore fix: `BASE_PORT + WORKTREE_INDEX * 10 + OFFSET` or per-worktree `.env` overrides (Upsun, PHP Architect). The one-liner everywhere: worktrees solve filesystem isolation, not runtime isolation.

**Cross-worktree grep bleed (WEAK but concrete).** "grep would yield results from a worktree and the agent would happily start to work in the wrong worktree" (HN comment) — an argument against in-repo `.claude/worktrees/` and for home-rooted directories.

### A2. Current solutions in the wild

- **Tier 0 — "just clone it again":** Simon Willison: "I haven't adopted git worktrees yet: if I want to run two agents in isolation against the same repo I do a fresh checkout, often into /tmp." Higher-status than expected.
- **Tier 1 — worktrees, now native in the agents:** Claude Code `--worktree`/`isolation: worktree`; Copilot CLI as an explicit mode choice; Codex App and Cursor per-thread worktrees.
- **Tier 2 — tmux + worktree + labeling:** dmux, cmux, Claude Squad, CodeAgentSwarm. The insight is tracking, not launching: what plain tmux lacks is "no desktop notification when an agent finishes or asks for an approval, no searchable history across agents, no dynamic titles, no task board" (mattorb).
- **Tier 3 — GUI orchestrators:** Conductor, Nimbalyst, Parallel Code, Pane, Superset, MindFlock. Heavy churn: Crystal deprecated (Feb 2026) → Nimbalyst; Vibe Kanban's vendor shut down (Apr 2026); Terragon dead (Jan 2026). A new Show HN lands every few weeks; the category is crowded and consolidating.
- **Tier 4 — containers/VMs:** Sculptor (Docker per agent), container-use, Docker Sandboxes. Best first-person on why devcontainers weren't enough: Arcjet found them "fundamentally single-instance," rebuilds up to 20 minutes; moved to one OrbStack VM per workspace cloned in 5–10 s with namespaced hostnames (`decide.agent-1.orb.local`) and shared DBs running once.
- **Tier 5 — cloud:** Cursor background agents, Codex Cloud, Copilot coding agent.
- **Tier 6 — refuse to parallelize / coordinate instead** (see A4).

**Typical power-user setup, 2026 (synthesized):** one worktree per _task_ outside the repo; a create-hook copying `.env*` and installing deps; a port block per worktree injected as env; labeled tmux windows or GUI panes; **2–4 agents, not 10**; one PR per worktree; manual deliberate cleanup.

### A3. Hidden costs after adopting worktrees (STRONG overall)

- **The gitignored tail** — the single most-repeated complaint: `node_modules`, `.env`, `dist`, `.venv`, caches don't come along. Copying `.env` is "the #1 missing piece most tutorials ignore"; a dedicated tool exists just for it (therohitdas/copy-env). Mitigation folklore: pnpm's content-addressable store makes per-worktree `node_modules` cheap.
- **Disk:** 46 stale agent worktrees ≈ 10.93 GB reclaimed; 256 worktrees / 28 GB / 700+ stale branches; "10+ GB in an afternoon" (all secondary; order-of-magnitude consistent).
- **Stale buildup as a ritual chore;** dedicated bulk-prune posts and skills; Claude Code v2.1.187 fixed leaked registrations from killed agents.
- **Branch-name friction:** "Can't check out the same branch twice, so you're making up branch names to keep git happy" (HN).
- **Shared `.git` is a security surface:** an agent can be induced to drop files into the `.git` it can already write.
- **Database/migration state — the quit-reason:** "the worktree dance is fine, real blocker for us was test data isolation… reasoning about which agent broke the shared migration when three of them touch it got old fast" (HN, kippinsula); another ended up with "a bunch of separate dbs and services" and unresolved docker port conflicts.
- **"Which terminal is which agent":** "You forget which terminal is working on which branch… Layout is not workflow" (johannesjo).
- **Merge/integration:** beyond the 41.7% conflict rate, most tools "lack user-friendly merge workflows and require strong git command-line skills" (HN); "More time spent reconciling branches after the work was 'done'" (Wells).
- **Review tooling gap:** "What are parallel agents worth to professional engineers if reviewing the code is a pain (aka non existent) in Zed?"

### A4. The skeptic take — mainstream and bounded

Willison's canonical objection (review is the bottleneck) and his partial recantation ("an increasing number of tasks that can still be fired off in parallel without adding too much cognitive overhead"). Osmani: "Your cognitive bandwidth doesn't parallelize… More agents running doesn't mean more of _you_ available… You can supervise more agents than you can deeply understand"; names the "ambient anxiety tax"; ceiling "three to four threads." The tried-and-abandoned cohort: prmph (built parallel tooling, now caps at 3–4: "I don't think I'm missing anything"); Wells ("I did not need more git ops. I needed better coordination"). Sharpest risk formulation: "the more parallel agents you spawn the more the probability of you vibe coding rather than guide coding." **The actionable reframe (recurs independently):** "The real bottleneck isn't human review per se, it's _unstructured_ review. Parallel agents only make sense if each worktree has a tight contract: scoped task, invariant tests, and a diff small enough to audit quickly." Convergent number: **three** (attention limit, multiple independent authors). "Parallelism theater" is not a live meme; the sentiment is, the label isn't.

### A5. What "ideal" looks like (explicit asks)

1. Isolation automatic and invisible — "Each AI session should behave like an isolated feature branch — automatically… Its own workspace, its own git worktree, clear visual boundaries, easy creation and disposal." Success metric is psychological: **"I hesitate less before spawning a new agent."**
2. Environment cloning including _services_ — "local config files copied over and Postgres duplicating my local dev and test databases"; the Phoenix/Conductor case study: per-workspace `.env.local` with `COMPOSE_PROJECT_NAME`, own Postgres on its own port, all Docker resources namespaced.
3. Ports assigned, not negotiated (Conductor's `CONDUCTOR_PORT` cited as what others lack).
4. Fast creation (Arcjet's 20 min → 5–10 s is the quoted benchmark; speed turns isolation from a decision into a default).
5. One surface for every agent's diff — "from a pile of terminal tabs into a single control surface."
6. Cleanup that cannot eat work — dirty checks, confirmations, never implicit `--force`, show-the-list, pinning; "adding roughly ten seconds per action but preventing catastrophic data loss."
7. Receipts and reversibility over speed — worktree isolation, auditability, rollback as baseline, memory "inspectable, scoped, and removable," provider agnosticism; fear of "another layer of state" unless the workspace makes agent work **supervisable**.
8. Per-agent preview URL + isolated DB with seed data + automatic teardown (imported from the preview-environment world).

### A6. Naming / mental model

"Worktree" is the practitioner default for the mechanism (used bare and unglossed across HN); plain nouns ("a fresh checkout," "multiple copies") when explaining the concept; "sandbox"/"VM" in cloud/container contexts. Tool split: worktree (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex App) / workspace (Conductor) / both-as-opposites (Copilot CLI: workspace = shared desk = _no_ isolation; worktree = private office) / container-sandbox (Sculptor, container-use) / thread (Zed) / attempt (vibe-kanban) / environment-sandbox (cloud vendors). **Copilot's inversion is the most load-bearing finding:** if DorkOS says "workspace" and the user's other tool is Copilot CLI, the word means the opposite. Generic overload (Slack/VS Code/SaaS) is documented structurally, though no direct "I was confused because Slack" quote was found (that specific claim: WEAK). "Worktree" carries reverse baggage: obscure until agents mainstreamed it, plus its own confusions (same-branch-twice, stale metadata). Practical read: name the _unit of work_, describe isolation in outcome terms; both most-read authors fell back to plain language.

### A7. Adjacent/emerging — transitional or durable?

**The location is transitional; the primitive is durable.** Neon: >80% of databases provisioned on Neon are now created by AI agents; agent pattern = short-lived TTL branches; June 2026 preview extends branching to functions/storage/gateway — exactly the gap practitioners quit over. Preview environments map 1:1 onto agent needs (isolated DB, seed data, shareable URL, invisible teardown), now exposed over MCP. Cloud-first agents are not obviously winning (Terragon dead, vibe-kanban cloud off) while sandbox infra booms — picks-and-shovels for platforms, not evidence the inner loop left the laptop. Synthesis: local and cloud converge on the same shape ("start from a structured task; run isolated; track; validate; PR; receipt"); local keeps isolation **controllable by the developer**. The right architecture is a workspace-provider seam where local-worktree, container, VM, and cloud sandbox are interchangeable backends.

### A8. Corroboration of prior DorkOS research

- "Port isolation is an unsolved gap DorkOS can own" — **confirmed, STRONG** (Conductor still alone).
- "Cleanup is the industry's biggest failure mode — be conservative" — **massively confirmed** (10+ issues + the 29-worktree incident).
- New since June: worktrees do not eliminate git lock contention; lock failure + eager cleanup is a documented work-destruction path.

## Appendix B — Competitor product/UX survey (full agent report, 2026-08-09)

> Agent-produced; 24 searches + 14 fetches, first-party docs and issue trackers weighted. Delta since `research/20260611`: Conductor Cloud + `.worktreeinclude`, Codex docs moved to learn.chatgpt.com with an explicit environment picker, Claude Code v2.1.198–212 worktree hardening, Zed Parallel Agents, Block's Buzz, three market exits.

### B1. Conductor (conductor.build, Melty Labs / YC S24)

**Noun:** **"Workspace"** — "An isolated workspace is a separate, git-backed copy of a project for one stream of work"; `git worktree` demoted to an implementation page. Auto-generated city-style names (`warsaw-v2`). **Unit:** 1 workspace = 1 branch ("a branch can only be checked out in one workspace at a time"); above it, task/issue ("Create workspace from issue," 0.66.0); below it, many chats per workspace — the workspace outlives any conversation. **Automatic vs manual:** creation automatic (branch cut from origin/main, no re-clone — shared Git backing); **no setup wizard** — repo owner hand-authors scripts; config layered: `.conductor/settings.toml` (shared) / `.local.toml` (machine) / `~/.conductor/settings.toml` (user). **Provisioning:** `setup`/`run`/`archive` scripts; env: `CONDUCTOR_WORKSPACE_PATH`, `CONDUCTOR_ROOT_PATH`, **`CONDUCTOR_PORT`** ("so multiple Conductor workspaces can run dev servers") — still the only local tool shipping port allocation. New: adopted **`.worktreeinclude`** (same filename as Claude Code, gitignore syntax, "does not make a file tracked. It only selects files that Git already ignores"). **Cloud (0.78.0, 2026-07-30):** "Cloud Computer" — org-shared snapshot builds ("a successful build automatically becomes active… new workspaces start from it"), up to 50 repos, preloaded toolchain, secrets unreadable by agents, isolated microVMs (8 vCPU/16 GB/32 GB) on Vercel Sandbox. **Cleanup:** Archive deletes the directory after the `archive` script (teardown of out-of-workspace resources); restorable from History; **Checkpoints** — per-turn snapshots "stored locally, separate from your working branch's Git history," framed as "a trust feature that make experimentation less scary." No documented cap. **Review/merge — the strongest loop in the field:** Diff Viewer → inline comments _sent back to the agent_ → a Review action (agent inspects its own diff) → **Checks tab** ("git status, PR metadata, CI checks, deployments, and unresolved comments… treat as blockers") → Create PR (agent-drafted description) → merge → archive. **Spotlight testing** checkpoints the workspace and checks it out at the repo root for stacks that only run there. **Praise:** "a clean GUI extension for Claude Code," "does one thing well," near-daily updates, no model lock-in. **Complaints → fixed:** GitHub over-permission backlash (fine-grained within days); re-clone model (gone); slowness (June 2026 rewrite: Bun agent processes, virtualized chat, "50% faster"). **Still open:** setup burden ("every new workspace needs to be bootstrapped"; troubleshooting page is a catalogue of env/port/db failures), Mac-only, "You're still the quality gate," "Token costs multiply," guidance of 3–5 parallel workspaces. **Pricing:** isolation IS the headline. Free local; Pro $50/mo (Cloud, Multiplayer, API, mobile); Teams $60/user/mo; Enterprise. Privacy pitch: local session data never leaves the device.

### B2. Claude Code (CLI + Desktop)

**Noun:** "worktree," no rebrand; `.claude/worktrees/<name>/`, branch `worktree-<name>`, generated names like `bright-running-fox`. **Unit — three at once (a confusion source):** CLI session (opt-in `--worktree`/`-w`), Desktop session (**forced, no opt-out**), subagent (`isolation: worktree`, only these are sweep-eligible); plus PR-as-unit (`--worktree "#1234"` fetches the PR head). **Automatic vs manual:** CLI opt-in; Desktop automatic; mid-session via `EnterWorktree` (since v2.1.206, entering a path outside `.claude/worktrees/` requires explicit approval that permission rules can't suppress). Config thin: `.worktreeinclude` + `worktree.baseRef`. **Provisioning — weakest of the big four:** "ask Claude to install dependencies, or run your project's setup yourself"; `.worktreeinclude` for ignored-file copying; `WorktreeCreate` hook replaces default logic entirely (and disables `.worktreeinclude`). No ports, no DB story, no install. Since v2.1.211 permission approvals save to the main checkout and survive worktree removal. **Cleanup — most conservative defaults, materially hardened through 2026:** exit-time dirty prompt; periodic sweep (`cleanupPeriodDays`) touches only subagent/background worktrees and "skips a worktree that still holds work… never removes worktrees you create with `--worktree`"; run-locks (v2.1.210 releases locks from killed sessions); symlink-escape refusals (v2.1.212). Historical: #46444. **Review/merge:** Desktop-only but complete — visual diff with comments, PR via gh, **PR monitoring with Auto-fix/Auto-merge (squash)**, auto-archive on merge/close. **Praise:** strictest isolation enforcement anywhere (blocks edits/Bash/git aimed at the main checkout, including via `git -C`/`GIT_DIR`/`cd`, for all subagents). **Complaint — loudest in the corpus:** forced Desktop worktrees, an eight-issue duplicate chain over five months (#27971→#60113): silent divergence between worktree and repo, "duplicate file copies can drift," desktop vs VS Code holding different working copies; asks are a tri-state Always/Never/Ask, `"worktreeIsolation": false`, or `--no-worktree`, plus dependency-directory reuse. Other asks: lifecycle/reuse across sessions (#43730), configurable location (#28242). **Positioning:** pure plumbing — "Session isolation | `--worktree` flag | Automatic worktrees" is one row in a feature table.

### B3. OpenAI Codex

**Noun:** **"environments"** — a first-class picker in the chat composer: **Local / Worktree / Cloud**. Supporting: "Codex-managed worktrees," "permanent worktrees" (user-created, cleanup-exempt), "local environments" (setup config), **"Handoff"** ("safely moves chats between Local and Worktree environments"). Docs migrated to learn.chatgpt.com. **Unit:** the chat/thread; worktree at `$CODEX_HOME/worktrees` (configurable); pinned chats protect their worktree. **Automatic vs manual:** semi-manual by design — pick Worktree in the composer, choose a base branch, submit; setup scripts then run automatically. Environment configured in a **GUI settings pane that persists to a committable `.codex/` repo file** — the closest thing to a setup wizard in the field. **Provisioning:** arbitrary setup scripts with OS variants; docs state the problem plainly ("might be missing dependencies or files that aren't checked into your repository"). No port allocator. **Cleanup — best-in-class on paper:** keeps the 15 most recent Codex-managed worktrees (configurable/disable-able); **snapshot before delete; reopening a deleted chat offers restoration**; skips pinned/in-progress/permanent. The only product with **cap + snapshot + pin + restore** together. **Review/merge:** "Create branch here" (worktree → branch → normal PR flow) or Handoff back to Local for IDE inspection; no in-app merge. **Complaints:** #24703 — cleanup `git worktree remove --force`d another live agent's worktree ("in multi-agent local development, worktree ownership is shared operational state" — local git/PR state is not a liveness signal); #12685 — permanent-worktree button skips env setup and leaks; #11181 — CLI parity gap. **Positioning:** plumbing in ChatGPT plans, but the presentation (isolation as a dropdown next to the prompt, with an explicit handoff verb) is a product decision worth stealing. **Symphony:** no substantive July/Aug update; model unchanged (workspace per tracker issue, hook-populated, removed on terminal state). Linear reported a workspace spike post-release; "500% more landed PRs in three weeks among some OpenAI teams" is vendor-sourced.

### B4. Buzz (Block / Jack Dorsey) — identification and why it matters

**Identification:** **Buzz by Block**, launched 2026-07-21, Apache-2.0, self-hostable, built on Nostr; ~17k GitHub stars in week one. "A workspace where humans and agents build together, on a relay you own." Agent harnesses attach via the Agent Client Protocol: Goose, **Codex, and Claude Code**. (Other "Buzz" products exist; none are coding-agent managers.) **The finding: Buzz does no working-copy isolation at all.** Its isolation axis is **identity**: "Agents have their own keys, their own channel memberships, and their own audit trail. Scoped by identity, not by permission flags." Units are channels/threads; the signature move is **a feature branch is a channel** — patches land as signed NIP-34 events, CI posts into the room, an agent runs first-pass review, "and the merge decision happens in the same place as all the evidence." Ships a **built-in git forge over Smart HTTP** (repos beside the discussion, review in-channel, no GitHub required). Community-scoped multi-tenancy verified in TLA+/Tamarin. No provisioning, no retention story, no ports; isolation mentioned only incidentally ("the agent works in an isolated branch or worktree"). **Praise:** the membership model — cryptographic chain-of-custody linking every agent action to the authorizing human; self-hosting; no lock-in. Cautioned as fast-moving. **Why it matters:** the closest thing in market to the DorkOS team-room thesis, from a well-resourced player — and it deliberately skipped the working-copy problem. Simultaneously the competitive threat (social + forge + provenance) and the open flank (no workspace lifecycle). The defensible DorkOS claim is the composition: team room **plus** runtime-agnostic workspace lifecycle.

### B5. Cursor

**Noun:** "worktrees" local ("Agent work in isolated Git checkouts… your main checkout stays untouched"), "cloud agents"/"cloud subagents" remote. **Unit:** agent/task in the Agents Window; `/worktree`, `/best-of-n` (N parallel attempts), `/apply-worktree`, `/in-cloud`. **Config:** repo-owned `.cursor/worktrees.json` + `.cursor/environment.json` (cloud). **Provisioning:** `setup-worktree[-unix|-windows]` command arrays with `$ROOT_WORKTREE_PATH` (canonical example: `cp $ROOT_WORKTREE_PATH/.env .env`); vendor anti-pattern warning against symlinking dependencies. No ports. **Cleanup — the field's cautionary tale:** machine-wide `worktreeMaxCount` 25, sweep every 6 h, newest-first — and manually-created `git worktree add` trees are **also eligible**. Incidents: WorktreeManager force-deleted pre-existing user branches (`git branch --contains` + force-delete); archiving an agent deletes its worktree within ~30 s even while open as a workspace folder; a Windows indexing incident left a repository apparently deleted with survivors under Cursor-managed worktrees. **Review/merge:** commit/push from the worktree, or **`/apply-worktree`** — the only mainstream first-class "fold this isolated copy back into my working tree without a PR" verb. **Praise:** `/apply-worktree`, `/best-of-n`, committed cloud env. **Complaints:** the deletions; can't attach an agent to a pre-existing worktree; and simultaneously a request for _more_ cleanup on archive — **the reconciliation: cleanup must be bound to a user-authored terminal state, never a heuristic.** **Positioning:** plumbing; cloud agents are the headline.

### B7. Cross-cutting findings

**B7.1 The "just works" consensus stack (converged since June; two parts now share filenames across vendors):**

1. Worktrees beat clones — argument over (Conductor abandoned re-cloning; only container/cloud tools legitimately clone).
2. **`.worktreeinclude`** — declarative, repo-committed, gitignore-syntax list of ignored files to copy; Claude Code and Conductor share the exact filename and the only-already-ignored invariant. The closest thing to a de facto standard in the space.
3. A repo-owned, version-controlled setup script run automatically on create (`.conductor/settings.toml`, `.cursor/worktrees.json`, GUI-authored `.codex/`, Symphony `after_create`). Codex's settings-pane-persists-to-committable-file is the friendliest instance.
4. Per-workspace port allocation injected as env — still essentially only Conductor.
5. Retention = cap + dirty-check + pin + snapshot (Codex is the reference; Cursor has the cap without the protections and pays in forum threads).
6. **Review is the product:** in-app diff → comment-back-to-the-agent → checks → PR → archive (Conductor, Vibe Kanban, Claude Desktop, Nimbalyst all converged; Conductor's Checks tab is the most complete).

**B7.2 Best-reviewed UX: Conductor**, by a clear margin (whole loop in one window; ports and out-of-workspace teardown solved; checkpoints as a trust feature; scope discipline and responsiveness; no model lock-in) — with **Codex best on retention safety** and **Sculptor best on "let me touch the running code."** Counterweights: Mac-only, was slow pre-rewrite, setup script still hand-written.

**B7.3 Still broken everywhere:** (1) databases/migrations — nobody's answer, DIY Neon/SQLite recipes only; (2) ports (Conductor aside); (3) local config/services never come along — `.worktreeinclude` fixes static files, not stateful services; (4) disk/dependency duplication; (5) cleanup that eats work in every product that automates it — no cross-tool ownership/lease protocol exists (Claude Code's `git worktree lock` is the only mechanism, and only within itself); (6) forced isolation with no opt-out; (7) the semantic-conflict problem nobody attempts ("that's where time savings from parallelization go to die"); (8) review capacity as the real ceiling (3–5 caps everywhere); (9) business-model gravity — three exits in seven months; isolation must be a capability inside a larger control plane, not the business.

**B7.4 Implications for DorkOS:** June architecture holds and is more validated (home-rooted, unit-of-work-keyed, hook-provisioned, conservatively cleaned). Adopt `.worktreeinclude` verbatim. Ports remain the differentiator and DorkOS is the only player with a persistent server to allocate them. Copy Codex's retention triad and Conductor's archive script; make isolation opt-in per unit of work (Zed). Server-side ownership leases beat git-state heuristics — exactly the multi-agent multi-runtime case DorkOS lives in. Buzz contests the team-room layer but skipped workspaces entirely: the defensible claim is the composition.
