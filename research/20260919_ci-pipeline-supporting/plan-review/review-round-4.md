Reviewer: Vesper. Scope: §4.9, phase 1b, and `research/20260919_ci-pipeline-supporting/05-queue-emergency-mechanics.md`. Verified this round, read-only:

- The queue is empty and one PR is open right now; the auto-merge, enqueue and merge actors on #1914 are all `doriancollier`, so the PAT's actions are indistinguishable from an agent's.

Verdict: §4.9 gets the **fix** to `main` in minutes. It does not get the **queue** unblocked in minutes, and the plan's own SLO (`unblock-time`) measures the wrong thing. Under full autonomy the break-glass fence is client-side and manufacturable. The section needs five changes before it is safe to build; the rest are cuts.

## 2. Break-glass under full autonomy (question 2)

**[blocker] The fence is client-side, and every agent already holds the credential.** "Break-glass runs only where admin exists, which is the operator's machine" is true and useless: every agent runs on that machine as that account. The preconditions are enforced by `/ci:break-glass` and a PreToolUse guard. The guard family parses `sh -c`, `$(…)` and `eval` to depth 2 (`git-guard.mjs`); a script file, `node -e`, or a Python one-liner walks past it, and the memory notes already record these guards false-positive on prose and are aimed at well-meaning agents, not adversarial ones. So the five "all mechanical" preconditions are mechanical for an agent that calls `/ci:break-glass` and advisory for one that calls `gh api -X PUT .../merge`. This is not worse than today (the bypass is `always`, so an agent could push to `main` right now), but the plan claims a fence it does not have.

What is actually server-observable and therefore enforceable after the fact:

- a commit on `main` with no merge-queue provenance (the `MergedEvent` actor and the absence of an `AddedToMergeQueueEvent`), which the daily collector can detect and the sentinel can treat as an unsanctioned break-glass: open the breaker, dispatch the post-merge suite, and revert if anything is red or if the ledger entry is missing;
- the break-glass counter derived from `main`'s history (commits merged outside the queue in 24 h), never from the data branch, which anything can write;
- `bypass_mode: pull_request` so the direct-push hole closes for free (research Q2, [R] recommendation; the releases land through the queue now).

Write the fence as: the guard and the command are the paved road; the collector's out-of-queue-merge detector plus auto-revert is the fence; the counter lives in git history. Do not call it "all mechanical".

**[blocker] Break-glass may edit the judge.** Precondition 2's allowed paths include `packages/ci-steward`, `ci/`, "test configuration" and every script a gate invokes. That is the census, `ratchet-assert`, the sentinel, `ci/ratchets.yaml`, `ci/required-checks.json`, `ci/slos.yaml`, vitest and Playwright configs (`testIgnore`, `grepInvert`, `retries`, `passWithNoTests`). §4.7's fence covers only `ci-improve/*` branches; a normal branch under RED is unfenced, unreviewed and unqueued. An agent, or a bug, can break-glass `retries: 5` or a `testIgnore` glob and nothing in the "post-merge full suite" objects, because those changes make the suite greener.

Fix, same rule as §4.7: **break-glass may change gates, never the judge.** Exclude `packages/ci-steward/**`, the `ci/` policy files, the assert scripts and test configuration from the break-glass path set. Two escapes for when the judge itself is what broke the queue: (a) a **pure revert** of the last commit touching those paths, verified mechanically (`git revert` reproduces the diff byte for byte); (b) an expiring `continue-on-error` allowlist entry (≤ 48 h) on the steward step in `typecheck.yml`, which the census already models. Everything else waits for L2.

**[major] RED is manufacturable.** The systemic signal is "the same required job fails in 2+ merge groups with disjoint PR sets within 60 min". An agent with two PRs that each fail the same job (one deliberately failing test) produces it, and it is the same agent that wants the break-glass. The `backlog` signal is manufacturable by opening PRs. The signals an agent cannot produce without a merge are **the targeted canary red on `main` HEAD** and **runner starvation**. Rule: ejection patterns and backlog reach AMBER and trigger the canary; only canary-red, starvation and red `main` open RED. And L3 is available only under canary-red or starvation where the diff touches the failing job's gate; under stall or backlog RED the lane is L2. Otherwise "queue is slow" (which is often) unlocks an unreviewed merge of any pipeline diff.

**[major] "Red on something the fix touched" is the wrong revert rule.** Attribution to "touched" is fuzzy and a fix that did not fix leaves `main` broken with an unreviewed change on it. Rule: the post-merge suite (the first wave's build) must be fully green or the break-glass is reverted. And the revert must be a mechanically generated inverse (diff equality checked), or "revert" becomes a label an agent applies to any diff to claim the always-allowed path.

**[minor] `field-changes` at break-glass.** Precondition 4 requires a ledger entry with a hypothesis; it must also require the `field-changes` block when the generated atlas diff shows retry, shard, timeout or required changes, and the verification set must run the atlas diff against `atlas.generated.json` on the data branch, because the PR-only coverage step never runs on a direct merge.

**[minor] The replacement token may still bypass.** Ruleset bypass is by actor role. A fine-grained PAT of the admin account without Administration probably cannot edit the ruleset, but whether the REST merge endpoint honours the bypass for that token is unverified. Phase 0's exit gate should include: a REST merge of a blocked PR with the new token is refused (403 or 405), measured, not assumed. A GitHub App with its own identity is the cleaner answer and removes the PAT-versus-agent ambiguity on every timeline event.

## 3. The sentinel's own failure modes (question 3)

**[major] False RED is expensive and the stall signal produces it on ordinary bad days.** "Non-empty queue and nothing merged for 90 min" is one XL PR at 55 min plus one ejection and rebuild. A 5-batch whose head fails at minute 50 and rebuilds is 100 min with nothing merged. Every false RED dequeues everyone (in-flight builds lost), cancels runs and re-drains in waves: about one queue cycle for every open PR, ~35-55 min × 20 PRs of lead time. Fix: stall and backlog are **AMBER-only**, and AMBER's automatic action is "stop arming new PRs" (cheap, reversible), never dequeue. Dequeue only on RED, and RED only on the unmanufacturable signals.

**Flapping.** Add hysteresis: RED → RECOVERING needs the fix merged; RECOVERING → GREEN needs wave 1 merged; re-opening needs a fresh canary-red with a 30-minute cooldown; more than 3 REDs in 24 h holds AMBER and notifies the operator instead of cycling.

**Two sentinels.** Same inputs, different times. Make every transition carry the observation timestamp and the inputs it was computed from; on a fetch-rebase conflict, re-evaluate rather than overwrite; a transition with an older observation than the current state is a no-op. Give **destructive actions one owner**: the local sentinel when its heartbeat is under 15 min old (it has admin anyway), the Actions sentinel otherwise. Idempotent re-arming is fine from both.

**Local sentinel asleep.** Heartbeat in `queue-state.json`. The Actions sentinel can freeze and shed with the replacement token (pull-requests and actions write); it cannot jump, edit the ruleset or break-glass. So an asleep machine degrades to L0 + L1, and the plan should say so. Note also that the Actions tick is `merge-tail.yml`, whose scheduled runs failed 40% of the time over 30 days (0% in 7); the dead-man's switch for the sentinel is the same `[Harness]` line as the collector's, but during an incident SessionStart is not where anyone is looking. Push notification on RED should be default-on, not "optionally".

**PreToolUse guard and the fetch.** Do not fetch in a PreToolUse hook. The local sentinel runs every 5 minutes on the same machine; the guard reads its last-written local state file (age-checked) and fails **open** with a warning if the file is older than 15 minutes, because arming into a broken queue costs an ejection, not data. The server-side half is `should-arm-automerge.sh` skipping `ci-hold`, which binds only merge-tail-armed PRs; for self-armed PRs the label is advisory until the guard sees it.

**Data-branch races.** Fetch-rebase-retry is enough for two writers on a small file. But `queue-state.json` now holds state that gates a PreToolUse guard and the watchers; keep it separate from `latest.json` so a collector push cannot clobber an incident transition.

## 4. Quarantine as a no-PR lever (question 4)

**[major] The bound is not a fence; the entry condition is.** Anything that can push the data branch can quarantine, and every agent can push. "10 entries, 7-day expiry" bounds the blast radius but not the pattern: re-add after expiry is a permanent disable of up to 10 tests. §4.9 rejects a gate switch on exactly this argument and then accepts quarantine on a weaker one. Fix:

- an entry is valid only for a test the collector has **classified flaky from data** (failed then passed on the same merge-group SHA at least twice in 14 days, from the flake reporter and Playwright retries already on the branch); `ratchet-assert` and the queue jobs ignore unclassified entries;
- a second quarantine of the same test after expiry needs a ledger entry with a `ratchet-release`;
- quarantined counts are a separate ratchet (size ≤ 10, days-in-quarantine per test), reported in `/ci:status` and the weekly report;
- under RED a non-classified quarantine is allowed only for the job the canary showed red, and expires when the breaker closes.

**[minor] Mechanism.** Vitest has no exclude-by-test-name; quarantine is **file-level** for vitest (`exclude` glob) and test-level for Playwright (`grepInvert`). Say so, and note that a file-level quarantine of a shared fixture file can silently drop a package's whole suite, which `assert-shard-union.sh` would then flag as zero collected files: the quarantine step must run after that assertion is adjusted, or the lever ejects its own build.

## 5. Ordering (question 5)

1b before 2 is right: the fail-closed gates add stall modes and the breaker should exist first. Dependencies inside 1b that the table does not name:

- **The main canary needs a dispatch leg in every required workflow**, or the targeted-job version needs one per job. None exists (`on:` verified). That is five pipeline PRs, each with a ledger entry, and the largest single deliverable in 1b. Either list it or adopt the "first held PR is the canary" design and drop the dispatch.
- The systemic signal needs per-job failure data per merge group: phase 1's `collect` via `check-runs`. Fine, but the sentinel runs every 5-10 min and the collector daily; the sentinel must query `check-runs` itself for the last hour, not read the daily snapshot.
- Quarantine's "counted in the ratchet" is phase 2; in 1b quarantined tests simply reduce counts with no ratchet to offend. Say that 1b's quarantine is unbounded by ratchets until phase 2, or ship the classification-from-data rule in 1b so the bound exists from day one.
- The exit gate "one week of detect-only with no false RED" is right; add "replayed W36 (the 56%-failure week) produces at most N REDs and no RED from stall alone", since that is the week that would have flapped.

## 6. Simpler (question 6)

- **Drop jump.** Under RED the queue is empty (everything was dequeued), so the priority PR is first by construction; under AMBER a jump destroys up to five healthy groups' progress (~5 × 35 min of runner time, research Q1 [V]) to save the fix one cycle, on a permission GitHub does not document for Team and a run-cancellation behaviour that is a research gap. L2 becomes: `ci-priority` exempts the PR from freeze, the sentinel re-arms it first. No admin needed. That also removes the last reason the local sentinel needs admin for anything but break-glass.
- **Drop ruleset edits as a lever** in v1. Research Q5: the effect on in-flight groups is undocumented and the note says not to use it before observing it once. `check_response_timeout` and `max_entries_to_merge: 1` are tuning, not incident response.
- **Drop the separate main-canary dispatch** (section 1). The first re-queued PR is the canary.
- **Drop the 2-5-all waves** for 2-then-all.
- Keep L0 (freeze, shed, stand watchers down) and L3 (fenced as in section 2). L1 shrinks to quarantine-from-data plus re-run infra failures. That is most of the value with one admin primitive instead of three.

## 7. Watchers and self-arming agents (question 7)

- **HELD must outrank EJECTED and FAILING in `classify()`.** Today's precedence is `EJECTED > CONFLICTING > FAILING > STUCK_UNMERGEABLE`; under RED a held PR is also EJECTED (it was dequeued) and possibly FAILING (the systemic red), and both remedies ("re-arm", "push an empty commit") are exactly the load the freeze exists to stop. The watcher must read the local sentinel state file, not fetch.
- **Twenty watchers are their own load.** At a 60 s interval, 20 watchers make ~2,400 calls an hour on the operator's token plus GraphQL points, beside the sentinel and the agents. Under incident, HELD should back the interval off to 5 minutes; silence is documented as "same state", so nothing is lost.
- **Self-arming after recovery breaks the waves.** When the guard sees GREEN, every agent that was told "re-armed automatically when it closes" may also re-arm itself, and 20 PRs enter the queue at once. The `ci-hold` label must be sentinel-owned: the guard refuses `--auto` on a labelled PR regardless of state, and the sentinel removes labels wave by wave. The sentinel must only ever remove labels it added (record the set in the incident record), so a human `hold` is never lifted by the machine.
- **`should-arm-automerge.sh` skipping `ci-hold`** binds merge-tail only; that is fine as the server-side half, but say that self-armed PRs are governed by the label-plus-guard pair, which is client-side, and that the collector reports any PR that entered the queue while labelled.

## Sign-off conditions for §4.9

1. **The fence is stated honestly and has a server-side half**: out-of-queue merges detected by the collector, post-merge full green or mechanical revert, the 2/24 h counter derived from `main`'s history, `bypass_mode` narrowed to `pull_request`. The guard and `/ci:break-glass` are described as the paved road, not the fence.
2. **Break-glass may change gates, never the judge**: `packages/ci-steward/**`, the `ci/` policy files, the assert scripts and test configuration excluded, with the pure-revert and expiring-allowlist escapes.
3. **RED only on unmanufacturable signals** (targeted canary red on `main` HEAD, runner starvation, red `main`); stall and backlog are AMBER with "stop arming" as the only automatic action; L3 available only under canary-red or starvation for the failing job's gate.
4. **Quarantine requires classification from data**, re-quarantine needs a release, size and age are a ratchet from 1b, and the vitest file-level limitation is stated.
5. **The timeline is in the plan** with the two SLOs split (`unblock-time`, `drain-time`), the targeted canary at first ejection, merge_group runs cancelled at freeze, the first re-queued PR as the post-merge canary, and 2-then-all waves. `detect-time` is measured from onset.
6. **Jump, ruleset edits and the separate canary dispatch are cut**, or each is listed in 1b with its workflow changes and a measured answer to the research gaps it depends on.
7. **Watchers**: HELD outranks EJECTED/FAILING, reads local state, backs off under incident; `ci-hold` is sentinel-owned and guard-enforced for self-armed PRs.
8. **Phase 0 exit gate** includes the measured refusal of a REST merge with the replacement token.

Items 1 to 4 are blockers for building 1b; 5 to 8 I would accept as edits to the phase-1b row and §4.9 text.
