# 04. Are we tracking pipeline changes, and their results?

Investigated 2026-09-19, read-only. Repo `dork-labs/dorkos`.

**Question.** Are we tracking the changes we make to the development lifecycle, CI and deployment pipeline? Do we track the results of those changes (did they help)?

**Short answer.**

- **Tracking the change:** yes, and in unusual depth. The records are scattered across six places, though, and nothing ties them into one ledger.
- **Tracking the result:** almost never on purpose.
  - Before merge, the evidence is excellent: before/after timings, mutation tests, run-ID samples.
  - After rollout, no change in the last 90 days had a planned "did it help" check that anyone actually ran.
  - When a prediction did get checked, it was by accident: a later incident re-measured the same thing from scratch. In three cases the reality was worse than the prediction and nobody noticed for 1 to 2 weeks.

## Method and caveats

- **Shallow clone.** The local clone is shallow and starts at 2026-07-29 (`git rev-parse --is-shallow-repository` returns `true`). I did not unshallow it, because the object store is shared.
  - Earlier history comes from the GitHub API: `gh pr list --state merged` returned 1,901 merged PRs, the first merged 2026-06-08 (`#1`).
- **PR bodies.** Pulled for 123 pipeline-relevant PRs (`gh pr view --json body`) and read the load-bearing ones in full.
- **Linear.** Queried read-only through `composio execute LINEAR_RUN_QUERY_OR_MUTATION --account personal`. This was GraphQL queries only, no mutations.
  - A title-keyword search of team DOR returned 434 issues. About 130 of them are about the pipeline.
  - I also read the comments on DOR-1818, DOR-1007, DOR-1701, DOR-1363, DOR-1839 and DOR-2022.
- **Other sources.** Also read:
  - ADRs, specs and `research/`.
  - The AGENTS.md CI section.
  - Workflow headers.
  - The CI-related auto-memory files.
- **Spot-check.** One spot-check pulled `merge_group` run conclusions from the Actions API, to show that the outcome data exists (see "Spot-check").

"Pipeline change" here means: workflows, lefthook hooks, Claude hooks, turbo/vitest config that changes how gates run, merge-queue/ruleset and merge-tail behaviour, the automated reviewer, Dependabot/CodeQL, release workflows, and the scripts those gates execute. Pure test-flake fixes are counted separately.

## Legend for the table

- **Hyp** (hypothesis stated):
  - `Q`: a quantified prediction ("~26 min to ~10 min").
  - `P`: a qualitative or projected claim.
  - `N`: none.
- **Outcome measured:**
  - `Pre`: before/after evidence gathered before merge (local or sampled runs). This proves the fix works; it does not show the effect in production.
  - `Inc`: the effect was measured after rollout, but incidentally, by later unrelated work.
  - `Post`: a deliberate post-rollout check. **Zero rows earn this.**
  - `N`: nothing found.
- **Incident?** `Y` if the PR responds to a specific observed failure (red main, stuck queue, killed run, leaked work, bypassed gate).

- **Last 8 weeks:** about 9 pipeline changes a week.
- **Last 4 weeks:** about 13 a week. The peak was 18 in the week of 2026-08-31, the same week as the 2026-09-05 queue outage.
- **By day:** local git shows up to 7 pipeline-touching commits in a single day (09-02, 09-05, 09-16).

**Churn: the same knob turned repeatedly.**

- **Automated reviewer:** 10 PRs in 10 weeks.
  - PRs: #58, #60, #63, #164, #387, #449, #462, #1410, #1682, #1743.
  - Turn cap went 25, then 40, then 50, then size-scaled 50/100, then decoupled from the check colour.
  - "Green with no verdict" was fixed (#1682), and 1 day later a new way to produce no verdict appeared (#1743).
- **`test.yml` topology:** 6 changes in 6 weeks.
  - Runs on PR (#549, 07-28), then adds merge_group (#581), then merge_group only (#1246, 08-24).
  - That deadlocked the queue, so an affected PR leg was restored the same day.

## 6. Verdict

**Are pipeline changes tracked? Yes: well documented one change at a time, badly consolidated.**

- Every change has an unusually good paper trail: PR body, workflow header, Linear ticket, often mutation-tested guards.
- But the trail is spread across six places. The changelog excludes it, and there is one CI ADR for about 94 changes.
- There is no single ledger answering "what did we change in the pipeline this month, and why".

**Are results tracked? No.**

- The loop that runs is incident, measure the cause, fix, prove it before merge, close the ticket.
- The step "check the effect in production after N days" does not exist as a practice.
  - Zero changes had a post-rollout check that was planned and then done.
  - At least five had a check promised in writing and dropped.
- Where outcomes surfaced, they surfaced by accident, and three of them contradicted the change's premise:
  - **#1246:** saturation relief led to a 56% queue failure rate.
  - **#39:** the retry budget was inert in 19 of 21 projects for 11 weeks.
  - **#1521:** the drift gate missed 4 of 4 breaks.
- The one genuine retrospective (DOR-1818) was closed at ideation, its recommendations parked, and its evidence left to expire.

**Consequences visible in the data:**

- Churn is high: about 13 pipeline changes a week lately, with reversals within 2 weeks (#1301 to #1641; merge-tail permissions).
- The same incident classes recur 3 to 15 times: ceilings, pre-push under load, flakes, vacuous gates.
- Flake tickets are rising month over month.

The team is very good at proving a fix works and has no mechanism for learning whether it helped.

**Cheapest fixes that would close the loop** (suggestions only; nothing written anywhere):

1. **A weekly scheduled job** that pulls the Actions API into a persisted series:
   - queue builds per merged PR;
   - `merge_group` failure rate per required check;
   - p50/p95 job durations against each `timeout-minutes`;
   - queue ejections;
   - named retried tests (the flake reporter output, aggregated).

   Section 5 is that job done once by hand.

2. **Every `ci(` PR states one metric, a predicted value and a check date.** The Linear ticket stays open, or gets a dated follow-up, until the check is posted.
3. **Correct stale header premises when a measurement disproves them.** First candidates: `browser-test.yml:22` and `:205`.
4. **Record the 08-24 restructure as an ADR.** Amend ADR 260728-112203 with measured queue throughput.
5. **Decide DOR-1818's R1/R2** rather than leaving them parked.
