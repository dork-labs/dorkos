# Composio (managed)

Composio is the simple default for connecting a service. It handles the sign-in for you and holds your login in its own secure vault. You give DorkOS one Composio key, and it lets you connect services like Gmail, Slack, and Notion.

<Callout type="warn">
  This path is built and browser-tested against a test provider, but not yet confirmed with a real
  Composio account. It is in alpha. Try it in a safe account first.
</Callout>

## Before you connect: where your login lives

DorkOS shows you this before you connect, and again on each connected account. For Gmail it reads:

> Connecting gmail takes you to that service to sign in. Composio stores your connected accounts' login access in its own secure vault, not on your computer. Your agents can then act for you; your password is never shared, and you can disconnect anytime.

In short: your password never touches DorkOS or Composio. Composio holds a token that lets your agents act, and you can disconnect at any time.

## What DorkOS stores

DorkOS keeps only your Composio key, saved in its encrypted secret store (never plain text on disk, never sent to the browser, never written to a log). It does not store the logins for Gmail, Slack, or any other service you connect. Those live in Composio's vault. That is the whole point of the managed path.

## Set it up

<Steps>

<Step>

### Get a Composio API key

Create an account at Composio and copy a project API key from its dashboard. This key lets DorkOS connect accounts and execute the exact actions you grant through the broker.

A Composio user account key that begins with `uak_` can still manage existing connections, but the SDK cannot provide DorkOS with pinned action schemas for that key type. DorkOS therefore reports action discovery and execution as unavailable instead of sending an unverified action.

</Step>

<Step>

### Give the key to DorkOS

Open **Connections** in the sidebar and scroll to the **Composio & Nango** section. Paste the key into the Composio card and click **Save key**. The card shows **Ready** right away, and the services Composio can reach appear in the grid above. Removing the key later turns the provider off again and stops brokered execution for its accounts.

</Step>

<Step>

### Connect a service

Click **Connect** on a service tile (for example, Gmail). DorkOS shows the custody sentence first, then a button that opens the service's sign-in page in a new tab. You sign in there, not in DorkOS. When the sign-in completes, the account shows up under **Connected accounts**.

</Step>

<Step>

### Connect a second account (optional)

Connect the same service again and give it a label, like "personal". DorkOS suggests one when a first account already exists. Both accounts show up in your list, each with its own label and its own custody line.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Give an agent access

Open the account under **Connections**, choose **Agent access**, and select a named agent. Review the exact actions and versions before saving. DorkOS executes only those pinned actions through its broker. If Composio later reports a changed action or schema, the new version stays off until you review it.

If an account expires, connect it again and review access for the new connection. If you paused it yourself, resume it under **Connections**. DorkOS refuses execution until that owner action is complete.
