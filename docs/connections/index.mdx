# Connections

The **Connections** page is one list of apps. **Yours** shows every app you connected, one row each, with its logo, what state it is in, and the one thing to do next. **All apps** shows everything you could connect, also by logo, so you can find the app you want at a glance.

Apps come in two kinds, and both live in the same list:

- Apps your agents **use**, such as Gmail. You choose an account, a named agent, and the exact actions or notifications it may use.
- **Chat** apps, such as Telegram and Slack, where people reach your agents. They carry a small **Chat** tag. See [Relay Messaging](/docs/guides/relay-messaging).

Click a connected app to open its side panel: who can use it, what agents did with it lately, and a few things to try. Everything else (its name, notifications, exact actions, pause, disconnect) is under **More**.

This guide covers apps your agents use.

<Callout type="warn">
  DorkOS-managed Accounts are available in alpha. A real Gmail sign-in, profile read, usage record,
  and access removal have been verified. This does not certify every service. Managed account
  notifications remain unavailable while their separate live delivery check is incomplete.
</Callout>

## Connect an account in three steps

<Steps>

<Step>

### Choose a service

Open **Connections** and find the app in **All apps**, or search for it. Select **Connect** on its row. Popular apps are always listed, even before anything is set up. An app that does two things, such as Slack, asks which one you want first.

The first time you connect an app, DorkOS asks how it should reach your apps. If a way already works (for example, your DorkOS account is linked in **Settings → DorkOS account**, or you saved your own key), it skips this step and goes straight to sign-in.

Supported services use Composio-managed sign-in by default, without a separate developer setup. An explicit custom setup takes priority when one exists. Services that need unsupported or custom setup stay visible and explain what is missing. When more than one route is ready, you can change the route before continuing.

</Step>

<Step>

### Sign in

Read where the service will keep your login access. Add a label such as “work” if you plan to connect the service more than once. Open the sign-in page and finish the service's flow. Managed OAuth consent may name Composio because Composio holds the connection in its vault. Some services instead ask for account fields on `dorkos.ai`. Our servers pass these details to Composio without saving them, and they never pass through your local installation or an agent. A service that needs no account fields still asks you to confirm. You can close the DorkOS dialog while an external sign-in flow continues.

</Step>

<Step>

### Choose who can use it

Once you're signed in, the same window asks **Who can use it?** Choose **Only agents I pick** and tick the agents you want, or **Every agent**, which includes agents you add later. Then pick **Read**, or **Read and write** where the app offers it (Gmail and Google Calendar today), and save. Before you save, the card tells you if an agent you unticked will lose access, or if every agent could send, change or delete things as you. This works the same for apps connected through your DorkOS account.

You can skip this and come back later: click the app's row in **Yours**, and the same question is at the top of its side panel. For exact actions, open **More** and choose **Exact actions per agent**. To stop sharing with every agent, open **More** and choose **Stop sharing with every agent**. OAuth scopes decide which service actions are available; your choice decides which of those actions an agent may run. Notification access is a separate choice. A level keeps its meaning as the app changes: agents on **Read** get the app's new ways to read, and lose an action that starts changing things, the next time DorkOS reads the app's actions (every 12 hours, right after DorkOS is updated, and whenever you open **Who can use it?**). This works the same for **Every agent**. Exact actions stay as you picked them, and a new or changed action stays off for them until you choose it.

</Step>

</Steps>

You can connect several accounts for one service: each account is its own row. To add another, open the app's side panel, open **More** and choose **Connect another** account (for Gmail, **Connect another Gmail account**). Labels keep a personal account separate from a work account. Access always belongs to the exact connection you selected.

## Agents can ask, but you decide

An agent can ask for a service and explain why it needs access. It asks for one of the same two levels you choose from: **Read** (look at things there) or **Read and write** (also change things there). The request does not reveal your private account list and does not grant anything.

You answer with one card, wherever you meet the request. In the chat or room where the agent asked, the card walks you through signing in (if needed) and then "Let DorkBot use Gmail?" — named for the agent that asked — with the reason it gave, starting on the level it asked for. The card is always about that one agent. If it also wants to know when something new happens (like a new email), the card asks you right after which updates it gets. You can also open **Connections** directly, where every pending request waits in a **Needs you** strip at the top and opens the same card.

An agent keeps one open request per app. Asking again, even with a different reason, doesn't add a second card, and an agent can't open requests faster than you could answer them.

If the request is still attached to a live turn, the agent receives the result there, right on the card. Whatever you answer, the agent is told plainly what happens next, including what you left out, such as "you can read Gmail, but not change anything in it". After a restart, DorkOS can continue the exact recorded session once. A denied, expired, failed, or deleted request grants no access.

## Actions and notifications are separate

DorkOS-managed account notifications are not available yet. The choices below describe notification access when the selected route supports it. Slack and Telegram Messaging are separate from this limitation.

An action grant lets an agent run a reviewed operation, such as reading a profile. It does not let the agent receive account activity.

A notification grant names all of these:

- the exact connection;
- the activity type and any supported filter;
- the receiving agent;
- the destination, such as an agent session, room, or supported messaging destination.

To change a notification's scope, remove it and create a reviewed replacement. Standalone notification editing and pause controls are not part of this release. Pausing the whole connection remains available.

Delivery timing comes from the service. Some services send webhooks, some check on a schedule, and some do not report their timing. DorkOS does not label every notification as real time.

## Choose where account access runs

| Route                 | Who supplies the service project | Who holds the service login | Hosted offline notification buffer                            |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| DorkOS-managed        | DorkOS                           | Composio's vault            | Yes, after that capability passes its separate readiness gate |
| Your Composio project | You                              | Composio's vault            | No. Your installation receives events directly.               |
| Nango on your server  | You                              | Your Nango database         | No                                                            |
| Raw MCP server        | You configure the server         | The remote server           | No                                                            |

DorkOS does not charge for account actions in this release. Usage records distinguish which party's service project handled an attempt, but there is no billing or invoice flow.

## When login is off

A local DorkOS installation without login has one local owner. Keep it on a trusted machine and network. Do not expose an installation without login to the public internet.

Program commands still name an agent and can use only that agent's approved connections. Omitting `--agent` never widens access. Through the supported agent and program paths, a request can ask for access but cannot approve itself.

One kind of write does not have that protection: changing who can use an app — sharing it, narrowing it, disconnecting it. With login off, DorkOS cannot tell that write apart from one the app itself made, so anything with a shell on this machine can make it, the same as the app could. **Every agent** is just the widest version of that change, not a special case. Turning login on closes the gap for all of them, because only your signed-in session can make those writes from then on. Until then, changing an app's access is a choice for a machine you trust.

These controls are boundaries inside DorkOS. They do not isolate other software running as your operating-system user. That software can reach the same local files and credentials that you can. The [threat model](/docs/self-hosting/threat-model#connections-and-account-access) explains token lifetime, runtime settings, and this same-user limit.

## Remove access

Removing an agent's access stops its next action and revokes its notification grants for that connection. Pausing a connection stops local use without deleting it. Disconnecting stops every agent from using the account at once. DorkOS then ends the account's access at the service in the background.

If the service can't be reached, DorkOS keeps trying on its own, and the account stays disconnected here while it waits. If DorkOS can't finish at all (for example, your DorkOS account is no longer linked), the panel says so and, for apps like Gmail, links to the page where you can remove the access yourself. You can remove a disconnected app from your list at any time. A request already sent to another service cannot be recalled.

To keep an agent away from an app in just one chat, open that chat's details. Each app the agent was given has a switch there. Turning it off only affects that chat. Turning it back on puts back the access that chat had, and never adds any.
