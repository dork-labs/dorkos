# Spaces

A space is a standalone place to share channels with other people. It has its own sign-in and
history. You do not need a DorkOS Cloud account to use one.

Paid plans, and spaces that run on DorkOS for you, are for people in the United States who are
18 or older. Running a space on your own computer or server is open to everyone, wherever they
live, and so is the free, open-source app.

A space can also connect to your local DorkOS installation. That lets people in a shared channel
mention an agent you choose. The space stays separate from your local DorkOS account.

A space's own pages still call it a "community" in some places, so a few button names below say
that. They are the same thing.

## Add a space

Open the menu at the top left of the sidebar and choose **Add a space**. You get two main choices:

- **Start a space** makes a new one that runs on DorkOS and belongs to you. It shows up only while
  this DorkOS is linked to a DorkOS account that offers it.
- **Join a space** connects this DorkOS to one that already exists. Paste its address or the
  invitation link you were sent. See [Join a space](#join-a-space).

**Your spaces** lists the spaces that run on DorkOS under your account. From there, **Move one
here** brings a space you run somewhere else over to DorkOS. Like **Start a space**, it only shows
while this DorkOS is linked to a DorkOS account.

**Advanced** holds the ways to run a space yourself: **Run your own space server** opens the
[setup guide](/docs/guides/cli-usage#space-server), and **Create a space on your server** appears
once you run a server this DorkOS is connected to.

## Join a space

Choose **Add a space**, then **Join a space**. Paste one of these into **Space address or invitation
link**:

- **The space's address.** It can be the space's full link, such as `https://example.com/c/…`, or
  its short web address, such as `https://example.com/acme`. DorkOS looks the short address up once
  and then remembers the space itself, so the connection keeps working if the short address changes
  later.
- **An invitation link** you were sent. Choose **Open invitation**. The link opens on the space's
  own site, where you sign in or create an account to join. The dialog stays open with the space's
  address filled in, so when you have joined, come back and choose **Connect**.

Check the **Name for this installation**, then choose **Connect**. The dialog gives you a page on
the space's site to open. Sign in there and choose **Approve connection**. Back in DorkOS, the dialog
closes by itself and the space is selected in the menu. Its channels appear in the channel sidebar.

If you close the dialog before approving, the space stays in the menu marked **pending**. Choose it
to pick up where you left off, or choose **Cancel approval** to stop.

## Set up a space on your own server

The person running a new space server opens its address in a browser. They enter the **Setup
secret**, choose **Continue**, then choose **Create account**. They add their name, email,
password, the space's name, and first channel before choosing **Create community**.

To invite someone, open **Manage** in the space, then choose **Create invite** under **Invite
someone**. The space shows a **One-time invite link**. Copy and send that link privately. A link
normally lasts seven days and admits one person.

The invited person opens the link in a browser. They choose **Continue**, then **Create account**,
and finish with **Join community**. An invite admits you to the space. It can also join you to a
channel.

The person running a space can set a minimum age. If they do, the sign-up form says how old you
must be, and you tick **I am at least N years old** before you create your account. This goes for
every way of signing up, including Google, GitHub, and single sign-on. Signing in to an account you
already have never asks.

## Add another space on the same server

One server can run more than one space. The person who set up the server can add another one:

1. Sign in to the server with the account that set it up. Then open `/host` on the server's
   address, for example `https://example.com/host`. If this DorkOS is connected to a space on that
   server through that account, the menu at the top of the sidebar opens it for you: choose **Add a
   space**, then **Advanced**, then **Create a space on your server**.
2. Enter a name, then choose **Create community**.
3. The page shows an **Owner claim link** once. Choose **Copy link** and send it privately to the
   person who will own the new space. You can also open it yourself to become the owner.

The new owner opens the link in a browser and chooses **Continue**. If they are not signed in, they
choose **Create account** or **Sign in**, then finish with **Create account and claim** or **Sign in
and claim**. If they are already signed in, they check the account shown and choose **Claim
community**. After that they choose **Open community** and start inviting people.

The link works once and lasts 24 hours. If it is lost or expires, choose **Reissue owner claim** on
the space in `/host`. The old link stops working as soon as you do.

## Use a channel

Open a channel from the left sidebar. If it is available to explore but you have not joined it,
choose **Join channel** before you can post.

Write in the **Message #channel-name** box and choose **Send**. To add a file, choose **Attach**.
You can add up to four files to one post. The person running the space sets the file-size limit. It
defaults to 10 MiB and can be raised to 25 MiB.

Choose **Reply in thread** on a message to keep a side conversation together. A thread has one level
of replies. The number of replies shows under the message that started the thread and goes up as
new ones arrive. Choose it to open the thread.

If the person running the space has set up a way to report abuse, each message has a **Report**
link. It opens their own page or email with only the space and message IDs filled in. It never sends
the message text or anyone's name.

When you are done with a channel, choose **Leave channel**. You can join it again if its access rules
allow it.

## Delete a message or file

To delete something you posted, open the message's actions (its **⋯** button) and choose
**Delete**. Everyone then sees "This message was deleted." in its place. Replies and threads stay
where they were, and the message's files are deleted too. To delete just one file, use the **⋯**
button beside the file and choose **Delete file**. Messages your agents posted count as yours.

A space's owners and admins see **Remove** on other people's messages. A removed message shows "This
message was removed by a community admin." Admins can't remove messages from the owner or from other
admins.

Neither can be undone. People who already saw a message may have a copy, and exports made before it
was deleted still contain it until they expire.

## Export or leave

Open **Manage** and find **Your data**. Choose **Download my data** to export your posts, agent
activity, and files that you can still access. Owners can choose **Export this community** after
entering their password, to export the whole space: its messages, files, members, and settings.

The space prepares an export in the background, and a large one can take a while. You can close the
page and come back; a progress bar shows how far it has got. When it is ready, choose **Download**.
The button shows the file's size, and the export is kept for a day. If a big download stops, your
browser can resume it from its downloads list.

The export is a `.zip`. Messages posted after the export starts aren't included. Messages deleted
while it's being prepared are left out or shown as deleted.

On the space's site, under **Leave community**, choose **Leave community** to remove your own access.
Your earlier posts stay in the shared history under your name. An owner must transfer ownership
before leaving.

Want less than that? The **Account** tab in **Manage** has two smaller steps. **Sign out of this
browser** ends your sign-in on this browser only. You stay a member, and your connected DorkOS
installations keep working. Under **Connected installations**, **Disconnect** stops one DorkOS
installation from reading or posting in the space. **Disconnect all installations** stops every one
of them after you enter your password. You stay a member either way.

## Erase your messages or delete your account

To remove your posts too, open **Manage**, then **Account**, and use **Erase your messages here**.
Type the space's name, confirm your password, and choose **Erase my messages**. For a space you
already left, open the page that lists your spaces and find it under **Communities you left**.

To leave every space on a server at once, use **Delete your account** on the page that lists your
spaces (**Manage**, then **Account**, then **Delete your account** takes you there). Type your email
and confirm your password. If you own a space, first transfer ownership to another member or delete
the space, and wait until the deletion finishes.

Both wait 72 hours, and you can cancel until then. After that, your name, messages, files, and your
agents' messages are removed. Your messages stay in their place in conversations, marked "This
message was erased." Deleting your account also signs you out everywhere.

We can't reach copies on other people's computers, including files they downloaded and anything
their agents saved, the server's backups for as long as it keeps them, or copies its operator keeps
for legal reasons.

DorkOS installations connected to the space do update their saved copies. When a message is erased
or deleted, each installation swaps in the new text the next time it checks, which is within 15
minutes while it's connected, and drops the old words from its search of that channel. An open page
on the space's site updates within 30 seconds. When an installation can no longer read a channel,
because its person left or was removed, or its agent is no longer in the channel, it deletes its
copy of that channel. What stays is anything that person's agents already saved, like their session
history.

If you sign in with Google or GitHub, leave the password empty. You may be asked to sign in again
first.

## If a space is on hold

The person running the server can put a space on hold. You can still read everything, and the owner
can still download an export, but no one can post, join, or change settings. A banner on every
channel says so. When the hold is released, the space goes back to how it was.

They can also announce that a held space will be deleted. The date shows on every channel. It is
always at least a week away, and two weeks unless they changed that. Use that time to export anything
you want to keep.

## If a space is taken down

If the person running the server learns that a whole space is illegal or breaks their terms, they
can remove it at once. Everyone then sees "This community was removed by its host." on the page that
lists their spaces, and nobody can open it, post, or download an export. Connected DorkOS
installations and agents lose access too.

The owner can still open it to see when it happened and, unless the server's operator keeps it
private, why. The space is deleted a few days later. Until then it can be undone; the space then
comes back paused, and everyone reconnects once it reopens. The owner can't cancel the deletion.

## If someone asks to take over your space

If you own a space, the person running its server can be asked to make someone else the owner. This
is for a space whose owner has left, for example someone who started it for a group and then moved
on. It never happens quietly, and you can always say no.

You are told in three places:

- **By email.** The server sends a message to your account's email address. It says when the change
  could happen and has a link to keep ownership.
- **On the space's own site.** A banner on every page says the same, with a **Keep ownership**
  button.
- **In DorkOS.** If your DorkOS installation is connected, the space's row in the switcher gets a
  warning dot, and its page shows the banner with **Open space**. Your Inbox also gets one
  notification when DorkOS first sees the request, and one if it goes through. DorkOS only checks
  while the DorkOS app is open, so you see the request the next time you open it. A finished
  request is shown for one week, so you only get the second notification if you open the app within
  a week of it.

Nothing changes until a waiting period ends. It is at least a week. It is longer, 30 days unless the
person running the server changed that, when they say you've left the group, when your email
address was never confirmed by a sign-in service, when the email couldn't be delivered, or when you
said no to an earlier request.

To keep ownership, do one of these before the date:

- **Open the link in the email and press Keep ownership.** You don't need to sign in. This works
  even if you can't sign in any more, for example because your organization closed your single
  sign-on account.
- **Sign in on the space's own site and press Keep ownership** in the banner. You don't need your
  password for this.

Either one ends the request. The person running the server is told, and can't ask again for 90 days
unless they set a different time. Any later request always gets the longer wait.

If you'd rather let the space go, you can also hand it to someone yourself or delete it. The banner
only offers what you can do right now. Handing it on works only while the space is not on hold or
archived. Both need your password, so if you only sign in through single sign-on, add a password to
your account first.

If you do nothing, the account named in the request can take ownership after the date. You stay a
member, with your messages, agents and connections as they were, and you get one more email saying
who the new owner is.

## The server's own pages

The person running the server can link their terms, privacy notice and a way to report a problem.
Terms and Privacy show under the sign-in form. All three show under **Manage**, then **Account**, in
a section called **This host**. If they set none of them, you won't see any of this.

A space does not send password-reset email. If you lose your password for a space, ask the person
running it for help.

## Manage a connected space

If a space is put on hold, you can still read it in DorkOS. Posting comes back when the hold ends,
without connecting again.

Once connected, choose the space in the menu, then open **Manage** followed by its name. **Invite
people**, **Space settings** and **Leave space** open the space's own site, where you sign in with
your account there. **Disconnect** removes the connection from this DorkOS installation. It does not
remove your account on the space. It does remove the agents this installation added to the space,
and the confirmation names them first. Agents another of your computers added stay. If the space
can't be reached, DorkOS still disconnects and lists the agents left there, so you can remove them
under **Agents** on the space's own site. If a space says **Reconnect required**, choose it in the
menu, then **Disconnect**, and connect it again.

## Add an agent to a space's channel

Open a connected space's channel in DorkOS and choose **Members**. Under **My agents**, select a
**Local agent**. You may set a **Space handle (optional)**, then choose **Add to space**.

Choose **Join channel** beside that agent before people can mention it there. People can then write
its handle, such as `@researcher`, in a new message. DorkOS gives that new message to the agent
according to the agent's response setting.

Choose **Stop agent** to stop one agent in the open channel. Choose **Stop my agents** to stop all
of your local agents in that channel. You can also choose **Leave channel** beside an agent or
**Remove from space** to end its participation.

## Running and recovering a space server

A space server has its own service and data. The person running it should keep its database and file
storage backed up. See the
[space server README](https://github.com/dork-labs/dorkos/blob/main/apps/community/README.md) for
setup, and the
[operations guide](https://github.com/dork-labs/dorkos/blob/main/apps/community/OPERATIONS.md) for
backups, key rotation, and deployment details.

Your local DorkOS installation keeps its own data too. If you run DorkOS with Docker, follow the
[backup and restore steps](/docs/self-hosting/docker#backing-up-your-data). For a lost local DorkOS
password, use the
[local recovery steps](/docs/self-hosting/securing-your-instance#recovering-a-lost-password).
