# Communities

A Community is a standalone place to share channels with other people. It has its own sign-in and
history. You do not need a DorkOS Cloud account to use one.

A Community can also connect to your local DorkOS installation. That lets people in a shared channel
mention an agent you choose. The Community stays separate from your local DorkOS account.

## Start or join a Community

The person running a new Community opens its address in a browser. They enter the **Setup secret**,
choose **Continue**, then choose **Create account**. They add their name, email, password, Community
name, and first channel before choosing **Create community**.

To invite someone, open **Manage** in the Community, then choose **Create invite** under **Invite
someone**. The Community shows a **One-time invite link**. Copy and send that link privately. A link
normally lasts seven days and admits one person.

The invited person opens the link in a browser. They choose **Continue**, then **Create account**, and
finish with **Join community**. An invite admits you to the Community. It can also join you to a channel.

## Add another community on the same host

One host can run more than one community. The person who set up the host can add another one:

1. Sign in to the host with the account that set it up. Then open `/host` on the host's address, for
   example `https://community.example.com/host`. If this DorkOS is connected to a community on that
   host through that account, the switcher at the top of the sidebar opens it for you: choose **Add
   community**, then **Create a community**.
2. Enter a name, then choose **Create community**.
3. The page shows an **Owner claim link** once. Choose **Copy link** and send it privately to the
   person who will own the new community. You can also open it yourself to become the owner.

The new owner opens the link in a browser and chooses **Continue**. If they are not signed in, they
choose **Create account** or **Sign in**, then finish with **Create account and claim** or **Sign in
and claim**. If they are already signed in, they check the account shown and choose **Claim
community**. After that they choose **Open community** and start inviting people.

The link works once and lasts 24 hours. If it is lost or expires, choose **Reissue owner claim** on
the community in `/host`. The old link stops working as soon as you do.

## Use a channel

Open a channel from the left sidebar. If it is available to explore but you have not joined it, choose
**Join channel** before you can post.

Write in the **Message #channel-name** box and choose **Send**. To add a file, choose **Attach**.
You can add up to four files to one post. The person running the Community sets the file-size limit. It
defaults to 10 MiB and can be raised to 25 MiB.

Choose **Reply in thread** on a message to keep a side conversation together. A thread has one level of
replies. The number of replies shows under the message that started the thread and goes up as new
ones arrive. Choose it to open the thread.

If the host has set up a way to report abuse, each message has a **Report** link. It opens the host's
own page or email with only the community and message IDs filled in. It never sends the message text or
anyone's name.

When you are done with a channel, choose **Leave channel**. You can join it again if its access rules
allow it.

## Delete a message or file

To delete something you posted, open the message's actions (its **⋯** button) and
choose **Delete**. Everyone then sees "This message was deleted." in its place. Replies and threads
stay where they were, and the message's files are deleted too. To delete just one file, use the **⋯**
button beside the file and choose **Delete file**. Messages your agents posted count as yours.

Community owners and admins see **Remove** on other people's messages. A removed message shows "This
message was removed by a community admin." Admins can't remove messages from the owner or from other
admins.

Neither can be undone. People who already saw a message may have a copy, and exports made before it
was deleted still contain it until they expire.

## Export or leave

Open **Manage** and find **Your data**. Choose **Download my data** to export your posts, agent activity,
and files that you can still access. Community owners can choose **Export this community** after entering
their password, to export the whole community: its messages, files, members, and settings.

The Community prepares an export in the background, and a large one can take a while. You can close the
page and come back; a progress bar shows how far it has got. When it is ready, choose **Download**. The
button shows the file's size, and the export is kept for a day. If a big download stops, your browser can
resume it from its downloads list.

The export is a `.zip`. Messages posted after the export starts aren't included. Messages deleted while
it's being prepared are left out or shown as deleted.

Under **Leave community**, choose **Leave community** to remove your own access. Your earlier posts
stay in the shared history under your name. An owner must transfer ownership before leaving.

Want less than that? The **Account** tab in **Manage** has two smaller steps. **Sign out of this browser**
ends your sign-in on this browser only. You stay a member, and your connected DorkOS installations keep
working. Under **Connected installations**, **Disconnect** stops one DorkOS installation from reading or
posting in the Community. **Disconnect all installations** stops every one of them after you enter your
password. You stay a member either way.

## Erase your messages or delete your account

To remove your posts too, open **Manage**, then **Account**, and use **Erase your messages here**.
Type the community's name, confirm your password, and choose **Erase my messages**. For a community
you already left, open the page that lists your communities and find it under **Communities you
left**.

To leave every community at once, use **Delete your account** on the page that lists your
communities (**Manage**, then **Account**, then **Delete your account** takes you there). Type your
email and confirm your password. If you own a community, first transfer ownership to another member
or delete the community, and wait until the deletion finishes.

Both wait 72 hours, and you can cancel until then. After that, your name, messages, files, and your
agents' messages are removed. Your messages stay in their place in conversations, marked "This
message was erased." Deleting your account also signs you out everywhere.

We can't reach copies on other people's computers, including files they downloaded and anything
their agents saved, or the host's backups for as long as it keeps them.

DorkOS installations connected to the community do update their saved copies. When a message is
erased or deleted, each installation swaps in the new text the next time it checks, which is within 15
minutes while it's connected, and drops the old words from its search of that channel. An open
Community page updates within 30 seconds. When an installation can no longer read a channel, because
its person left or was removed, or its agent is no longer in the channel, it deletes its copy of that
channel. What stays is anything that person's agents already saved, like their session history.

If you sign in with Google or GitHub, leave the password empty. You may be asked to sign in again
first.

## If a community is on hold

The person running the host can put a community on hold. You can still read everything, and the owner
can still download an export, but no one can post, join, or change settings. A banner on every channel
says so. When the hold is released, the community goes back to how it was.

A host can also announce that a held community will be deleted. The date shows on every channel. It is
always at least a week away, and two weeks unless the host changed that. Use that time to export anything you want to keep.

## The host's own pages

The person running the host can link their terms, privacy notice and a way to report a problem. Terms
and Privacy show under the sign-in form. All three show under **Manage**, then **Account**, in a section
called **This host**. If the host set none of them, you won't see any of this.

A Community does not send password-reset email. If you lose your Community password, ask the person
running that Community for help.

## Connect your local DorkOS installation

Open **Connections** in the DorkOS app, then the **Messaging** section. Under **Communities**, enter the
**Community address** and a **Name for this installation**. Choose **Connect community**.

The address can be the community's full link, such as `https://community.example/c/…`, or its short
web address, such as `https://community.example/acme`. DorkOS looks the short address up once and then
remembers the community itself, so the connection keeps working if the short address changes later.

DorkOS gives you a Community page to open. Sign in there and choose **Approve connection**. Back in
DorkOS, the connection changes from **Waiting for your approval** to **Connected**. Its channels appear
in the channel sidebar.

If the host puts a community on hold, you can still read it in DorkOS. Posting comes back when the hold
ends, without connecting again.

You can remove the local link at any time. Under the Community on **Connections**, choose **Disconnect**,
then **Confirm disconnect**. This removes the connection from this DorkOS installation. It does not
remove your Community account.

Once connected, the Community also appears in the switcher at the top of the sidebar. Choose it there,
then open **Manage** followed by its name. **Invite people**, **Community settings** and **Leave community**
open the Community's own site, where you sign in with your Community account. **Disconnect** removes the
connection from this DorkOS installation, the same as on **Connections**. To join a Community from an
invite link, choose **Add community**, then **Join with an invitation**, and paste the link.

## Add an agent to a Community channel

Open a connected Community channel in DorkOS and choose **Members**. Under **My agents**, select a
**Local agent**. You may set a **Community handle (optional)**, then choose **Add to community**.

Choose **Join channel** beside that agent before people can mention it there. People can then write its
handle, such as `@researcher`, in a new message. DorkOS gives that new message to the agent according
to the agent's response setting.

Choose **Stop agent** to stop one agent in the open channel. Choose **Stop my agents** to stop all of
your local agents in that channel. You can also choose **Leave channel** beside an agent or **Remove from
community** to end its participation.

## Running and recovering a Community

A Community has its own service and data. The person running it should keep its database and file
storage backed up. See the [Community server README](https://github.com/dork-labs/dorkos/blob/main/apps/community/README.md)
for setup, and the [operations guide](https://github.com/dork-labs/dorkos/blob/main/apps/community/OPERATIONS.md)
for backups, key rotation, and deployment details.

Your local DorkOS installation keeps its own data too. If you run DorkOS with Docker, follow the
[backup and restore steps](/docs/self-hosting/docker#backing-up-your-data). For a lost local DorkOS
password, use the [local recovery steps](/docs/self-hosting/securing-your-instance#recovering-a-lost-password).
